# Scooter Winter Cover Guide

Scooters are the vehicles most likely to be kept outside through winter, and the least likely to be prepared. They are bought as practical transport, parked on a street or in a front garden, and often have no garage, no power supply and no time for maintenance.

A scooter winter cover therefore does more work than a motorcycle one, on a machine that is small, low and covered in plastic.

## What winter does to a scooter

**Frost on the seat and controls.** The seat is the largest horizontal surface on a scooter, collects frost directly, and is sat on immediately.

**Frozen locks and cables.** Scooter locks are small and exposed, and moisture in the mechanism freezes. Cable-operated brakes and throttles are vulnerable at their low points, where water collects.

**Condensation in the bodywork.** Plastic panels enclose the engine and electricals, and cold nights condense moisture inside them. This is how clean-looking machines end up with corroded connectors.

**Battery drain and cold.** Small batteries lose cranking ability in the cold. A cover does not warm the battery, but keeping the machine dry and out of wind reduces the swing it sees.

**Road salt on the lower body.** Scooters sit low and throw salt onto the engine cases.

## Why a scooter cover differs from a motorcycle cover

- **Shorter and wider.** A motorcycle cover leaves a slack skirt that hangs to the ground and soaks up water.
- **Mirrors are the widest point**, and on many scooters they sit high and wide relative to the body.
- **No exposed frame or tank** to take the shape, so a cover tends to sit like a bag.
- **The front wheel and mudguard** are close together, so the cover must not bridge under the mudguard where it will collect water.
- **Top boxes and screens** are common and change the profile completely.

A cover measured for a scooter with a top box and tall screen is a different product from one measured for a bare machine — a distinction that matters more than nominal engine size.

## What to specify

**A fitted, elasticated hem.** This is the single most important feature on a scooter cover. It keeps the cover off the ground, stops it flapping, and prevents water from wicking up from the hem.

**A breathable top.** Condensation is the scooter's main winter problem. A completely sealed cover traps moisture against the machine and inside the bodywork.

**Sealed seams.** A scooter sits low, and water on the ground splashes upward. Seams are the first place it enters.

**Clearance for the screen and top box.** A cover that pulls tight over a screen stresses the stitching at the highest point, which is also the most wind-loaded.

**No absorbent lining.** A brushed or fleece lining on a machine put away wet holds water against the panels.

## A winter routine that works

1. Fit the cover dry, with the hem clear of the ground.
2. Remove the battery for long stands, or connect a maintainer if power is available.
3. Leave the cover on through frost rather than removing it daily, since each removal starts another condensation cycle.
4. On a dry day, remove it and let both machine and cover dry fully.
5. Treat locks and cables with a water-displacing lubricant before the first hard frost.

## Buying notes

- fitted, elasticated hem clear of the ground
- breathable top panel
- sealed seams
- sized for the machine as ridden, including screen and top box
- no absorbent lining
- reflective elements, since scooters are often parked on street
