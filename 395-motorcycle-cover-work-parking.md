# Motorcycle Cover for Work Parking

Parking at work is a distinct problem from parking at home. The bike stands in a shared, open, unfamiliar location for eight or more hours, in daylight, in front of the same people every day. There is no garage and no camera the rider controls.

A cover is the only practical intervention, and what it does there differs from a driveway.

## What the cover is actually for at work

**Weather, obviously.** Eight hours of sun, rain or bird traffic is the visible problem.

**Attention.** An uncovered bike invites handling: people sit on it, put bags on the seat, park bicycles against it. A fitted cover removes the invitation. This is not a security device, but it is a genuine deterrent to casual contact.


**Keeping the seat usable.** A covered bike has a dry seat at the end of the shift, which matters more than most riders expect on a wet commute home.

What a cover does not do is prevent theft. It should never be sold as if it does.

## The practical constraints of a workplace

- **Fitment in a car park**, often in a suit, often in a hurry, sometimes in front of colleagues.
- **No secure place to leave the cover** during the shift.
- **No drying opportunity** if it is wet in the morning.
- **Wind exposure**, because office car parks are usually open and often windier than a residential street.
- **Restricted space** between parked vehicles, so the cover must go on without a wide sweep.

These constraints favour a cover that packs small, goes on fast and does not require the bike to be walked around.

## What to specify

**A compact pack.** The cover has to go into a bag that is carried into the building, so packed volume matters more than fabric weight.

**One-person, one-pass fitment.** A cover that can be thrown over from the front and closed at the hem is faster than one with four separate straps.

**A wet-tolerant bag.** If the cover is wet when removed, the bag must not turn the rider's luggage damp. A mesh panel or a separate dry bag solves this.

**Wind security at the hem.** An open car park is a windy place. An elasticated or drawstring hem, plus one strap under the bike, is the minimum for a cover that will be left unattended all day.

**Reflective elements.** Not for the rider, but so the covered bike is visible to drivers manoeuvring in the car park.

## A realistic routine

The routine that works is short enough to be repeated:

1. Park so the bike faces out, with space to walk around the front.
2. Fit the cover front-first, from the bars backwards.
3. Close the hem, then the under-bike strap.
4. Push the packed cover into its bag immediately, before walking away.

Riders who skip step four end up carrying a loose cover, which is how covers get left at home.

## The honest limits

A cover left in a shared car park all day sees more UV, more wind and more handling than one used at home, so expect the hem and fastenings to wear first. For a bike parked in the same open spot daily, a simple, inexpensive, well-hemmed cover that is actually carried and used beats an expensive one too bulky to bring.

## Buying notes

- compact packed size, with a carry bag
- fast, single-pass fitment
- elasticated or drawstring hem
- one under-bike strap for wind
- reflective elements for car-park visibility
- quick-drying fabric
- no claim of theft prevention
