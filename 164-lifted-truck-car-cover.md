# Car Cover for Lifted Trucks and 4x4s — Sizing the Extra Height

Lifted trucks present a unique cover problem: a 6-inch suspension lift adds height without adding length. Standard truck covers sized by length alone come up short. Here's how to get it right.

## The Lifted Truck Problem

A lifted truck is taller than stock but often the same length. Cover sizing charts based on length assume stock height. The result: a cover that fits bumper-to-bumper but pulls tight over the roof, stressing seams and leaving the windshield exposed.

## Critical Measurements for Lifted Trucks

| Measurement | How to Measure | Why It Matters |
|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| Overall height | Ground to highest point (usually cab roof or bed cap) | Determines height clearance |
| Overall length | Bumper to bumper | Primary size class |
| Tire size | Outside diameter of mounted tires | Larger tires add height |
| Bed accessories | Toolbox, bed cap, rack height | Changes the profile |

## Sizing Strategy

**Rule of thumb:** For every 2 inches of suspension lift, add 4-6 inches to the height allowance when selecting a cover. If you have 35-inch tires on a standard-height truck, treat it as a lifted truck.

**When in doubt, size up one class.** A cover that's slightly long cinches with straps. A cover that's too short leaves your hood or bed exposed.

## Features for Lifted Trucks

- **Deep mirror pockets:** Wide mirrors on trucks need room
- **Extended side panels:** Cover the taller bodywork
- **Reinforced seams:** Extra fabric over the roofline stresses seams
- **Adjustable straps:** Must reach the taller frame

## The Bed Consideration

If your truck has a bed cap, tonneau, or rack, the height calculation changes entirely — measure to the tallest accessory, not the cab roof. Custom sizing based on your full setup is the safest option.
