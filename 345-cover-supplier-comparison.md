# Cover Supplier Comparison Checklist: How to Compare OEM Factories

Buying car covers, boat covers, or motorcycle covers in volume comes down to one decision: which factory gets the order. Comparing suppliers on price alone is a common mistake. A structured checklist protects your margin, your lead time, and your brand reputation. Here is how to compare cover factories properly.

## The Comparison Criteria

| Criterion | What to Check |
|-----------|---------------|
| Certifications | ISO 9001, OEKO-TEX, BSCI or similar audits |
| Fabric sourcing | Own fabric supply vs bought-in; fabric tests (denier, hydrostatic head, UV) |
| QC process | Inline inspection, final AQL inspection, third-party inspection option |
| Sampling | Sample cost, lead time, and whether samples match production quality |
| MOQ flexibility | MOQ per SKU vs mixed models; trial-order options |
| Lead time | Production time, peak season delays, shipping schedule |
| Communication | Response time, English fluency, technical knowledge |
| Customization | Logo, color, packaging, size chart customization |
| Pricing transparency | Fabric cost breakdown, tooling fees, payment terms |

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Sample quality much better than production quality
- No physical factory address or vague audit history
- Fabric specs that change after the order is confirmed
- Very low quotes with no fabric breakdown
- Refusal to arrange third-party inspection
- Long response gaps during critical production stages

## How to Run a Trial Order

1. Order samples from the top 3 shortlisted factories
2. Test sample fabric (weight, coating, stitching) against your spec sheet
3. Place a small trial order of 50-200 units, not a full container
4. Inspect the trial order with a third-party QC service
5. Evaluate on-time delivery, packaging quality, and communication before scaling

## What to Ask in the First Call

- Ask for the factory's product range and which covers are their core line
- Request a standard size chart and compare it with your market's vehicle list
- Ask how many years they have exported covers and to which regions
- Request references from two existing distributors in markets similar to yours
- Confirm who owns the tooling for custom sizes and logos, and what it costs

## Quick Takeaways

- Compare factories on certifications, QC, and communication, not price alone
- A trial order costs a little but prevents a bad container load
- Always request a fabric spec sheet with denier, coating, and UV ratings
- Use third-party inspection for your first few orders
- Negotiate payment terms after trust is built, not before
