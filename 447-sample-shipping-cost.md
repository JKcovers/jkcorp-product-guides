# Managing Sample Shipping Costs

Sample shipping is a small line item that repeatedly becomes a large one, because it is usually handled informally. A buyer requests three samples from three suppliers, each ships separately by express at the buyer's account, and the freight ends up costing more than the samples.

The costs are predictable, and so are the ways to control them.

## What you are actually paying for

- **Courier freight.** Priced on volumetric weight, which for a bulky cover is usually the volumetric figure rather than the actual weight.
- **Duties and taxes.** Samples are not automatically exempt. Many markets charge duty and VAT on sample value, and the courier often advances it with a handling fee.
- **Supplier handling.** Some factories charge a sample fee plus freight; others absorb the sample and charge freight only.
- **Return or disposal.** Returning a sample usually costs more than the sample is worth.

## The volumetric trap

A cover packs large and weighs little. Couriers bill on the greater of actual and volumetric weight, so a light carton in a big box is charged as if it were heavy.

Two practical fixes:

- ask the supplier to compress and vacuum-pack the sample, and to state the packed dimensions before shipping
- consolidate several samples into one shipment rather than shipping piece by piece

The second is usually the largest saving available, and it requires only coordination.

## Declaring the value

Declaring a sample at zero or a nominal value to reduce duty is common and risky. If the shipment is lost, the claim is limited to the declared value; if it is inspected, an undervalued declaration is a problem for the importer, not the supplier.

A defensible approach is to declare the actual commercial sample value and accept the duty. On a single sample the amount is usually small; on a consolidated shipment it is worth calculating before dispatch rather than discovering it on delivery.

## Getting the cost back

Three arrangements work in practice:

**Freight collect on your account.** You control the courier and the cost, and you see the invoice. Best when you are sampling several suppliers.

**Sample fee credited against the first order.** Common and reasonable. Get it in writing, including whether freight is included in the credit.

**Supplier-paid sampling for approved programmes.** Realistic once you are a repeat buyer with a forecast, not on a first enquiry.

## A workable routine

- batch sample requests so they ship together
- specify packing dimensions before dispatch
- use one courier account so costs are visible in one place
- agree in advance whether the sample fee is credited
- keep the sample; the freight is already paid, and a retained sample is useful in a quality dispute

The last point is the one buyers most often waste. A sample that arrives and is thrown away after ten minutes has cost the same as one that becomes the reference standard for the order.
