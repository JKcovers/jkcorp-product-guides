# Cover Sizing Mistakes — Top 10 and How to Avoid Them

A cover that does not fit does not protect — and often damages. Sizing mistakes are the #1 source of cover complaints, returns, and paint damage. These are the 10 most common cover sizing mistakes and how to avoid every one of them.

## Mistake 1: Measuring Only Length

Most buyers size by length alone. Wrong. Width, height, and shape matter equally — a long, narrow cover will not fit a tall SUV, and a wide cover sags on a sedan.

**Fix:** measure length, width (with mirrors), and height, then compare all three against the size chart.

## Mistake 2: Forgetting Mirrors and Antennas

Mirrors add 10-15 cm to width; antennas add height. Covers without mirror pockets or antenna allowance tear at the seams.

**Fix:** note mirror and antenna measurements, and buy covers with mirror pockets if your car has protruding mirrors.

## Mistake 3: Buying Universal by Guesswork

Universal covers claim to fit "sedans" — but a compact sedan and a full-size sedan need different sizes. Guessing wastes money.

**Fix:** measure your specific car and check the manufacturer's fit table before buying.

## Mistake 4: Ignoring the Height of the Load

Trucks with caps, RVs with AC units, boats with T-tops — accessories raise the vehicle profile. A cover sized for the bare vehicle will not close.

**Fix:** measure the highest point of the loaded vehicle, not the base body.

## Mistake 5: Sizing Down to Look Tight

A cover should fit snugly, but a size too small strains seams, pops straps, and leaves parts exposed.

**Fix:** if between sizes, always size up.

## Mistake 6: Sizing Up Too Much

An oversized cover flaps in wind, pools water, and rubs paint. "Room to spare" is a mistake, not a safety margin.

**Fix:** choose the closest size with a small tolerance (5-8 cm) — not the biggest size available.

## Mistake 7: Not Checking Shape, Only Dimensions

A boat with a tall windshield and a flat-bottomed runabout can share dimensions but need different covers. Shape matters.

**Fix:** match the cover type to the vehicle type (car vs boat vs motorcycle), not just the numbers.

## Mistake 8: Measuring a Modified Vehicle

Lifted trucks, wide-body kits, roof racks — modifications change fit completely.

**Fix:** measure the current, modified vehicle, not the stock dimensions from the manual.

## Mistake 9: Forgetting Trailer and Mounting Points

Trailers, hitches, and antennas stick out beyond the measured body. The cover binds against them.

**Fix:** include every protrusion in your measurements, or buy covers with cutouts.

## Mistake 10: Guessing Instead of Measuring

The classic error: estimating "about 4.5 meters" instead of measuring. Cover size charts leave no room for guessing.

**Fix:** measure twice with a tape measure — length, width, height, and any accessories.

## The Golden Rule

**Measure the actual vehicle, in its actual condition, and buy the closest size — sizing up only when truly between sizes.** A correctly sized cover protects; a wrong one damages.
