# Side Mirror Cover Sourcing Guide

Side mirror covers are a small, high-margin accessory that is easy to buy badly. The product looks simple, but the fit depends on mirror geometry that varies enormously between vehicles, and the attachment method decides whether the cover stays on at speed.

This guide is written for buyers building a product line rather than for a vehicle owner choosing one pair.

## Why mirrors need separate treatment

A full car cover usually includes mirror pockets, but a mirror cover is bought on its own for reasons a full cover cannot address:

- the car is parked under a carport with the cover off
- the mirrors are the part most exposed to frost, ice and insect strike
- the owner wants protection without covering the whole car
- the mirrors are the most expensive painted part to repair

The product is therefore less about coverage and more about targeted protection and convenience.

## Sizing across a vehicle range

Mirror housings vary in three dimensions that matter: width, height and depth, plus the angle of the arm.

A practical approach for a supplier is to group vehicles into coverage classes rather than to offer one universal size:

- **Compact and hatchback mirrors**: small, often rounded.
- **Saloon and SUV mirrors**: larger, frequently with integrated indicators.
- **Van and truck mirrors**: wide, often twin-lens and on long arms.

Within each class, an elasticated cover with a generous size range will fit most vehicles. A single universal size will fit none of them well.

Publish the size range against actual mirror dimensions, not against vehicle names alone.

## Attachment methods

**Elasticated hem.** The default. Simple, cheap, fits a range, and adequate for parked use. It can lift at speed on a smooth mirror housing.

**Drawstring or toggle.** More adjustable and more secure, at the cost of fitting time.

**Hook-and-loop strap behind the housing.** Secure at speed, but the strap is the failure point and can abrade the mirror paint.

**Full sock with an arm channel.** The most secure and the most complex; suited to vans and trucks with long arms.

**Magnetic edges.** Fast, but only work on steel; most modern mirror housings are plastic.

## Material

- **Coated polyester**: water-resistant, light, prints well.
- **Fleece or brushed lining**: protects paint, holds frost protection, but absorbs water.
- **Neoprene**: thick, insulating, good for frost, heavier.
- **Reflective outer face**: useful where mirrors are struck in low light.
- **Flocked or soft inner face**: reduces abrasion on the housing.

For frost protection, insulation matters; for summer, UV and insect strike matter more and a light reflective shell is better.

## Testing before committing

- **Fit across the size range**, on real mirrors rather than a mock-up.
- **Retention at speed**, since a mirror cover that lifts is a safety concern.
- **Abrasion**, by fitting and removing repeatedly and inspecting the housing.
- **Water ingress**, particularly where an elastic hem gaps at the arm.
- **Print durability**, if the product is branded.

## Commercial points

- **Pairs, not singles.** Mirrors are sold in pairs; packing should reflect it.
- **Seasonality.** Frost covers sell in winter, UV and insect covers in summer.
- **Bundling.** Mirror covers pair naturally with windscreen frost guards and full covers.
- **Low tooling.** Most constructions need no tooling, which lowers the entry cost but also the barrier for competitors.

## What to specify

- the size classes and the mirror dimensions each covers
- attachment method and expected retention at speed
- outer and inner material
- whether the product is sold as a pair
- print capability
- packed dimensions and weight
