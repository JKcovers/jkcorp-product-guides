# Motorcycle Handlebar Cover — Grip, Switch and Lever Protection

The handlebar is the most exposed part of a parked motorcycle. Grips crack in the sun, switches corrode in the rain, and levers get knocked by passing carts in a shared garage. A motorcycle handlebar cover is the cheapest protection you can buy, and it solves a problem most riders do not notice until the grips are ruined.

## What Gets Damaged

- **Rubber grips** — UV cracks them in one hot season; they harden and lose grip feel
- **Switch housings** — water seeps in and corrodes the contacts
- **Brake and clutch levers** — scratched, bent, or snagged in tight storage
- **Bar-end weights and mirrors** — the widest, most vulnerable points
- **Heated grip wiring** — exposed connections corrode in rain

## Types of Handlebar Covers

| Type | Coverage | Best For |
|------|----------|----------|
| Simple sleeve | One grip | Short-term parking |
| Full bar cover | Both grips, switches, levers | Overnight parking |
| Mirror + bar set | Bars and mirror heads | Long-term storage |
| Neoprene wrap | Custom, form-fitting | Daily use |

## Key Features

1. **Waterproof outer shell** — keeps rain off switches and grips
2. **Soft fleece lining** — does not scratch the bar and controls
3. **Elastic or drawstring closure** — holds in wind, prevents pooling water
4. **Vent openings** — allow condensation to escape when fitted after a ride
5. **UV-resistant material** — the sun is the biggest killer of grips

## How to Fit

Fit the cover over the bars, pulling it down over both grips and the switch housings. The elastic hem should sit below the switch clusters but above the fork tubes. In rain, angle the cover slightly so water runs off the bar rather than pooling on the switches. For storage, fit the cover over the mirrors too if it has mirror pockets.

## Daily Parking vs. Storage

For daily parking, a handlebar cover protects the most-used controls — the ones that fail first on any bike. Fitting takes ten seconds. For long-term storage, pair the handlebar cover with a full motorcycle cover. The handlebar cover adds a second layer where moisture and UV do the most damage.

## Common Mistakes

- **Fitting a wet cover** — always wipe the bars down first; trapped moisture corrodes switches
- **Buying too small** — a tight cover strains the switch housings
- **Ignoring the wiring** — check that heated grip cables are not pinched under the hem
- **Leaving it on while riding** — these covers are for parked bikes only

## Materials That Last

Neoprene is the premium choice — it stretches to fit, seals well, and shrugs off UV. Oxford and PU-coated polyester are the budget option with a shorter life. Avoid thin plastic sleeves; they crack in cold weather and tear on the first fitting.

## The Bottom Line

A handlebar cover protects the parts you touch every ride for the price of a tank of fuel. Add one to any parking or storage routine.
