# Ski Boat Cover Guide

A ski boat is the opposite of a wake boat in cover terms: low, flat and unobstructed, with a small windshield and often no tower. That sounds easier, but the low profile creates its own problems, particularly when trailered.

## What defines the shape

- **Low freeboard and a flat deck**, so the cover sits close to the water and to the trailer.
- **A small, low windshield** that cannot support a cover and is easily damaged by one.
- **A flat engine box or sun pad** at the rear, which is a good support but a poor drain.
- **No tower** on most tournament boats, so there is no high hard point.
- **Tracking fins and a prop** close to the trailer, which the cover's hem must clear.

The result is a cover with a long, low, gently curved top and very little height. Water has nowhere to run except off the sides, and the sides are close to the waterline.

## Drainage on a low deck

Because the deck is nearly flat and the cover follows it, drainage depends on the cover being slightly peaked rather than following the deck exactly.

Two approaches:

**A low pole or ridge support** over the cockpit, lifting the cover just enough to shed water sideways and aft. On a ski boat this does not need to be tall — a modest lift is sufficient, and a tall pole would create a sail.

**A pattern with a built-in crown**, where the cover is cut with a slight ridge along the centreline. This works on a narrow boat, and it avoids hardware entirely.

What must be avoided is a cover that fits the deck exactly. It will hold a thin sheet of water across the whole surface, which is heavy, accelerates coating wear, and finds every seam.

## The windshield problem

A ski boat windshield is low, curved and often made of tempered glass in a thin frame. It is not designed to carry load.

A cover that rests on it will:

- press the frame against the deck, stressing the mounting
- abrade the frame's finish at the contact line
- transmit wind load into the glass

The pattern should clear the windshield or rest on the deck and dash either side of it, never bridge across the top.

## Trailering

Ski boats are trailered more than almost any other type, often at highway speed, and the low profile means the cover is fully exposed to airflow.

- **A closing hem is mandatory.** A loose hem at 60 mph will lift the cover.
- **No bridged spans.** The windshield-to-bow span is short here, but any unsupported span will flap.
- **Straps must not cross the gelcoat** without a soft interface, because a low cover's straps sit against the hull side where they are most visible and most likely to chafe.
- **The rear hem must clear the prop and trailer lights.** This is a common oversight and a common tear.

## What to look for

- a pattern with a slight crown, or a low ridge support
- clearance at the windshield, with no load on the glass
- a closing hem rated for trailering
- soft interfaces under straps
- rear hem clearance at the prop and trailer
- vents, because a low cover over a large cockpit still condenses

## Buying notes

A ski boat cover should be a tight, low, slightly crowned shape. If a supplier offers a generic "v-hull" pattern, check the windshield line and the rear clearance specifically, since those are where a generic cover fails on this hull type.
