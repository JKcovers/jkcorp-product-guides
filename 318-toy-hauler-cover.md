# Toy Hauler Cover — Full Protection for Your Garage on Wheels

Toy haulers combine living quarters with a garage bay, which means two different protection needs under one roof. A toy hauler cover shields the RV body, the ramp door, and the garage area from weather. This guide covers sizing, materials, and care.

## What Makes a Toy Hauler Cover Different

Toy haulers have unique features a standard RV cover ignores:

- **Ramp door** — the rear door seals the garage; a cover with a rear panel protects the seal and hardware
- **Garage windows and vents** — condensation forms in the garage bay, so ventilation panels matter more
- **Heavier roof loads** — most toy haulers carry AC units and vents close together; reinforced roof panels are essential
- **Taller overall height** — toy haulers often sit higher than comparable fifth wheels

## Sizing Rules

Measure three numbers — never order by length alone:

1. **Length** from the front cap to the rear bumper
2. **Width** at the widest point (usually the slide-outs)
3. **Height** from the ground to the tallest roof accessory

Compare all three against the cover chart. If height exceeds the cover's spec, the cover will strain at the roof and may tear at the sides.

## Material and Ventilation

Look for multi-layer fabric with a waterproof coating and a soft inner lining that will not scratch the gel coat. Ventilation is non-negotiable on a toy hauler: the garage bay traps moisture, and unvented covers create mildew between the fabric and the siding.

## Installation Tips

1. Open and latch the ramp door before covering — covering a closed ramp can trap damp air in the garage.
2. Walk the cover from the front cap backward over the roof.
3. Center the reinforced panels over each AC unit.
4. Secure the elastic hem under the chassis and tie down the rear straps.
5. Check the cover after the first rain — pooling on the garage roof area means the cover is too loose.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Covering with the ramp down.** A lowered ramp door means the cover cannot reach the rear corners, and the exposed ramp takes the weather. Latch the ramp before covering.

**Sizing to the empty height.** Toy haulers sit higher than they look — measure to the roof AC, not the roofline. An undersized cover tears at the roof seams within one season.

**Skipping the rear tie-downs.** The garage area is the lightest part of the coach and the first to lift in wind. Always secure the rear straps.

**Forgetting to clean the garage floor.** Mud and moisture in the garage bay migrate upward under the cover. Sweep and dry the bay before covering the coach.

## The Golden Rule

**Vent the garage bay and reinforce the roof — a toy hauler cover must handle the tallest, widest points of the coach.** Measure everything, then choose the next size up if any number is close to the limit.
