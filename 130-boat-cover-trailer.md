# Trailering Boat Covers — Highway-Proof Protection for Your Watercraft

A boat cover that works fine at the dock can fail catastrophically at 60 mph on the highway. Trailering puts unique stress on covers: wind lift, abrasion, and vibration. Here's what trailering-specific covers do differently.

## The Highway Stress Test

At 60 mph, wind forces on a boat cover are 10-20x stronger than any dock wind. Loose fabric lifts, flaps, and abrades against the hull. The cover's attachment points take repeated shock loads from bumps and potholes.

## Trailering-Specific Features

**Reinforced attachment:** Trailering covers use heavy-duty tie-downs (bungee cords or straps) at multiple points, not just an elastic hem. Look for reinforced webbing loops at the tie points.

**Higher fabric weight:** 600D+ Oxford or marine-grade fabric resists wind abrasion. Lightweight dock covers tear quickly on the highway.

**Snug fit:** Trailering covers fit tighter than storage covers — less fabric means less flapping. Custom-fit is strongly preferred.

**Reinforced stress points:** Bow, stern, and windshield areas get extra layers and stitching.

## The Two-Cover Strategy

Many boat owners use:
- **Storage cover** (heavier, full protection) at the dock
- **Trailering cover** (snug, reinforced) on the road

Using one cover for both means compromising one job.

## Speed and Weather Rules

- Never exceed the cover manufacturer's rated speed (usually 55-65 mph)
- Stop and check tie-downs every 100-150 miles
- In rain, reduce speed — water pooling at speed stresses the cover
- Retighten straps after the first few miles (they stretch)

## The Bottom Line

If you trailer your boat, a storage cover is not enough. Invest in a trailering-specific cover — it's cheaper than a new hull finish.
