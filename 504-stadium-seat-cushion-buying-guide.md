# Stadium Seat Cushion Buying Guide

A stadium seat cushion looks like a simple product, which is exactly why it is easy to get wrong. The gap between a cushion that sells through and one that comes back is usually the same short list of decisions: foam type, base construction, attachment method, and how the cushion behaves after three hours under a seated adult.

This guide covers the specification points worth settling before a first order.

## Start with the seat, not the cushion

Stadium seating covers several very different surfaces, and they do not share requirements.

- **Aluminium bleachers** — narrow, hard and thermally conductive. The cushion has to add depth as well as softness, and it must not slide.
- **Moulded plastic bucket seats** — already contoured, so a thin pad works better than a thick one that lifts the sitter.
- **Concrete tiers** — abrasive and cold. A durable base fabric matters more here than extra cushioning.
- **Folding chairs and benches** — the cushion is carried, so weight and packed size dominate.

One SKU cannot do all four well. Deciding which surfaces the product targets is the first specification decision, and it determines everything downstream.

## Foam is the core decision

The foam sets comfort, thickness, weight and whether the cushion survives rain.

- **Open-cell polyurethane** is soft and breathable but absorbs water. It suits indoor arenas and covered stands.
- **Closed-cell polyethylene or EVA** does not absorb water and holds its shape, which makes it the safer choice for open-air stadiums and for any product stored in a bag between games.
- **Moulded foam with memory properties** gives a premium feel but adds cost, weight and pack size.

Thickness is a trade-off rather than a number to maximise. Too thin and the metal underneath is still felt; too thick and the cushion pushes the spectator into the row behind. Moulded contours usually solve that better than raw thickness.

## Base, cover and attachment

The base fabric is what contacts the bleacher. A coated or laminated backing resists damp metal and abrasion; a soft lining protects moulded plastic seats.

Attachment decides whether the cushion stays put. Common options are elastic loops over the seat edge, hook-and-loop straps, or a full sleeve threaded onto the bench. On bleachers, a sleeve or strap arrangement is more secure than relying on friction against the surface.

## Customisation and the buying checklist

The cushion is a flat, visible panel, so printed versions are common in team, school and sponsor programmes. Confirm artwork placement first: printing close to a seam distorts when the cushion curves around a seat.

Before ordering, verify:

- foam type and thickness against the intended surface
- base fabric and whether it is water-resistant
- attachment method tested on the real seat, not a bench
- print durability after handling and cleaning
- packed size and weight for the retail channel
