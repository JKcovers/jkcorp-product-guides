# Camper Shell Cover — Full Protection for Your Truck Cap

A camper shell (truck cap or topper) is a major investment — fiberglass, aluminum, or plastic — sitting on the bed year-round. Sun, hail, and tree debris beat down on it daily. A camper shell cover is the cheapest insurance you can buy for it. Here is what to know.

## What a Camper Shell Cover Protects

- **Paint and gel coat** on fiberglass shells — the top fades and oxidizes first
- **Windows and seals** — UV cracks glazing and hardens rubber gaskets
- **Roof racks and accessories** — hardware corrodes without protection
- **Resale value** — a covered shell looks new after five years

## Choosing the Right Cover

**Size:** measure the shell's length, width, and height at the highest point (usually the roofline or a roof rack). The cover should fit snugly — not so tight it strains seams, not so loose it flaps.

**Material:** multi-layer fabric with a waterproof coating for outdoor parking; breathable single-layer for garage storage.

**Features to look for:** adjustable straps under the bed rails, mirror pockets where the shell meets the cab, and a reinforced front edge to shed water away from the windshield.

## Fitting Tips

1. Clean the shell — dust under a cover acts like sandpaper.
2. Drape the cover over the cab-to-shell junction for full coverage.
3. Buckle the straps under the bed rails, snug but not drum-tight.
4. Check the cover after heavy wind or snow; reseat it if it shifted.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Sizing by the truck bed, not the shell.** Bed length is meaningless once a shell is installed. Measure the shell itself — caps are taller and wider than the bed rails, and the cover must clear the roofline.

**Forgetting the cab junction.** The gap between the cab and the shell collects leaves and moisture. A cover that stops at the bed rails leaves this seam exposed; choose one long enough to overlap it.

**Skipping the under-rail straps.** Wind finds every loose edge. Secure all strap points under the bed rails, or the cover lifts and abrades the shell's paint.

**Covering a dirty shell.** Dust and pollen trapped under the fabric act as sandpaper on the gel coat. Wash the shell before covering, especially after gravel-road trips.

## The Golden Rule

**Fit the cover to the shell's tallest point, and secure it at every strap point.** A cover that shifts in the wind does more harm than no cover at all.
