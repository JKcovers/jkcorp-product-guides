# Car Cover for Lifted Trucks and 4x4s — Sizing the Extra Height

Lifted trucks present a specific cover problem: a suspension lift adds height without adding length. Covers sized by length alone assume stock height, so a cover can fit bumper-to-bumper and still pull tight over the roof, stressing seams and leaving the windshield shoulder exposed.

## The Lifted Truck Problem

Pickup covers are selected by overall length, in tiers running from T1 at roughly 195 inches to T5 at roughly 268 inches. A lift does not move a truck between those tiers, because the length is unchanged. What changes is the third dimension.

The result is a cover that is correct in tier but short in height. On a lifted truck the fabric is stretched over the roofline instead of draping, which loads the seams constantly and shortens service life.

## Critical Measurements for Lifted Trucks

| Measurement | How to Measure | Why It Matters |
|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| Overall length | Bumper to bumper | Selects the tier (T1–T5) |
| Overall height | Ground to highest point (cab roof or bed cap) | Determines height clearance |
| Tire size | Outside diameter of mounted tires | Larger tires add height |
| Bed accessories | Toolbox, bed cap, rack height | Changes the profile |

## Sizing Strategy

Start with the tier. A crew-cab half-ton with a short bed sits in T4; a heavy-duty crew cab with a longer bed sits in T5. A lift does not change that selection.

Then deal with height separately. As a working allowance for a universal pattern, add roughly 4 to 6 inches of height for every 2 inches of suspension lift, and treat oversized tires as adding height in their own right — a truck on 35-inch tires needs the same consideration as a lifted one, even at stock ride height.

Where a supplier offers a height-adjusted pattern within the tier, specify it rather than sizing up a whole tier. Sizing up solves the height problem by adding length the truck does not need, which leaves excess fabric to flap at the bumpers.

**When in doubt, size up one class.** A cover that is slightly long can be cinched with straps and the elastic hem; a cover that is too short leaves the hood or the bed exposed.

## Features for Lifted Trucks

- **Deep mirror pockets:** wide truck mirrors need room, or a mirror-free front section with generous A-pillar width
- **Extended side panels:** cover the taller bodywork and reach below the rocker panels
- **Reinforced seams:** extra fabric over the roofline stresses seams first
- **Adjustable straps:** must reach the taller frame and be re-tensioned after storms

## The Bed Consideration

If the truck carries a bed cap, tonneau or rack, the height calculation changes entirely — measure to the tallest accessory, not the cab roof. A bed cap on a T4 truck can push the required height past what a standard T4 pattern provides, and the same applies to a headache rack or a loaded ladder rack. Custom sizing based on the full setup is the safest option.
