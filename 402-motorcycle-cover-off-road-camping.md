# Motorcycle Cover for Off-Road Camping

Covering a bike at a campsite is a different problem from covering it at home: no garage, no power, often no shelter, and weather that arrives without warning.

The cover also has to earn its place in luggage that is already full.

## What the cover is up against

**Overnight dew.** The most consistent problem, and the one riders underestimate. A bike left out under a clear sky is wet by morning even when it does not rain, because the surfaces cool below the dew point.

**Rain arriving sideways.** Campsites are often exposed, and a bike on a side stand presents a large flat area to wind-driven rain.

**Dust and grit.** Trailheads and desert camps are dusty, and dust on a wet bike becomes an abrasive film.

**Wildlife and livestock.** On farmland and in some wilderness areas, animals investigate bikes. A cover reduces the appeal of an open seat and exposed wiring.

**Condensation from the ground.** A bike parked on grass or damp ground pulls moisture upward through the cover's hem.

## The luggage constraint

This is the deciding factor. A cover carried on a motorcycle has to justify its weight and volume against everything else in the luggage.

- **Packed volume matters more than fabric weight.** A bulky cover that fills a pannier will be left at home.
- **A separate stuff sack is essential**, because a cover that shares space with clothing will make everything damp.
- **Wet packing must be anticipated.** A cover removed in the rain is a wet object for the rest of the trip.

For a two-night trip, a thin, compact cover is the right answer even if it is less durable. For a base camp where the bike stands for a week, a heavier cover carried in a vehicle makes more sense.

## What to specify for this use

**Compact packed size**, with a stuff sack that has a drain or mesh panel.

**A hem that closes.** A drawstring or elasticated hem stops the cover inflating in wind and stops it lifting off in the night. On an exposed site this is the difference between a cover and a sail.

**Breathability.** A sealed cover used night after night in cool conditions will condense water onto the bike. Vents or a breathable panel let vapour escape.

**Quick drying.** The cover will be packed wet at least once. A fabric that dries during a day's riding is worth more than one that arrives home damp.

**Adequate coverage of the seat and controls.** At a campsite, the seat and switchgear are what the rider needs dry in the morning. A cover that stops above the seat is not doing the job this use requires.

## Practical routine at a campsite

1. Park with the cover's hem clear of long grass, which wicks water.
2. Fit the cover before the evening dew rather than after it.
3. Close the hem and, if the site is exposed, run a strap under the bike.
4. Remove the cover in the morning and shake it out before packing, so grit does not travel with it.
5. Dry the cover at the first opportunity — over a pannier in the sun, or at a fuel stop.

## What not to expect

A lightweight travel cover is not a substitute for a proper storage cover during a long off-season. It is a short-term shelter that travels. Buying one product to do both usually produces a cover that is too heavy to carry and too light to last.
