# Tornado and Wind Storm Car Cover Security

High winds are the most dangerous weather for a parked car with a cover on it. A loose cover in a storm becomes a flapping sail that can scratch the paint, snap antennas, or tear itself to shreds. Wind storm car cover security comes down to three things: the right fabric, the right fit, and correct securing. Here is the full guide.

## How Wind Damages Covers and Cars

- Flapping fabric acts like sandpaper on the clear coat
- Loose hems catch wind and balloon, lifting the cover off the car
- Straps that snap can whip across the paint
- A cover blown off the car leaves it fully exposed mid-storm
- Fabric rubbing on sharp edges (mirrors, antennas) wears through quickly

## Choosing a Wind-Ready Cover

### Fabric Weight and Weave

- 420D Oxford minimum; 600D+ for exposed, high-wind locations
- Tight weave resists tearing at stress points
- Multi-layer covers with a waterproof shell handle wind-driven rain better
- Avoid thin single-layer covers for storm-prone areas

### Elastic and Straps

The securing system matters as much as the fabric:

| Feature | Why It Matters |
|---------|----------------|
| Reinforced elastic hem | Keeps the cover snug on the lower body |
| Anti-flap straps | Cross under the car to lock the cover down |
| Wind clips | Attach straps to wheels to prevent lift |
| Center tension straps | Stop ballooning on the roof |
| Reinforced grommets | Extra anchor points for bungee cords |

### Custom Fit

A custom-fit cover has less loose fabric than a universal cover, which means less surface area for the wind to grab. For permanent outdoor parking in wind zones, custom fit is worth the extra cost.

## Securing Protocol for Storm Warnings

1. **Remove the antenna** or use an antenna pocket — a whipping antenna tears covers from inside
2. **Fold side mirrors in** — reduces snag points and wind profile
3. **Tighten all straps** — go around the car twice, checking each strap
4. **Add wind clips on all four wheels** if your cover supports them
5. **Park nose-to-wind** — the car's shape deflects wind better than its flat side
6. **Check after the first gusts** — re-tension straps once the cover settles

## The Parking Position Matters

- Park with the front of the car facing the prevailing wind
- Avoid parking beside open fields where wind accelerates
- Use a windbreak (wall, fence, or another vehicle) when possible
- Park on level ground — hillside gusts are stronger and more turbulent

## When NOT to Use a Cover in a Storm

In extreme conditions — hurricane-force winds, tornado warnings, or wind speeds above 60 mph (100 km/h) — the cover can do more harm than good. A flapping cover in 100 km/h wind can scratch paint even with straps. In these cases:

- Take the cover off and accept exposure, or
- Move the car to shelter, or
- Use a heavy-duty storm cover with a full strap system designed for high wind

## Maintaining a Wind-Worn Cover

After storm season:

- Inspect seams and strap anchors for fatigue
- Check the elastic hem for loss of tension
- Look for thinning fabric at contact points (mirrors, bumpers)
- Replace straps immediately if frayed — a snapped strap in the next storm is a paint scratch
