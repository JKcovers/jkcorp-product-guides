# Top 5 Motorcycle Cover Buying Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

Most motorcycle cover problems come from the same five buying mistakes. Here's how to spot and avoid each one.

## Mistake 1: Buying by Bike Brand Instead of Size

**The mistake:** "I have a Honda CB650R, so I'll buy the Honda cover."

**Why it fails:** Cover sizes follow dimensions, not brands. A sport bike and a cruiser with the same brand share nothing in dimensions.

**Fix:** Measure overall length and height. Buy by the sizing chart, not the brand logo.

## Mistake 2: Ignoring the Exhaust Heat Problem

**The mistake:** Buying any cover without checking heat resistance.

**Why it fails:** Exhaust pipes stay hot 30-60 minutes after a ride. Standard fabrics melt on contact.

**Fix:** Require heat-resistant lower panels rated to 300°F+.

## Mistake 3: Choosing Waterproof Over Everything

**The mistake:** "100% waterproof" sounds ideal, so you buy the fully sealed cover.

**Why it fails:** Zero breathability traps condensation against the bike, causing rust and mildew — especially in humid climates or after a warm ride.

**Fix:** Look for "waterproof AND breathable" — a multi-layer construction with a vapor-escape membrane.

## Mistake 4: Skipping Wind Security

**The mistake:** A cheap cover with no straps.

**Why it fails:** The first windy night, the cover lifts and flaps, abrading your paint — or blows off entirely.

**Fix:** Require a belly strap and elastic hem as non-negotiables.

## Mistake 5: Ignoring Lock Compatibility

**The mistake:** No lock hole, so you can't secure the cover or the bike.

**Why it fails:** An unsecured cover is a theft convenience — it hides the bike from casual view but doesn't slow a thief at all.

**Fix:** Choose covers with reinforced lock holes.

## The Checklist

- [ ] Sized by measurement, not brand
- [ ] Heat-resistant panels (300°F+)
- [ ] Waterproof AND breathable
- [ ] Belly strap + elastic hem
- [ ] Reinforced lock holes
