# BSCI Audits for Cover Factories: How to Read the Report

"We are BSCI audited" is one of the most common claims in the cover trade and one of the least informative on its own. BSCI is a social compliance system, not a product certification, and a report is only meaningful when you know the grade, the date and the scope.

This guide explains what the audit covers, what it does not, and which four details turn a claim into evidence.

## What BSCI is

BSCI is operated by amfori, a business association for trade. Factories are audited against a social compliance code covering working conditions: working hours, wages and benefits, child and young worker protection, forced labour, freedom of association, discrimination, health and safety, and environmental practice at site level.

Audits are carried out by approved auditing companies, and the results are shared through the amfori platform between the factory and its customers. A factory cannot issue a BSCI report to itself.

## What it does not cover

This is the part buyers most often misunderstand:

- **Product quality.** Nothing in the audit measures whether a cover is sewn correctly.
- **Chemical safety of the product.** Substance restrictions are a REACH and testing question, not a social audit question.
- **Fabric performance.** No test data comes out of a social audit.

A BSCI report and a fabric test report answer different questions. Holding one does not imply the other.

## The four details that matter

**The grade.** Results are graded, with the best outcomes indicating a well-managed site and lower grades indicating issues that require correction. A grade without a date is close to meaningless, because it can be several cycles old.

**The date and cycle.** Audits are periodic, and the report has a validity window. Ask for the current one, not a certificate that has been framed since before the pandemic.

**The scope.** Which legal entity, which site, and whether it covers the lines that will produce your order. A group holding a report for one building does not mean your order runs in that building.

**The findings.** A report with a mid-range grade usually lists specific issues and a corrective action plan. Read the findings rather than the headline — a working-hours finding and a fire-safety finding are not equivalent risks.

## How to use it in sourcing

Use the audit as a filter at shortlist stage, then verify the specifics:

- compare the audited entity name against the contracting entity
- check that the site address matches where production will run
- ask how open findings were closed
- ask what the factory changed as a result

That last question separates factories that treat the audit as a marketing asset from those that use it to fix things.

## What to ask for alongside it

A social audit says nothing about the product. For a cover order you also want fabric test reports, an inspection clause, and a signed specification. Buyers who collect only the BSCI certificate often arrive at the quality conversation with nothing to show.
