# Car Cover Inner Lining Guide — Why the Inside Matters More Than the Outside

Most buyers obsess over the outer fabric of a car cover — waterproof rating, denier, UV rejection. But the inner lining is what touches your paint, and it's where cheap covers fail catastrophically.

## The Three Inner Lining Types

**Fleece (best):** Soft brushed polyester that feels like a blanket. It cushions the paint against impacts, wicks away light moisture, and won't scratch even on soft clear coats. Premium covers use 150-200 gsm fleece.

**Non-woven (good):** Bonded polypropylene fabric — the same material as quality shopping bags but softer. It's breathable and dust-tight, but less plush than fleece. Standard on mid-range covers.

**Bare polyester (avoid):** The inner face of plain polyester is rough at the microscopic level. On hot days, when paint is soft, a bare polyester lining can micro-scratch the clear coat. This is the most common cause of "the cover scratched my car" complaints.

## Why Hot Weather Makes Linings Risky

Paint softens above 140°F (60°C). A cover sitting in summer sun traps heat between the fabric and the panel. If the inner lining is rough, the combination of heat-softened paint and fabric movement (from wind or installation) creates swirl marks. Fleece minimizes this because the fibers are too soft to abrade.

## What to Check Before Buying

- **Rub test:** Run the back of your hand across the inner surface. It should feel like a soft sweater, not a shirt.
- **Ask for the GSM:** Quality fleece is 150+ gsm. Anything under 100 gsm is likely thin polyester sprayed to feel soft.
- **Check the seam construction:** Lining seams should be flat or covered — exposed seams are pressure points that can mark paint.
