# Spare Tire Cover Size Chart: Matching 13 to 17 Inch Wheels

Spare tire cover sizing goes wrong for one recurring reason: buyers order by rim diameter, but covers are made to fit the overall diameter of the inflated tire. A 15-inch rim can carry anything from a compact passenger tire to a large off-road tyre, and those two assemblies are several inches apart in diameter.

This guide explains how to calculate the number that actually matters, and how to translate it into a cover size range.

## The calculation

Overall tire diameter is derived from the sidewall code. For a tire marked 225/75R15:

1. Section width = 225 mm
2. Aspect ratio = 75%, so sidewall height = 225 × 0.75 = 168.75 mm
3. Convert to inches: 168.75 ÷ 25.4 = 6.64 inches
4. There are two sidewalls across the diameter: 6.64 × 2 = 13.29 inches
5. Add the rim diameter: 13.29 + 15 = **28.3 inches**

That last figure is the number a cover must fit.

## Worked examples

| Tire code | Sidewall height | Overall diameter |
|---|---|---:|
| 185/65R15 | 4.73 in | **24.5 in** |
| 225/75R15 | 6.64 in | **28.3 in** |
| 235/75R15 | 6.94 in | **28.9 in** |
| 265/70R16 | 7.30 in | **30.6 in** |
| 275/65R18 | 7.04 in | **32.1 in** |

Note how the two 15-inch examples differ by more than four inches overall. Ordering "a 15-inch cover" for both would be a mistake.

## Converting diameter to a cover size band

Production covers are normally grouped into overlapping diameter bands rather than one size per rim. A typical structure groups smaller passenger sizes, a mid band covering common SUV and light truck assemblies, and larger bands for off-road and commercial tires. Because the bands overlap, one cover often fits several popular tire codes — which is exactly why a range-based size chart reduces returns.

## Depth matters too

Diameter alone is not sufficient for rear-carrier covers, because the cover wraps the tread. A wide, aggressive off-road tire has a larger section width and needs more depth than a narrow passenger tire of the same diameter. When specifying, give the supplier both overall diameter and section width, and confirm the depth allowance.

## Practical specifying tips

- Measure the **mounted, inflated** tire with a tape across the centre, not the rim.
- Allow for manufacturing tolerance; specify a target diameter with a range.
- For winch-mounted spares tucked under the vehicle, clearance to exhaust and chassis components matters more than aesthetics.
- Publish a size chart that lists tire codes, not just inches — buyers search by the code on their sidewall.

## Common mistakes

Fitting a cover sized to the rim rather than the tire; ignoring depth on wide off-road assemblies; and assuming a "universal" cover will hold shape on a large tire at highway speed. Heavier fabric and a stronger hem are the fix for the last one.

A correct size chart is one of the highest-value assets a supplier can publish, because it removes the buyer's biggest source of uncertainty.
