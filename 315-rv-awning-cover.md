# RV Awning Protection — The Complete Cover Guide

The RV awning is the most expensive fabric on the coach — and the most exposed. Sun, wind, and rain attack it every season, and replacing an awning costs hundreds. RV awning protection starts with a proper cover. This guide covers types, fitting, and maintenance.

## Why the Awning Needs Its Own Cover

Rolled-up awnings sit on the coach side for months between trips. During storage they face:

- **UV degradation** — the top fold of the fabric fades and becomes brittle
- **Bird droppings and tree sap** — acidic stains that etch the fabric
- **Dust and pollen** — grit that abrades the fabric when the awning rolls and unrolls
- **Wind whipping** — loose fabric flaps and fatigues the end caps

## Types of RV Awning Covers

| Type | Protection | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Fabric sleeve cover | UV + dirt + light rain | Full-time storage |
| Rigid ABS cover | Impact + UV + hail | Harsh sun and storm areas |
| Universal stretch cover | UV + dust, easy on/off | Between trips |

## Fitting the Cover Correctly

1. Extend the awning 30-60 cm so the fabric is exposed and the cover slides on easily.
2. Slide the cover over the rolled awning, centered end to end.
3. Secure both end caps with the sewn-in straps or buckles.
4. Zip or close the cover's full-length opening if it has one.
5. Retract the awning until the cover seats against the coach.

## Maintenance That Extends Awning Life

- Clean the awning fabric before covering — trapped grit grinds into the weave.
- Check the cover's seams twice a year; replace worn straps before they fail.
- In snow areas, brush heavy snow off the cover so the weight does not distort the awning.

## Common Questions

**Can I leave the cover on while driving?** No. Fabric sleeves can catch wind and chafe the coach side at highway speed. Install the cover for storage; remove it for travel.

**How often should I replace the cover?** A quality awning cover lasts 2-4 seasons depending on sun exposure. Replace it when the stitching frays or the fabric no longer sheds water — a worn cover traps moisture against the awning.

**Does the cover protect against hail?** Fabric sleeves reduce hail impact but do not stop it. Rigid ABS covers are the only option for genuine hail protection in storm-prone areas.

**Should I cover the awning arms too?** Most covers cover the rolled fabric only. The arms and springs benefit from a light coat of lubricant and a wipe-down before the storage season begins.

## The Golden Rule

**Cover a clean, dry, fully rolled awning — never a wet one.** Moisture trapped under the cover is the fastest route to mildew on both fabrics.
