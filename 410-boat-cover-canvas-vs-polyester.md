# Boat Cover: Canvas vs Polyester

Canvas and polyester are the two fabric families that dominate boat covers, and the choice between them is usually presented as a quality question. It is not. They are different materials with different failure modes, and the right answer depends on how the boat is stored and what the owner will actually maintain.

## What each material is

**Canvas** in the marine trade usually means a woven cotton or poly-cotton fabric, often treated for water repellency. It is heavy, breathable and traditional.

**Polyester** covers are woven synthetic fabrics, commonly coated or laminated on the inside. They are lighter, resist water at the surface, and are usually UV-stabilised.

## Head-to-head

| Property | Canvas | Coated polyester |
|---|---|---|
| Breathability | High | Low to moderate |
| Water shedding | Good when treated, poor when saturated | Good, from the surface |
| Weight when wet | Very heavy | Barely changes |
| Condensation risk | Lower | Higher under a sealed cover |
| Mould risk | Higher if stored damp | Lower on the face, higher in seams |
| Abrasion resistance | Good | Moderate; coating can scuff |
| Repairability | Excellent — sewn and patched | Good, but coating damage is hard to restore |
| Appearance | Traditional, matte | Technical, often glossy |
| Maintenance | Re-treat periodically | Rinse; avoid harsh detergents |

## The breathability question

This is the property that decides most real cases. A cover that cannot breathe traps moisture from the boat, the air and the ground, and that moisture condenses on the coldest surfaces — usually metal and gelcoat.

Canvas manages this by allowing vapour to pass. Coated polyester manages it by blocking liquid water while, in better constructions, allowing some vapour through, or by adding vents.

On a boat stored outdoors in a damp climate, breathability is worth more than a higher water resistance. On a boat stored under cover or in a dry climate, the balance shifts toward water shedding and weight.

## The weight question

A wet canvas cover is heavy. On a large boat, that matters in three ways:

- **Handling.** Fitting and removing a saturated canvas cover is hard work and a safety issue on a wet deck.
- **Support.** A heavy wet cover loads its poles and frame far more than a dry one.
- **Seams.** The weight pulls on every seam, and the lowest seams take the most.

Coated polyester sheds water and stays light, which is why it dominates the trailered-boat market.

## Where each wins

**Canvas suits:** boats stored in dry climates, traditional boats where appearance matters, owners willing to re-treat the fabric, and situations where condensation is the dominant problem.

**Polyester suits:** trailered boats, boats stored under trees or in high dust, owners who want low maintenance, and any application where weight and packed size matter.

A middle option exists: poly-cotton canvas with a synthetic coating, which keeps some breathability while shedding water better than untreated canvas.

## What to ask a supplier

- the exact fibre composition, not just "canvas"
- the coating type and whether it is on one or both faces
- the fabric weight, and how it is measured
- UV stabilisation and any test evidence
- seam treatment, since this is where both materials leak
- care instructions, including whether re-treatment is required
