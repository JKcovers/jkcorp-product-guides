# Spare Tire Cover for SUVs and Off-Road Vehicles

Rear-mounted spares are common on SUVs, pickups and dedicated off-road vehicles, and they are exposed in a way that a hidden spare never is. The tire sits at the back of the vehicle facing traffic, taking the full force of sun, rain, road spray and, on off-road vehicles, mud and grit. A cover is a small item with an outsized effect on how the tire and the vehicle look after a few seasons.

## Why SUV and off-road fitment is different

Three factors separate this segment from passenger car applications:

**Tire size.** Off-road assemblies are substantially larger in overall diameter than passenger tires, and often much wider. A cover sized for a typical passenger car spare will not fit.

**Depth requirement.** A wide, aggressive tread pattern means the cover must wrap a greater depth. Diameter alone is not sufficient to specify the fit.

**Airflow at speed.** A large cover on a rear carrier is fully exposed to the slipstream. Lighter fabrics and weak hems flap, which stresses stitching and can mark the vehicle's paint over time.

## Fitment by carrier type

- **Rear door-mounted carrier** — the most common. The cover wraps the tire and is retained by a full elastic hem or a drawstring. Access to the door handle and any rear camera must remain unobstructed.
- **Under-vehicle winch mount** — the tire is tucked beneath the body. Fit here favours a fully elasticated cover, and clearance to exhaust components and the chassis should be checked.
- **Swing-away carrier** — common on off-road builds. The cover must tolerate the movement of the carrier without working loose.
- **Hard-shell carrier** — a cover is optional and usually fitted for appearance or branding.

## Specification priorities for this segment

| Priority | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Heavier fabric | Holds shape on large tires and resists flapping |
| Reinforced hem | Takes the highest tension and fails first on budget covers |
| Soft inner layer | Protects alloys and painted wheels from abrasion |
| Wider size bands | Off-road tires vary more than passenger sizes, so range coverage matters |
| Secure retention | A cover lost at highway speed is a safety issue, not just a nuisance |

## Customisation is a strong seller here

Off-road owners are unusually receptive to printed covers. Vehicle graphics, brand logos, terrain motifs and club marks all translate well onto the panel. Suppliers should confirm how the print behaves when the cover is stretched over a large tire: artwork placed close to the hem will distort.

## Practical buying notes

Ask the customer for the tire sidewall code and calculate overall diameter rather than relying on the rim size, which is unreliable across this segment. Confirm section width so the depth is right, and where a vehicle has a rear camera or sensor mounted on the carrier, verify that the cover does not interfere with it.

For suppliers, a size table expressed in tire codes — the markings a customer can actually read off their own vehicle — converts better than one expressed only in centimetres.
