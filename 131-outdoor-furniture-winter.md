# Outdoor Furniture Winter Storage — Covers vs Indoor Storage

When winter hits, outdoor furniture faces its harshest test. The right strategy — covers, indoor storage, or both — determines whether your furniture survives to spring.

## The Winter Threats

- **Freeze-thaw moisture:** Water in cushions, frames, and welds freezes and expands
- **UV (still present):** Winter sun is weaker but fabric still fades
- **Snow load:** Heavy snow bends and stresses frames
- **Ice:** Expanding ice in crevices cracks materials
- **Wind:** Blows covers and furniture around, causing impacts

## Covers vs Indoor Storage

| Factor | Quality Covers | Indoor Storage |
|--------|:--:|:--:|
| Cost | $20-60 | Free (if space exists) |
| Space needed | Minimal | Garage/shed space |
| Protection level | ⚠️ Good | ✅ Best |
| Convenience | ✅ Keep outside | ❌ Move furniture |
| Lifespan effect | Extends 2-3x | Extends indefinitely |

## The Right Strategy by Furniture Type

**Wicker/rattan:** Indoor storage strongly preferred — wicker is fragile and moisture-sensitive. If outside, waterproof cover + elevated off the ground.

**Aluminum frames:** Covers work well — aluminum doesn't rust. Store cushions separately.

**Steel frames:** Covers + rust inspection. Steel rusts; treat scratches before winter.

**Wood (teak, cedar):** Covers protect the finish. Teak can stay outside with a breathable cover.

**Cushions:** ALWAYS store indoors. Cushions hold moisture and mildew faster than anything else.

## Cover Essentials for Winter

- Waterproof with sealed seams
- Ventilation (condensation kills furniture too)
- Straps/drawstrings for wind
- Breathable fabric over plastic (plastic traps moisture)

## The Pro Routine

1. Clean and dry furniture before covering
2. Store cushions indoors
3. Use breathable waterproof covers
4. Elevate off the ground (airflow)
5. Check covers monthly, brush off snow
