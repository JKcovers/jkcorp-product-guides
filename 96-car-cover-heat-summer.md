# Car Cover Heat Management in Summer — Keep Interior 20°F Cooler

A car parked in summer sun becomes an oven — interior temps can hit 150-170°F. The right cover reduces this by 15-20°F, protecting your dashboard, seats, and electronics.

## How Heat Builds Up

Sunlight passes through windows, heats interior surfaces, and the trapped air can't escape. This is the greenhouse effect, and it's why a shaded car is dramatically cooler than a sunny one. A cover that blocks sunlight at the exterior breaks the cycle.

## Cover Features That Reduce Heat

**Light color:** The single biggest factor. A silver or white cover reflects 70-80% of solar radiation. A black cover absorbs up to 90%, turning your car into a heat trap.

**Reflective outer layer:** Aluminum or titanium-dioxide-coated fabric reflects sunlight before it reaches the car. Measured results: 15-20°F cooler interior.

**Breathable construction:** A non-breathable cover traps the heat it blocks. Breathable multi-layer fabric lets trapped hot air escape.

**Full coverage:** A cover that only reaches the roofline leaves the greenhouse effect intact through the windows.

## The Temperature Data

| Scenario | Interior Temperature |
|----------|:--:|
| No cover, direct sun | 150-170°F |
| Dark cover | 135-150°F |
| Light cover | 120-135°F |
| Reflective cover | 110-125°F |
| Reflective + ventilated | 100-115°F |

## Beyond Temperature: UV

Heat is one problem; UV is another. A cover with 99% UV rejection blocks both the heat AND the radiation that fades dashboards and cracks leather. Temperature reduction matters, but UV blocking is what actually preserves your interior.

## Summer Usage Tips

- Let the car cool 15-20 minutes before covering (hot engine stress)
- Use a windshield sunshade UNDER the cover for maximum effect
- Choose light colors — the color matters more than the material
