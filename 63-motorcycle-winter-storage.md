# Motorcycle Winter Storage Guide — Protect Your Bike Through the Cold Season

Storing a motorcycle for winter is more than parking it in the garage. Without proper preparation, cold weather and moisture damage engines, batteries, and components. Here's the complete winter storage playbook.

## Step 1: Prepare the Bike

**Change the oil.** Old oil contains combustion byproducts and moisture that corrode engine internals during months of inactivity.

**Stabilize the fuel.** Fill the tank and add fuel stabilizer. A full tank prevents condensation from forming inside the tank.

**Protect the battery.** If you can't keep the battery on a maintainer, remove it and store it indoors on a trickle charger. Cold kills batteries faster than any other factor.

## Step 2: Clean and Detail

Wash the bike thoroughly before storage. Road salt, bugs, and grime attract moisture and corrode metal surfaces. Apply a coat of wax to paint and chrome. Treat the chain with a heavy layer of chain lube to prevent rust.

## Step 3: Choose the Right Cover

For winter storage, the cover does two jobs: keeps dust off and prevents condensation.

**Indoor storage:** A breathable fleece-lined cover is ideal. The key is breathability — a non-breathable cover traps moisture against cold metal, causing condensation that corrodes components.

**Outdoor storage:** You need a waterproof cover with a breathable middle layer. Look for 420D Oxford or heavier, with heat-resistant panels and a belly strap. If the bike sits outside all winter, consider a two-layer approach: fleece liner inside, waterproof shell outside.

## Step 4: Monthly Checks

Even covered, check the bike monthly: look for moisture under the cover, verify the battery maintainer is working, and confirm no pests have nested in the exhaust.
