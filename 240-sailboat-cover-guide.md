# Sailboat Cover Guide — Protecting Mast, Boom, and Cabin

Sailboats present a unique covering challenge: the mast and rigging stay up, the boom spans the cockpit, and the cabin top carries hardware that a flat cover can't accommodate. A sailboat cover protects the deck, winches, sails (if left furled), and cabin from sun and weather — but only if it's designed for the job.

## What a Sailboat Cover Protects

- **Cabin top and dodger** — sun damage to canvas and fiberglass
- **Winches and deck hardware** — corrosion from salt and moisture
- **Furled sails** — UV damage when left exposed
- **Cockpit cushions and electronics** — weather protection
- **Hatches and ports** — leak prevention through aged seals

## Cover Types by Sailboat Size

| Boat Size | Recommended Cover |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Daysailer (under 20 ft) | One-piece full cover |
| Coastal cruiser (20-30 ft) | Two-piece: cabin cover + boom cover |
| Offshore cruiser (30+ ft) | Custom full-length cover with mast collar |

### Mast Collar

A mast collar seals the cover around the mast so rain and wind don't enter at the mast base. Look for:

- Adjustable drawstring or bungee closure
- Padding to protect the mast finish
- A design that sheds water away from the mast step

## Material Selection

- **Acrylic canvas (Sunbrella-type)** — gold standard for sailboat covers; UV-stable, breathable, mildew-resistant
- **Heavy Oxford polyester with PU coating** — budget alternative for trailerable boats
- **Avoid PVC** — traps condensation under the cover, damaging varnish and teak

## Design Features to Look For

- **Vents** — sailboat cabins need airflow; look for solar or passive vents
- **Reinforced grommets** — for tie-downs around stanchions and cleats
- **Lift points** — some covers have loops for boom lift attachment
- **Access zippers** — to reach winches or companionway without uncovering
- **Teak protection** — padded contact points where the cover touches wooden rails

## Fitting and Securing

1. Install the mast collar first, sealing around the mast
2. Drape the cover over the boom, working from mast to stern
3. Secure with lines tied to stanchions and cleats — bungee cords alone are not enough in wind
4. Tension the cover so water runs off, not pools
5. Check all contact points for chafe where the cover meets hardware

## Mooring vs. Trailer Storage

- **Moored sailboats** — the cover must shed wind-driven rain and resist flapping; use dedicated sailboat covers with reinforced hems
- **Trailerable sailboats** — the mast often stays up on the trailer; the same full cover works, but add highway-grade strapping

## Maintenance

- Wash with a soft brush and boat soap
- Re-apply water repellent annually
- Inspect the mast collar area regularly — it wears first
- Repair small tears immediately; wind finds any weak point
