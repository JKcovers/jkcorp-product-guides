# Boat Cover FAQ — 20 Questions Every Owner Asks

Boat covers are the cheapest insurance a boat owner can buy — yet they cause more confusion than almost any other marine purchase. Sizing, fabric, venting, and winter use all have traps. This boat cover FAQ answers the 20 questions owners ask most.

## The Top 20 Questions

**1. Does my boat really need a cover?**
Yes if it sits outside — covers protect gel coat from UV, keep rain out of the cockpit, and stop bilge pooling.

**2. Indoor or outdoor boat cover?**
Indoor covers block dust. Outdoor covers must handle UV, rain, wind, and pooling water — a much higher specification.

**3. What fabric is best for boat covers?**
Marine-grade acrylic, heavy polyester, or canvas with water resistance. Marine acrylic breathes and resists UV; coated polyester sheds water better. Both beat generic tarps.

**4. How do I measure my boat?**
Measure the beam (width) and the length at the widest points, plus the height to the windshield. Covers are usually sized by beam and length ranges.

**5. Should I buy a custom cover?**
For boats over 18 feet or with T-tops and hardtops, custom-fit covers are worth it; universal covers fit poorly on tall boats.

**6. What are support poles for?**
Poles create a peak so rain runs off; without them, water pools and collapses the cover.

**7. Waterproof or breathable?**
Outdoor boat covers need water resistance and breathability — a sealed cover grows mildew inside the boat.

**8. Can I cover a wet boat?**
Never. Water trapped under the cover causes mildew, corrosion, and damage. Dry the boat first.

**9. How do I keep the cover on in wind?**
Use a tie-down system — straps or rope through the cover's grommets to the trailer or cleats. Never rely on elastic alone.

**10. Does a cover protect against winter snow?**
Yes — with support poles and straps rated for weight. Heavy snow collapses unsupported covers.

**11. How long does a boat cover last?**
3-6 years for quality marine fabric. UV and wind are the main wear factors.

**12. Can I machine wash a boat cover?**
Some, with care — check the label. Most boat covers are too large for home machines; hand-wash or professional clean.

**13. Will a cover scratch the gel coat?**
A soft-lined cover on a clean boat won't. Rough fabric or trapped debris can. Keep the boat clean before covering.

**14. Do I need vent panels?**
Yes, especially in humid climates — vents stop mildew on upholstery.

**15. What about trailering with the cover on?**
Only a cover rated for highway travel with proper tie-downs — mooring covers rip at speed.

**16. Can a cover protect electronics?**
Indirectly — it keeps rain off the console but does not waterproof electronics. Protect sensitive gear.

**17. How do I stop water pooling?**
Support poles, a taut fit, and a slight peak in the middle. Check after heavy rain.

**18. Should I cover the outboard motor?**
Use a separate motor cover, or one that includes the cowling area to block UV.

**19. How do I store the cover?**
Clean, dry, and fold loosely in a storage bag. Never store it damp.

**20. Is a cheap tarp good enough?**
Short-term, yes. Long-term, tarps chafe, trap moisture, and fail in UV within a season. A real boat cover pays for itself.

## Final Advice

Measure carefully, choose marine fabric, use support poles and proper tie-downs, and always cover a dry boat. Do that and the cover protects your boat for years.
