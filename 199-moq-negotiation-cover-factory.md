# MOQ Negotiation for Cover Factories — How to Get Lower Minimums

Minimum order quantity (MOQ) is often the first wall new importers hit. A factory quotes 500 pieces; you want 50. The good news: MOQs for covers are negotiable when you understand where they come from. Here is how to negotiate without breaking the relationship.

## Why Factories Have MOQs

MOQ exists to cover fixed costs:

- **Fabric waste** — cutting a small run wastes more fabric per piece
- **Setup time** — pattern making, cutting templates, machine setup
- **Print plates/screens** — logo printing has fixed setup costs
- **Packaging minimums** — printed bags and boxes have print-run minimums
- **Production slots** — small orders displace bigger ones

## Realistic MOQ Levels for Covers

| Order Type | Typical MOQ |
|------------|-------------|
| Stock design, standard size | 30-100 pcs |
| Custom size, standard fabric | 50-100 pcs |
| Custom logo on stock product | 100-300 pcs |
| Full custom product | 300-1000 pcs |

## Negotiation Levers That Work

1. **Combine SKUs** — ask if you can mix colors and sizes toward one MOQ
2. **Use standard fabric** — custom fabric drives MOQ up; stock fabric keeps it low
3. **Accept stock packaging** — plain polybags instead of printed boxes
4. **Start with a sample order** — prove the product works before demanding low MOQs
5. **Offer a deposit** — a 30% T/T deposit signals commitment
6. **Order off-peak** — factories are more flexible in slow seasons
7. **Commit to repeat orders** — "this is a test run for a 2,000-pc program" is honest and effective
8. **Trade MOQ for price** — higher MOQ is often acceptable if the price drops accordingly

## What NOT to Do

- Don't demand below-cost MOQs — you'll get lower quality instead
- Don't ask for free samples AND minimal MOQ AND rush delivery together
- Don't switch suppliers over MOQ alone — switching costs are hidden

## How to Structure the Conversation

A professional approach:

> "I'm launching with a limited budget. Can you offer 80 pcs at the 100-pc price if I place a repeat order within 3 months? I can commit to that in writing."

Most factories would rather keep a customer growing than lose an order entirely.

## The Trade-Off to Remember

MOQ and unit price trade against each other: lower MOQ usually means higher price per piece, because fixed costs spread over fewer units. A good factory shows you the math instead of hiding it.
