# The Science Behind Waterproof Car Covers — Hydrostatic Head Explained

"Waterproof" means different things to different manufacturers. The only objective measure is hydrostatic head — a lab test that tells you exactly how much water pressure a fabric can withstand before leaking.

## What Is Hydrostatic Head?

A column of water is placed over the fabric, and the height (in millimeters) is increased until water penetrates. The taller the column before leakage, the more waterproof the fabric.

- **1000mm:** Light rain protection. Waterproof for 30-60 minutes of moderate rain.
- **2000mm:** The minimum for outdoor car covers. Handles sustained rain.
- **3000mm:** Standard for quality outdoor covers. Handles heavy rain and pooled water.
- **5000mm:** Premium protection. Handles extreme downpours and snow loads.

## Why This Matters

A cover labeled "waterproof" with no hydrostatic head rating is unverified. The manufacturer might mean "water-resistant in light drizzle" or "truly waterproof in a hurricane" — you can't tell. Always look for a stated rating.

## How Coatings Affect Waterproofing

PU (polyurethane) coating is the most common. It adds 1000-2000mm to the fabric's natural resistance. PVC coating is heavier but adds 2000-3000mm. The best covers combine a dense base fabric (420D+ Oxford) with a PU or PVC coating.
