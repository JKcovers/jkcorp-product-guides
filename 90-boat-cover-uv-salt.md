# Boat Cover UV and Salt Protection — Why Marine Fabric Matters

Boat covers face the most hostile environment of any cover: intense UV, salt spray, and constant moisture. Standard car cover materials fail within a season. Here's what marine-grade actually means.

## The Double Threat: UV + Salt

**UV radiation:** Breaks down polymer bonds. Untreated polyester loses 50% of its strength after 12 months of direct sun. Treated marine fabric retains 90%+ strength at 3 years.

**Salt:** Accelerates degradation by forming crystals that abrade fibers and attract moisture. Salt-damaged fabric tears at the seams first.

## Solution-Dyed vs Coated Fabric

**Solution-dyed polyester** has color pigments mixed into the fiber before extrusion:
- Color cannot fade (pigment is integral)
- UV resistance is built in, not a surface coating
- 5-7 year lifespan in marine use
- The marine industry standard

**Coated polyester** has a surface UV treatment:
- Coating degrades in 2-3 years
- Fabric becomes stiff and brittle
- 2-4 year lifespan

## The UV Rejection Numbers

| Material | UV Rejection | Lifespan (Marine) |
|----------|:--:|:--:|
| Untreated polyester | ~60% | 1-2 years |
| Coated polyester | ~80% | 2-4 years |
| Solution-dyed polyester | 95%+ | 5-7 years |

## Marine Testing Standards

Quality boat cover fabric passes:
- **ASTM D4355** (UV exposure testing)
- **Salt spray testing** (ASTM B117)
- **Hydrostatic head testing** (3000mm+ for covers)

## What to Look For

- "Solution-dyed" explicitly stated (not just "UV treated")
- Marine-grade, not automotive-grade fabric
- Double-stitched, reinforced seams
- Corrosion-resistant hardware (never plain steel snaps)
