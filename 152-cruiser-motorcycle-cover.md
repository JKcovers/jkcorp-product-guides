# Motorcycle Cover for Cruisers — Wide Bikes Need Wide Covers

Cruisers and baggers — Harley-Davidsons, Indian Chiefs, and similar — have a different shape than sport bikes. Their wide handlebars, forward controls, and saddlebags change the cover requirement entirely.

## Why Cruisers Are Harder to Cover

Cruisers are wide. A typical cruiser measures 38-42 inches across at the handlebars, plus saddlebags add another 8-12 inches. Their low, long profile also means more fabric sag if the cover isn't sized right.

## The Cruiser Cover Checklist

**Width allowance:** Choose a cover rated for your handlebar width plus saddlebag clearance. When in doubt, size up one full size.

**Saddlebag coverage:** The cover must fully enclose both saddlebags. Exposed bags crack and fade in UV faster than the rest of the bike.

**Forward control clearance:** Low-slung bikes with forward controls need covers that don't snag on the foot pegs and controls.

**Windshield height:** Tall touring windshields on cruisers add height. Check the cover's height rating.

## Sizing Reality

Many cruiser riders make this mistake: they buy a "Large" sport bike cover and it barely fits. Cruiser covers are sized differently:

| Cover Size | Fits (Length) | Typical Cruiser |
|------------|:--:|----------------|
| Large | 92-100 inches | Dyna, Softail |
| X-Large | 100-108 inches | Road King, Street Glide |
| XX-Large | 108+ inches | Full dressers with bags |

## Features That Matter for Cruisers

- **Extended side panels:** Cover the saddlebags completely
- **Heat-resistant lower sections:** Wide exhaust systems run both sides
- **Lock holes at the sides:** Saddlebag access with a chain lock
- **Extra-long belly straps:** Wide bikes need wider straps to cinch

## The Bottom Line

For cruiser owners, cover width is as important as length. Measure handlebar-to-handlebar and bag-to-bag before buying. An ill-fitting cover flaps, sags, and wears out fast on a cruiser's wide frame.
