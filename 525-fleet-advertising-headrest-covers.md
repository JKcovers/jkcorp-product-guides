# Fleet Advertising Headrest Covers

For a fleet operator, a headrest cover is the cheapest advertising space in the vehicle. It sits directly behind every passenger's head, at eye level, for the whole journey, and it is read without any effort by the reader.

That is why taxi companies, ride-hail operators, corporate fleets and rental firms all use them — and why the specification is driven by operational needs rather than by comfort.

## What makes fleet use different

A private buyer fits a cover once. A fleet rotates it between drivers and vehicles.

- **Turnover.** Covers are changed when a driver changes, when a vehicle is cleaned, or on a fixed cycle.
- **Standardisation.** A fleet has mixed vehicle models, so the cover must fit a range rather than one headrest.
- **Brand control.** The logo must look identical across every vehicle, including older models whose headrests differ.
- **Cleaning.** Covers are washed far more often than a private one, and the print must survive.
- **Replacement.** Covers are a consumable line item, reordered at intervals.

A product designed for retail will usually fail on standardisation and wash durability.

## Fitting a mixed fleet

The practical answer is a stretch cover with no post holes, sized to a range of headrests.

- **Elastic or stretch knit** accommodates different widths and shapes.
- **No fixed post holes** means one SKU covers sleeve-type and winged headrests.
- **A deep hem** stops the cover riding up when a passenger leans back.
- **One size, checked against the widest and narrowest headrests in the fleet**, rather than against an average.

Where the fleet is single-model, a fitted cover looks better and is worth the extra SKU.

## Print and durability

- **Print method.** Screen printing is durable and cost-effective at volume; sublimation gives full-colour coverage on polyester and is the usual choice for photographic or multi-colour logos.
- **Colour fastness to light.** The rear of a headrest sits in direct sun through the rear window. Fading is the most common complaint.
- **Wash durability.** A print that cracks after ten washes is not fit for fleet use. This should be tested, not assumed.
- **Colour matching.** Specify against a physical reference, not a screen colour, and keep the reference with the order.

## Operational details

- **Fitting time.** If a driver changes over at shift handover, the cover must fit in seconds.
- **Labelling.** A discreet vehicle or driver identifier helps with rotation and audit.
- **Kits per vehicle.** Fleet buyers order in sets, so packing should match the vehicle count.
- **Removal without damage.** Hook-and-loop fasteners can pull the headrest fabric; elastic is gentler.

## The commercial case

Headrest covers are usually justified on cost per impression rather than on protection. That changes what to optimise:

- **Coverage of the fleet** matters more than the quality of any single cover.
- **Repeatability** matters more than the initial print.
- **Ease of replacement** matters more than longevity, because a faded cover is worse than a cheap one that is replaced.

## What to ask a supplier

- the headrest size range the cover fits
- fabric composition and stretch
- print method and wash-fastness evidence
- light-fastness evidence
- minimum order and reorder lead time
- whether the print reference is retained for repeats
- packing format for fleet distribution
