# Truck Car Cover Guide: Full Coverage for Pickups

Trucks need a different cover approach than cars: longer wheelbases, cab-plus-bed proportions, and accessories like bed caps and racks change how covers fit. This guide covers cover styles, the tier system used to size pickups, and the specs that keep a cover on in wind.

## Truck Cover Styles

| Style | Coverage | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Full-length cover | Entire truck, cab to tailgate | Outdoor storage, full protection |
| Cab-only cover | Cab and windshield | Quick cover, cab interior protection |
| Bed cover / tonneau | Bed area only | Cargo protection (see tonneau guide) |
| Custom fit per model | Tailored to one truck model | Best fit and wind behaviour |

## Sizing a Pickup: The Tier System

Pickup covers are sized by overall length, bumper to bumper including the tailgate. Universal patterns divide into five tiers:

| Tier | Overall length | Representative models |
|---|---|---|
| T1 (PK-S) | 195–206 in | Tacoma Regular/Access Cab 6' bed; Colorado Extended Cab 6' bed |
| T2 (PK-M) | 207–218 in | Tacoma Double Cab 5' bed; Ranger Crew Cab 5' bed; Jeep Gladiator; Hilux |
| T3 (PK-L) | 219–232 in | F-150 SuperCab 6.5' bed; Silverado 1500 Double Cab 6.5' bed; RAM 1500 Quad Cab 6'4" |
| T4 (PK-XL) | 233–248 in | F-150 SuperCrew 5.5' bed; Silverado 1500 Crew Cab 5.8'; RAM 1500 Crew Cab 5'7"; Tundra CrewMax 5.5' |
| T5 (PK-XXL) | 249–268 in | F-150 SuperCrew 6.5' bed; Super Duty F-250/F-350 Crew Cab; Silverado 2500HD Crew Cab |

Length alone is not enough. Two trucks of equal length can need different patterns because the cab-to-bed ratio differs: a crew cab with a short bed steps down near the middle of the vehicle, while a regular cab with a long bed steps down behind the rear axle. Check both the tier and the cab configuration.

## Accessories and Fit

- **Bed caps and toppers:** need taller patterns or adjustable panels
- **Roof racks and toolboxes:** measure and allow clearance
- **Antennas:** removable or pocketed
- **Towing mirrors:** wider front sections required. Universal patterns avoid fixed mirror pockets and rely on a wide A-pillar section instead

## Fabric and Construction

Heavy-duty fabrics are the norm: 420D to 600D Oxford with coatings, plus reinforced hems. Trucks sit outside more and face wind from all angles, so look for:

- Double-stitched seams along the hem
- Multiple tie-down straps (front, middle, rear)
- Elastic hem specified with around 15 percent stretch allowance, plus grommet points
- UV-stabilized fabric for sun exposure

## Wind Performance

Trucks present a large surface to wind, so strap systems matter more than on cars. Cinch straps at the hood, cab and tailgate, and check tension after storms. In very windy regions, consider a cover with an extra centre strap.

## Quick Takeaways

- Full-length covers protect the whole truck; cab-only covers suit indoor parking
- Size by overall length, then check the cab-to-bed ratio
- Heavy-duty 420D–600D fabric with multiple straps is the minimum for pickups
- Bed caps, racks and towing mirrors change the pattern; measure with them installed
- Check strap tension after storms, because large surfaces catch wind hard
