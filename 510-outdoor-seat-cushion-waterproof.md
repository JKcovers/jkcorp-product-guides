# Waterproof Seat Cushion for Outdoor Use

Waterproof is one of the most overused words in outdoor accessories, and in the seat cushion category it is usually being asked to cover three different things: a base that survives damp ground, a whole cushion that shrugs off a rain shower, and a product that can be left outside without damage.

Those are not the same claim, and a buyer who specifies only one of them will get a product that fails the other two.

## Water-resistant and waterproof are different

Water-resistant fabric resists water for a limited time and sheds splashes. Waterproof describes a material that does not let water pass under the conditions tested. A cushion can have a waterproof base and a water-resistant top, which is often the sensible and economical combination.

The first question to settle is therefore not which material is best, but where the water is coming from: the ground, the sky, or a wet spectator sitting down. Each points to a different part of the construction.

## The base is where failures actually happen

In practice a cushion gets wet from underneath. Damp grass and wet concrete transfer moisture by contact, so an absorbent base fabric turns a dry cushion damp within minutes regardless of how the top performs.

A coated or laminated synthetic base solves this cheaply. The base is also the part that abrades against concrete, so it needs durability as well as water resistance.

## Seams, stitching and zippers

Water enters a stitched product at the needle holes. Where a cushion claims protection from rain rather than only from the ground:

- seams should be taped, sealed or eliminated on the exposed face
- a zipper, if present, should sit behind a flap
- thread should be a synthetic that does not wick

A zipper is convenient for cleaning but is also the most common entry point on a supposedly waterproof cushion.

## Foam choice decides what happens after a soaking

The cover determines whether water gets in. The foam determines what happens if it does.

Open-cell polyurethane foam absorbs water and holds it, so a cushion that takes on water becomes heavy, cold and slow to dry, and can develop odour in storage. Closed-cell polyethylene or EVA foam does not absorb water, so a brief soaking is a surface problem rather than a structural one.

For any cushion intended to be left out, stored damp or used on the ground, closed-cell foam is the specification that makes the waterproof claim meaningful.

## Cleaning and drying

Practical care instructions matter for this product class. A wipe-clean cover over closed-cell foam can be dried with a cloth. A machine-washable cover is only a valid claim if the print and the backing survive the cycle, and that should be verified rather than assumed.

## What to ask a supplier

- which parts of the construction are waterproof and which are only water-resistant
- the base fabric and its coating
- how seams on the exposed face are sealed
- the foam type and whether it absorbs water
- how print durability was checked against cleaning
