# Pontoon Boat Cover and Railings

A pontoon is a deck surrounded by a fence, and the cover must deal with that fence on all four sides — clearing it, resting on it, or wrapping around it.

Getting this wrong produces two failures: a cover that presses on the rail tops and wears through, or a cover that hangs outside the rails and collects water in the gap.

## Three approaches to the railing

**Over the rails.** The cover spans the deck and rests on the top rail: the largest protected volume and the simplest pattern, but every rail top becomes a contact line and every corner a wear point.

**Inside the rails.** The cover fits within the fence, covering the deck and seating but leaving the rails exposed. Lower wind load and no rail contact, but the rails and deck edge are unprotected.

**Around the rails.** The cover is shaped to follow the rail line, with openings or sleeves at the corners. The most protective, and by far the most complex to pattern.

Most are built over the rails with reinforcement at the contact points — a reasonable default if the reinforcement is genuine.

## The contact points

A pontoon rail is a hard, often square-edged aluminium tube. Where the cover rests on it:

- **Abrasion** wears the fabric from the outside and the rail's finish from the inside.
- **Water runs along the rail** and drips at the lowest point, which is often over a seat.
- **Wind lifts the cover** at the rail line, because the rail is a ledge that catches airflow.

Reinforcement at the rail line — a wear strip, a padded section, or a bound edge — is the single most valuable feature on a pontoon cover.

## Straps and the fence

Straps on a pontoon cover cannot simply run to the trailer, because the fence is in the way.

- **Straps must pass through or over the fence** at defined points, and each crossing is a chafe point.
- **Under-deck attachment** is more secure but needs access to the deck structure.
- **Cleat attachment** works where cleats are accessible, but a cleat is a small anchor for a large sail area.

A pontoon cover with a large unsupported span and only four cleat ties will lift in wind, regardless of how well it fits.

## Gates and access

Pontoons have gates, and the cover must accommodate them.

- **A gate used for boarding** is where the owner needs access, which argues for a panel or a shaped opening at that point.

## Support

A pontoon deck is long, flat and wide, so the cover needs support across a large span.

- **Poles along the centreline** at intervals, each under a reinforcement.
- **The fence itself** can carry the perimeter, but not the middle.
- **Seating and consoles** provide hard points but must not be loaded directly without protection.

The span between the fence and the centreline poles is where water pools if the poles are too few or too low.

## What to look for

- reinforcement at every rail contact line
- a pattern that clears the rail corners rather than pressing on them
- strap routing defined relative to the fence, with chafe protection
- gate awareness, with access at the boarding gate
- sufficient centreline support for a long, flat deck
- vents, since an enclosed pontoon volume condenses heavily

## Buying notes

Ask whether the cover is designed over, inside or around the rails, and where the reinforcement is. A pontoon cover that does not state this has probably not been fitted to a pontoon.
