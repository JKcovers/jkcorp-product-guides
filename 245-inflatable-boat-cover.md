# Inflatable Boat Cover — Protection for RIBs and Dinghies

Inflatable boats — from RIBs with rigid hulls to PVC and hypalon dinghies — have unique covering needs. The inflatable tubes are the most expensive part of the boat and the most vulnerable to UV, the very thing covers are meant to block. Here is the complete guide to inflatable boat covers.

## Why Inflatables Need Special Covers

- **Tube UV damage** — sunlight degrades PVC and hypalon fabrics, causing cracking and fading
- **Tube pressure** — a covered boat can heat up like a greenhouse; pressure must be managed
- **Valve protection** — valves are vulnerable to damage and debris
- **Lightweight hulls** — covers must fit snugly or they flap and abrade the tubes
- **Flat profile** — dinghies and RIBs sit low, inviting water pooling on the cover

## Cover Options by Boat Type

### RIBs (Rigid Inflatable Boats)

- **Full cover** — protects tubes, hull, and console; best for long-term storage
- **Tubes-only cover** — for boats used frequently, protecting just the vulnerable tubes
- **Custom fit recommended** — RIB tube shapes vary significantly

### Dinghies and Tendfers

- **Full cover with drawstring hem** — simple, secure on flat profiles
- **Universal dinghy covers** — often sufficient for symmetrical shapes
- **Storage bag style** — some inflatables store best deflated in a bag, not covered

## Material Considerations

- **UV-resistant polyester (420D-600D)** — the coating must be UV-stable
- **Light colors** — reflect heat; dark covers turn the tubes into an oven
- **Breathable options** — for boats stored inflated with full pressure
- **Avoid heavy rigid covers** — they don't conform to tube contours and flap

## Key Features

- **Elastic/drawstring hem** — conforms to tube shapes without straps that chafe
- **Vent panels** — manage heat and condensation
- **Padded valve covers** — protect valves from debris and pressure
- **Reinforced seam tape** — inflatable boats are stored outdoors in demanding conditions

## Fitting and Storage Tips

1. **Release pressure slightly** (if storing long-term) — covers fit better and tubes stress less
2. Fit the cover over the tubes, working evenly around the perimeter
3. Secure the drawstring or elastic hem — don't rely on straps across the tubes
4. Check that no straps or hem edges press hard against tube seams

### Deflated vs. Inflated Storage

| Approach | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|
| Inflated + cover | Short-term storage, frequent use |
| Deflated + bag | Long-term storage, space constraints |
| Inflated, no cover | Indoor storage only |

## Common Mistakes

1. Covering a hot boat — heat builds under the cover and stresses tubes
2. Tightening straps across tubes — creates pressure points and abrasion
3. Using dark covers in hot climates — accelerates heat buildup
4. Forgetting valve protection — debris enters and damages valves

## Maintenance

- Wash tubes and cover with inflatable-safe cleaners
- Re-apply UV protectant to tubes even under the cover
- Check the cover for chafe marks at tube contact points
- Store the cover dry and unfolded
