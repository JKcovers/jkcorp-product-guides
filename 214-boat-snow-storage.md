# Snow Storage for Boats — The Complete Guide

Winterizing a boat is a multi-step process, and covering it correctly is the step that protects your entire investment through months of snow, ice, and freezing temperatures. Proper boat snow storage prevents frozen bilge water, mildew in the cabin, and snow load damage to the cover and hull. Here is the complete guide.

## Why Snow Storage Is Different

Snow is heavy and persistent. A single snowfall can add 10-20 kg per square meter to a cover. Over a winter, the cover faces:

- Accumulated snow load pressing on the cover and hull
- Freeze-thaw cycles that refreeze water against the gel coat
- Ice expansion inside drains, vents, and seams
- Trapped moisture that never dries until spring

## The Pre-Storage Checklist

### 1. Winterize the Boat Systems

- Drain and fog the engine (inboard/outboard)
- Drain all water lines, pumps, and tanks
- Add antifreeze to the cooling system per manufacturer specs
- Remove batteries and store them indoors on a maintainer

### 2. Clean and Dry Everything

- Wash the hull and trailer — salt and dirt hold moisture
- Dry the interior, bilge, and cover storage areas completely
- Mildew starting in a damp corner will spread all winter

### 3. Inspect Before Covering

Check the cover for tears, worn seams, and missing straps. A small rip in fall becomes a shredded cover by spring. Repair or replace before the first snowfall.

## Choosing the Right Winter Cover

| Cover Type | Best For | Snow Load |
|------------|----------|-----------|
| Fitted canvas cover | Trailered boats, custom shapes | Good |
| Heavy-duty tarp + frame | Larger boats, outdoor storage | Best with poles |
| Shrink wrap | Winter storage yards | Excellent |
| Lightweight summer cover | Indoor storage only | Poor |

### Key Winter Cover Specs

- **Waterproof but breathable** — traps must be vented or the boat sweats
- **UV-resistant fabric** — winter sun at altitude is intense even in cold air
- **Reinforced seams** — seams are the first failure point under snow
- **Support poles** — prevent snow from pooling in the center; a flat pool of snow can collapse the cover and damage the boat

## Setting Up Support

Never let snow rest directly on a flat cover span:

1. Install a center pole or ridge pole that runs bow to stern
2. Add side poles on wide boats to keep the cover off the gunwales
3. Tension the cover so water and snow slide off rather than pool
4. Slope the cover toward the drains with pole positioning

## The Winter Maintenance Routine

- **After each snowfall** — brush snow off with a soft broom (never a shovel)
- **Every 2-3 weeks** — check strap tension and pole positions
- **After freeze-thaw cycles** — check that ice isn't pushing vents closed
- **Monthly** — look inside if possible for condensation or ice buildup

## Spring Removal

- Remove snow before removing the cover — dragging a cover loaded with snow across the gel coat scratches it
- Let the boat dry before folding the cover
- Store the cover clean and dry; a dirty damp cover will be unusable next winter
