# Pet Cover for Bench Seat Trucks

A front bench seat is the one layout where a pet cover cannot use the standard playbook. The usual installation assumes two removable headrests and a seat that stays put; a truck bench often offers neither.

Getting a cover to work here is a design problem, not a sizing problem.

## What a bench seat does differently

- **Three positions, two headrests.** Outboard headrests are usually adjustable or removable; the centre is often a low hump with nothing to anchor to.
- **A fold-down centre section.** Many modern benches have a centre backrest that folds forward into an armrest and storage bin. A one-piece cover bridges it and the fold fights the fabric.
- **Belts integrated into the bench.** The buckles sit low in the cushion, between positions, and a full-width skirt covers them.
- **A flat cushion with a flat floor.** No transmission tunnel to tuck side flaps into, and often a column shifter that brings the dog close to the driver's controls.
- **Frequent work use.** Bench-seat trucks are often work vehicles: tools, dust and boots share the cabin with the dog.

## Cover designs that fit a bench

**A full-width bench cover with a defined centre fold.** Works best when the fabric is pliable and the backing is flexible. The fold zone should carry no stiff panel and no seam that sits exactly on the hinge.

**A three-section cover.** Outboard sections plus a centre section that lifts with the armrest. More complex and more expensive, but the only clean solution for a bench whose centre section is used.

**A two-piece cover split at the centre.** A practical compromise: the driver's side and the passenger side are covered independently, leaving the centre free.

**A bench cover plus a centre console cover.** Where the centre section folds down, protecting the console separately avoids fighting the folding mechanism.

## Anchoring without three headrests

Anchor plates pushed into the seatback joint do most of the work on a bench, because the joint runs the full width and there is no need for a headrest post. Supplemental straps to the seat base or the frame gaps hold the front edge.

Side flaps are less useful here than on a car, because a bench cushion usually has no gap to tuck into on the outboard ends. Instead, the cover should be tensioned across the width so it cannot creep sideways.

## Work-truck considerations

The environment changes what to specify:

- **Abrasion resistance matters more than softness.** Boots and tools share the seat.
- **A hose-able surface beats a plush one.** Dust and mud are routine, and a smooth face fabric cleans far faster.
- **Buckles and straps must survive repeated fitting**, because the cover will be taken out whenever the truck carries a load in the cab.

## Safety points that are easy to miss

Do not cover the seatbelt buckles. On a bench they sit between positions, at cushion level, and a full-width drop skirt can hide them completely.

If the dog rides in the centre or passenger position, secure it with a harness rather than leaving it loose, and check that the harness routing does not cross the driver's reach to the column shifter or the parking brake.

## Buying notes

- measured width across the full bench, including the cushion edges
- a fold zone or split matched to the centre section
- anchor plates for the seatback joint, not headrest straps alone
- flexible backing so the bench can still fold
- unobstructed buckles
