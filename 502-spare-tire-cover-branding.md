# Spare Tire Cover Branding for Dealers

For a dealer or distributor, a branded spare tire cover is one of the few low-cost items that stays visible for years. It sits on the back of a customer's vehicle, in traffic, in car parks and on driveways. Very few promotional products get that much sustained outdoor exposure from a single unit.

This guide covers how to run a branded cover programme that actually reflects well on the brand.

## Why the format works

The cover is a large, flat, high-visibility panel. It is fitted to a vehicle that is by definition outside, and it remains in place rather than being discarded after a week. For automotive businesses — dealers, accessory retailers, workshops, rental and tour operators — the audience match is direct.

The main risk is the opposite of the main benefit: because it stays visible, a poorly made cover is also long-lasting negative advertising. Fabric that fades unevenly, print that cracks, or a hem that fails at speed all reflect on the brand every day.

## Getting the specification right first

Before artwork, settle four points:

| Point | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Size band coverage | A single size will not fit every vehicle in a mixed fleet |
| Fabric and backing | Heavier fabric holds shape; a soft backing protects customer wheels |
| Retention method | Determines fit security across different carrier types |
| Print durability | UV and wash resistance, not just initial colour |

A branded programme that admits it needs two or three size bands will outperform one that pretends a single universal size fits everything — returns and complaints cost more than the extra SKU.

## Artwork guidelines

- Keep the design clear of the elastic hem; artwork near the edge distorts when the cover is stretched over the tire.
- Design for a curved surface. A layout that reads well flat can wrap awkwardly around a tire.
- Favour simple, high-contrast marks. Fine detail and small text are lost at typical viewing distance.
- Decide whether the background is printed or left as base fabric colour; this affects both appearance and cost.

Approve a physical sample mounted on a tire. A digital proof will not show how the design reads once the material is under tension.

## Where branded covers fit in a dealer programme

Three common uses:

1. **Point-of-sale giveaway** with a vehicle purchase or service.
2. **Accessory upsell** as part of a protection package alongside floor mats and boot liners.
3. **Fleet identification** for rental, tour and commercial operators, where covers mark vehicles in a uniform way.

## Running repeat orders

Branded covers generally carry higher minimums than plain stock because of setup and material runs. Ask the supplier to hold the approved artwork and production files so repeat orders can be placed without re-approval, and confirm that a repeat run will use the same fabric and print specification. Changing either silently is a common cause of mismatch.

## Measuring the investment

The relevant comparison is not cover cost against a leaflet — it is cover cost against other long-lived branded items on the vehicle. Judged that way, the format is efficient, provided the product is specified well enough to stay in service.
