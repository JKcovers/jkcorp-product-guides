# Cover Installation Mistakes — Top 10 That Damage Cars

Installing a car cover seems foolproof: drape, close, done. In practice, the installation step is where most cover-related damage happens. These 10 cover installation mistakes are the most common — and each one is easy to avoid.

## Mistake 1: Covering a Dirty or Wet Car

The #1 mistake. Dirt and grit under the cover act like sandpaper on paint; trapped water feeds mildew and rust. Even a light dusting grinds into the clear coat as the cover moves in wind.

**Fix:** wash or wipe the car down before covering — always dry before draping.

## Mistake 2: Not Starting at the Front

Draping a cover randomly leaves it misaligned and stretched. The cover never settles into its proper position and strains the seams.

**Fix:** unfold the cover, align the front (marked on most covers) over the hood, then walk the cover back over the roof and trunk.

## Mistake 3: Skipping Mirror Pockets

Mirrors catch the fabric and tear seams. Buyers with mirror-pocket covers often skip lining them up properly.

**Fix:** ease each mirror into its pocket before tightening the rest of the cover.

## Mistake 4: Forgetting Antennas and Accessories

An antenna pokes a hole in the cover; a roof rack tears the fabric. Both also prevent the cover from sitting flat.

**Fix:** remove or lower antennas where possible, and pad sharp accessories before covering.

## Mistake 5: Leaving the Straps Loose

A loose cover billows in wind, rubs the paint, and can lift off. Under-tension is as bad as over-tension.

**Fix:** secure the straps or elastic under the bumpers and wheels — snug, not stretched to the limit.

## Mistake 6: Ignoring Wind Direction

A cover installed facing the wind lifts at the front and floods. Orientation matters for how the cover sheds wind and water.

**Fix:** install the cover so the front (usually marked) faces the vehicle's front, and anchor the windward side first.

## Mistake 7: Covering a Hot Engine or Exhaust

Heat melts fabric and traps moisture. Covering right after a drive is a common, costly error.

**Fix:** let the engine, exhaust, and body cool completely — 30 minutes minimum after a drive.

## Mistake 8: Forcing a Too-Small Cover

Stretching a tight cover strains seams, pops straps, and leaves corners exposed. If the cover fights, it is the wrong size.

**Fix:** stop, re-check the size chart, and size up.

## Mistake 9: Not Adjusting After Wind

A cover that fit this morning can loosen in the afternoon breeze. Leaving it unadjusted lets it flap all night.

**Fix:** re-tension straps and re-center the cover after any windy period.

## Mistake 10: Ignoring Water Pooling

A flat cover traps rain on the roof, which pools, stretches fabric, and eventually leaks through.

**Fix:** install support poles or ensure the cover's peak shape sheds water, and check after heavy rain.

## The Golden Rule

**Clean, dry, cool, aligned, and anchored — in that order.** Five minutes of correct installation adds years to both the cover and the paint.
