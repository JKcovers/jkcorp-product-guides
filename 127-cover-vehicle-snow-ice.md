# Vehicle Covers for Snow and Ice — Full-Vehicle Winter Protection

When your car sits outside in snow country, a full-vehicle cover is the ultimate winter defense. Here's what it protects and how to choose the right one.

## The Winter Assault on Vehicles

- **Snow load:** 12" of wet snow weighs 200+ lbs on a roof
- **Freeze-thaw:** Water in micro-cracks expands, widening gaps
- **Road salt:** Corrodes underbody, brakes, and fasteners
- **Ice scrapers:** Micro-scratches in glass and paint
- **Cold-weather rubber:** Seals and gaskets stiffen and crack

## What a Winter Cover Protects

| Element | Protection |
|---------|-----------|
| Paint | Snow-free, no scrape damage |
| Glass | No frost/scrape micro-scratches |
| Seals | Ice can't form directly on rubber |
| Snow load | Cover distributes and sheds snow |
| Salt mist | Barrier when parked near roads |

## Winter Cover Specs

- **600D Oxford or heavier:** Withstands snow load
- **5000mm waterproof:** Melting snow must not penetrate
- **Fleece inner lining:** Cold paint is brittle; fleece prevents scratches
- **Full strap system:** Winter winds demand security
- **Dark color:** Absorbs weak winter sun, helps snow melt
- **Ventilation:** Even in winter, condensation needs an escape

## Usage Routine

1. Wash the car before extended covering (salt under a cover corrodes)
2. Brush snow off with a soft broom after storms
3. Let the cover dry weekly (sunny days)
4. Wash the cover monthly to remove salt residue

## The Trade-off

Full winter covers add 5-10 minutes per use. For daily drivers, many owners use covers only during long-term parking. For vehicles parked for weeks, the cover is essential.
