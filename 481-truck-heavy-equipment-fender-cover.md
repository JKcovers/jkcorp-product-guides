# Fender Cover for Trucks and Heavy Equipment

Working on a truck, bus or piece of heavy equipment raises the protection problem to a different scale. The panels are larger, the working height is greater, and the technician is leaning further over a body panel that costs considerably more to repair than a passenger car wing. The cover has to be sized, weighted and retained accordingly.

## Why the specification changes

**Panel size.** A commercial vehicle wing is substantially larger than a car wing, so the cover needs more area and more mass. A light cover on a large panel lifts at the edges and will not lie flat.

**Working height and load.** On a truck or machine, a technician may be leaning at chest height or above, putting more weight and more lateral force on the mat. Retention and the anti-slip property matter more, not less.

**Access and anchors.** Heavy equipment often has fewer convenient anchor points for hooks and straps, and body panels made of materials that magnets may not grip. Combined retention is frequently the only reliable answer.

**Environment.** Agricultural and construction equipment is often worked on outdoors or in yards, where wind is a factor. A cover that lifts is a cover that stops protecting.

## Sizing for commercial vehicles

| Vehicle type | Practical approach |
|---|---|
| Light commercial van | Standard or large fender cover |
| Rigid truck | Large fender cover; consider a wing and grille panel |
| Bus and coach | Vehicle-specific panel, given wing length |
| Agricultural tractor | Vehicle-specific or oversize; retention is the priority |
| Construction and plant | Oversize mat with combined retention |

Because commercial vehicles are often worked on in fleets, uniformity has value. A single size that fits the fleet's dominant model — rather than a perfectly tailored cover per vehicle — is often the more practical specification for a fleet manager.

## Retention on large panels

On a large, tall panel, a single retention method is usually insufficient. Magnets alone may not hold across the full area in wind; hooks alone allow the centre of the panel to balloon out.

The workable construction combines encapsulated magnets along the leading edges with hooks or straps at the corners, plus a weighted or stiffened lower edge to stop the panel lifting at the base. Reinforcement at every attachment point is essential, because that is where a large panel fails first.

## Materials for the environment

Fluid resistance matters more in this segment than in general automotive work. Lubricants, coolant, hydraulic fluid and, on agricultural equipment, fuel and crop residues all come into contact with the cover. A wipe-clean vinyl-faced construction is the practical default, with a soft backing to protect the paint.

Padding is also worth specifying: dropped tools on a commercial vehicle are heavier, and the panel beneath is large enough that a mark is highly visible.

## Checks before ordering

Confirm coverage on the largest vehicle in the intended fleet. Test retention with someone leaning on the mat at working height, not simply laid flat. Verify clearance from hot components on a running engine. And confirm the cover can be positioned and removed by one person working alone, since that is how it will actually be used.
