# Stadium Cushion Fabric and Foam Guide

Two materials decide how a stadium cushion performs: the foam that carries the load and the fabric that meets the weather. Both are usually specified together and cheapened together, which is why so many budget cushions fail as a pair rather than as individual parts.

## Foam families and what each is for

Foam is not one material, and the names overlap in the trade.

**Open-cell polyurethane** is the classic comfort foam. Air passes through the cell structure, so it feels soft and breathes, and it can be moulded into contours. Its weakness is water: open cells take in moisture and hold it.

**Closed-cell polyethylene and EVA** have sealed cells. They do not absorb water, they hold shape under repeated loading, and they insulate a sitter from a cold surface. They are firmer per unit of thickness, which is why they suit thin bleacher pads.

**Memory foam** is a high-density open-cell variant that responds to body heat. It delivers a premium feel at a premium cost, and it carries the same water limitation as other open-cell foams.

The practical rule: if the cushion will be used outdoors and stored without drying, specify closed-cell.

## Firmness, thickness and load

Two cushions of the same thickness can behave completely differently. What matters is how the foam behaves under a seated adult over a whole game, not how it feels when pressed by hand in a showroom.

- Thin and firm resists bottoming out on a hard bench.
- Thick and soft feels better initially but compresses to the hard surface underneath.
- Moulded contours distribute load better than a flat slab of the same foam.

Ask how the foam was specified — by type and thickness, or by a measured property under load. The second answer is the one that travels between suppliers.

## Fabric and water resistance

Outdoor cushion fabric is normally a synthetic with a coated or laminated backing:

- **Coated fabric** — a polyurethane or PVC coating applied to the reverse. Light, flexible, and the common choice for folding cushions.
- **Laminated backing** — a bonded film or fleece layer. Softer against plastic seats but heavier.
- **Higher-denier polyester** holds shape and resists abrasion better than light fabric, at a small weight penalty.

Be precise about the claim being made. Water-resistant fabric resists brief rain and sheds splashes. Waterproof describes a material that does not let water through under the conditions tested. The two are not interchangeable on a product page, and the base of a cushion matters as much as the top.

## Seams, zippers and stitching

Water gets in at seams before it gets through fabric. Stitched seams create holes, so a cushion that claims full protection needs taped or sealed seams, or a construction with no seam on the base at all.

Zippers are convenient for cover removal and cleaning, but an uncovered zipper is a path for water. Where a removable cover is required, specify a flap over the zip.

## Print surface

Fabric weight and weave determine how print looks. Light fabric with a tight weave takes fine detail; heavy textured fabric takes solid colour blocks better than fine lines. Confirm the print method and colour-fastness expectation against the actual fabric, not against a paper proof.
