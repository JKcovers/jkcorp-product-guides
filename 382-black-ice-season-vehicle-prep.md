# Black Ice Season Vehicle Prep

Black ice is a road hazard rather than a paint hazard, and that distinction matters when preparing a vehicle for the season. Frost protection covers the surfaces at rest. Black ice preparation covers everything that decides whether the car can be controlled, seen out of and stopped when the surface turns to glass.

The two overlap in exactly one place: the windscreen and windows. Everything else is a different checklist.

## What black ice actually is

Black ice is a thin, transparent layer of ice on the road, usually forming where moisture refreezes — on bridges, in shaded bends, at the mouth of tunnels and anywhere water pools and then loses heat to a clear sky. It is transparent because it is thin, so the road surface shows through and the hazard is invisible.

The conditions that produce it are the same ones that produce windscreen frost: a clear, calm night with surface temperatures at or below freezing. If the car is frosted in the morning, the road may be too.

## Visibility: the windscreen and windows

This is where a protective cover earns a place in the black ice routine.

A frozen windscreen is not just inconvenient. Clearing it by scraping takes time, and drivers rushing in cold conditions cut corners — leaving a patch of ice at eye level, or starting the wipers while still frozen to the glass and damaging the blades and the wiper motor.

- A fitted windscreen cover overnight means the glass is clear on the frostiest morning, with no scraping at all.
- Side windows and mirrors need clearing too. A cover that extends over the side glass saves the same job twice.
- Check the washer jets and fluid. Winter-grade washer fluid matters more in this season than at any other time, and a frozen jet leaves a screen that cannot be cleaned while driving.
- Replace wiper blades before the season starts. A blade that chatters on a cold screen has already failed.

## Traction and control

- **Tyres.** The tread depth and compound are the decisive factor on ice. Check tread depth before the season, and consider winter or all-season tyres in regions where black ice is routine.
- **Brakes and ABS.** Have the system checked before the season. Braking on ice is a system problem, not a driver problem.
- **Battery.** Cold reduces battery capacity, and a marginal battery fails on the first hard frost. Test it before the season rather than during it.

## The in-car kit

The kit that matters for this season is small and specific:

- an ice scraper and a soft brush, kept in the cabin rather than the boot
- de-icer for the door seals and lock, not just the glass
- a warning triangle and a high-visibility vest
- a torch, gloves and a blanket
- a phone charger that works without the engine running

## What protects the car at rest

- A windscreen or full cover overnight prevents the morning ice that so often leads to rushed and incomplete clearing.
- Silicone treatment on door seals prevents the doors freezing shut.
- Keeping the fuel tank above half avoids condensation in the tank through repeated cold cycles.

## The real point of the season

Black ice season is won in advance. Nothing on the road can be controlled, but visibility, tread, battery condition and a clear windscreen can all be settled before the first frost — and those are the variables that decide whether the hazard is a near miss or a collision.
