# Winter RV Storage Guide — Protecting Your Home on Wheels

An RV is the most expensive vehicle most owners will ever store outdoors. Winter is when it takes the worst beating: snow load, ice, UV even in cold air, and temperature swings that stress every seal. This winter RV storage guide covers prep, covering, and mid-winter care so your RV is ready when spring arrives.

## The Winter Threats to an RV

- **Snow load** — a wet snowfall can exceed the roof's design load if it pools
- **Ice expansion** — water freezing in seams, vents, and compartments cracks seals
- **UV damage** — winter sun at low angles still degrades fiberglass and decals
- **Freeze-thaw cycling** — daily expansion and contraction stresses caulk and seals
- **Rodents** — an RV stored outdoors is a tempting winter shelter

## Pre-Winter Preparation

### 1. Winterize the Plumbing

- Drain all fresh and gray water tanks completely
- Blow out water lines with compressed air
- Add RV antifreeze to traps and pumps per the owner's manual
- Remove and store inline water filters — they freeze and crack

### 2. Protect the Interior

- Remove batteries; store indoors on a maintainer
- Clean the interior thoroughly — food smells attract rodents
- Open cabinet doors to let air circulate behind them
- Use moisture absorbers in the cabin; RVs collect surprising amounts of winter condensation

### 3. Exterior Prep

- Wash and wax the exterior before storage
- Lubricate all hinges, locks, and stabilizer jacks
- Check the roof seals and caulk — fix any cracks now, not in spring
- Remove anything that can freeze or attract pests (propane tanks, food, awnings retracted)

## Choosing the RV Cover

| Cover Type | Protection | Best For |
|------------|------------|----------|
| Custom-fit RV cover | Excellent | Long-term outdoor storage |
| Universal RV cover | Good | Budget, short seasons |
| Tarp + straps | Basic | Temporary protection only |

### Key Specs for Winter

- **420D-600D heavy oxford or marine-grade fabric** — must hold up to wind and UV
- **Waterproof with breathability** — vents prevent the greenhouse effect
- **Reinforced grommets and straps** — snow load and wind pull hard
- **Zippered access panels** — let you enter the RV without removing the cover
- **Soft padding at contact points** — prevent cover wear against the roof edges

### Support the Roof

A cover alone cannot carry deep snow. Install roof supports (inflatable or rigid poles) to create a peak that sheds snow and prevents pooling. This is the most common mistake in RV winter storage — snow pools in the center, the cover sags, and weight transfers to the roof.

## The Mid-Winter Routine

- **After each heavy snowfall** — brush snow off the roof with a soft broom
- **Monthly** — check straps and grommets; re-tension as the cover settles
- **Monthly** — check the interior for condensation or rodent activity (use the access zipper)
- **After wind storms** — inspect the cover for tears and flapping damage

## Spring Removal

- Remove snow before uncovering — dragging snow across the fiberglass scratches it
- Uncover on a dry day, and let the RV air out completely
- Inspect the cover for mildew or tears; clean and dry it before storing
- Check all seals for damage from winter movement
