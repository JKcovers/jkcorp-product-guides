# Custom Printed Stadium Cushions for Teams

Stadium cushions have become a standard item in team merchandise, and the reason is structural rather than sentimental: the cushion is a large, flat, high-visibility panel that a supporter carries in public for hours at a time. Few low-cost promotional products get that much exposure.

For a team, school or sponsor programme, the value comes from three things working together — artwork, construction and quantity.

## Artwork placement

The printable area is not the whole cushion. Printing runs to the edges of the panel, but the edges are also where the cushion curves around a seat and where seams take up fabric. Artwork placed hard against a seam distorts when the cushion is used, and text close to an edge can disappear into the fold.

Set the artwork to the flat panel area and leave a margin. Logos and crests usually sit centre-panel; sponsor marks and calls to action work better along the base or the side, where they read when the cushion is placed on a bench.

## Print methods

Three methods cover most programmes:

- **Screen printing** suits solid colours and larger runs. It is durable and cost-effective at volume but adds a setup cost per colour, which is why it penalises complex artwork.
- **Sublimation** prints into synthetic fabric and handles photographs and gradients. It needs a light-coloured synthetic base and does not work on every fabric.
- **Transfer printing** handles full-colour artwork with less setup than screen printing and is a common middle path for smaller runs.

The choice is driven by artwork complexity and run size, not by preference. A four-colour crest is a different decision from a photographic image.

## Colour matching

Team colours are usually specified by a code, and matching them across a printed panel, a binding and a stitched label is where programmes go wrong. Confirm which parts of the product carry colour, and ask for a physical sample rather than a digital proof — screens and print processes render the same code differently.

## Personalisation and numbering

Individual sales benefit from personalisation: a name, a squad number or a seat block printed on the panel. This moves the product from merchandise to memorabilia and supports a higher price point, but it also changes the production model from one print run to many unique items. Confirm how the supplier handles variable data and what minimum applies per unique item.

## Minimums and sample approval

Printing adds setup and material waste, so minimum order quantities for printed cushions are higher than for plain ones and are driven by the print method as much as by the fabric. Ask for the minimum per design and per colour, not just a headline figure.

Always approve a physical pre-production sample. Check artwork position against the finished cushion, colour against the specified code, and how the print behaves when the cushion is compressed, because a panel that is fine flat can crack where it folds.
