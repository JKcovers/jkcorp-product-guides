# Pollen Season Car Cover

Pollen is not ordinary dust. The grains are small, sticky and mildly acidic, and they bond to paint rather than sitting on top of it. That is why the standard advice to wipe a pollen-covered car with a dry cloth makes things worse: it drags abrasive grit across the clearcoat.

A cover is the simplest way to keep pollen off the paint in the first place. What matters is choosing one that does not become a pollen trap itself.

## Why pollen is harder on paint than dust

Three properties separate pollen from road dust:

- **Adhesion.** Pollen grains are built to stick to surfaces. They resist being blown off, so a parked car builds a visible film within hours at peak season.
- **Acidity.** Pollen deposits are mildly acidic. Left on paint through repeated dew cycles, they etch the surface the same way other organic deposits do.
- **Moisture activation.** Once damp, pollen becomes a paste that clings harder and holds moisture against the panel.

The practical consequence: pollen has to be removed by rinsing, not wiping. Rinsing works far better if the pollen never reached the paint.

## What makes a cover suitable for pollen season

Pollen is seasonal, so the cover only has to do this one job well for a few weeks.

**A smooth, tightly woven outer surface** sheds pollen instead of gripping it. Textured fabric and fleece-lined covers hold pollen in the nap, which then transfers onto the car when the cover is fitted.

**Fine-particle blocking.** The relevant measure is how small a particle the fabric stops, not how heavy it is. A light fabric with a tight weave outperforms a heavy open weave against pollen.

**Correct fit.** A loose cover flaps and pumps pollen-laden air underneath. During pollen season the fit is the specification that matters most.

**Colour.** Light covers show pollen less and heat less; a dark cover in strong spring sun bakes deposits onto its own surface.

## The pollen-season routine

1. **Rinse before fitting.** Fitting a cover over a pollen-covered car presses the pollen into the paint. Rinse the car first, or fit the cover before the season starts.
2. **Fit before the peak.** Pollen counts rise in the morning and fall in the evening. Fitting in the evening reduces what lands during the process.
3. **Rinse the cover weekly.** A pollen-loaded cover transfers its load to the car the next time it is handled. Hose it down, away from the car, and let it dry.
4. **Never wipe dry.** Both the car and the cover should be rinsed, not brushed.
5. **Store clean.** Pollen left in folded fabric encourages staining and odour over the summer.

## What not to expect

A cover does not stop pollen entering the cabin through the ventilation system, and it does not help a car parked indoors with open windows. Those are cabin air and filtration problems, not paint protection problems.

It also does not remove existing pollen etching. Once the clearcoat has been marked, the fix is polishing, not covering.

## Buying for a pollen season

- tight weave over heavy weight
- accurate fit for the specific vehicle
- light colour for spring sun
- a material that can be rinsed and air-dried repeatedly
