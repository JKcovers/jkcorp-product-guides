# Car Cover Weight Guide — GSM, Denier, and What They Mean for Durability

Car cover weight directly affects durability, ease of use, and protection level. Understanding GSM and Denier helps you choose the right weight for your situation.

## GSM (Grams per Square Meter): The Total Package

GSM measures the total fabric weight including coatings. A bare 420D Oxford might be 160 GSM. The same fabric with a heavy PU waterproof coating could be 200 GSM. Higher GSM generally means more coating and better waterproofing.

## Denier (D): The Fiber Thickness

Denier measures individual fiber thickness. 210D is lightweight, 420D is mid-weight, 600D is heavy-duty. Denier only describes the base fabric — it doesn't account for coatings.

## Real-World Weight Comparison

| Fabric | Denier | Typical GSM | Best Use |
|--------|--------|:--:|------|
| Light Oxford | 210D | 130-160 | Indoor, light outdoor |
| Standard Oxford | 420D | 160-200 | Year-round outdoor |
| Heavy Oxford | 600D | 200-250 | Extreme weather |
| PEVA | N/A | 80-100 | Budget, temporary |
| Multi-layer composite | 420D+ | 250-350 | Premium outdoor |

## Weight vs Usability

A 600D cover for an SUV weighs 8-12 lbs — fine for set-and-forget storage. A 210D cover for a sedan weighs 3-5 lbs — manageable for daily on/off. Choose weight that matches your usage frequency.
