# Trailer Cover Guide — Sizing, Materials, and Protection

Utility trailers, enclosed trailers, and car haulers all sit outside between jobs — and all of them rust, fade, and leak faster than they should. A trailer cover is the cheapest maintenance you can buy. This guide covers sizing, materials, and installation.

## Why a Trailer Needs a Cover

- **Metal bodies** — rain and road salt attack bare steel and aluminum
- **Wood floors** — moisture wicks up through trailer floors in damp storage
- **Tires and wheels** — UV cracks sidewalls even on parked trailers
- **Seals and lights** — water intrusion ruins wiring and marker lights

## Sizing Your Trailer Cover

Measure the trailer with the tongue included:

1. **Overall length** from the coupler to the rear frame
2. **Width** at the widest point (usually over the fenders)
3. **Height** from the ground to the tallest accessory — spare tire mount, roof vent, or cargo tie-downs

Choose the size that covers all three. Trailers with spare-tire carriers or jacks on the tongue need covers with cutouts or extra length to clear them.

## Material Choices

| Material | Best For | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy-duty polyester, waterproof coated | Outdoor storage | Heavier to handle |
| Breathable multi-layer fabric | Humid climates | Less waterproofing |
| Lightweight tarp-style | Temporary shelter | Shorter lifespan |

## Installation Steps

1. Clean and dry the trailer before covering.
2. Drape the cover from the front (tongue side) toward the rear.
3. Seat the cover over the tallest accessory first.
4. Buckle the under-trailer straps front, center, and rear.
5. Re-tighten straps after the first windy day.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Covering a dirty or wet trailer.** Trapped grit scratches paint; trapped moisture corrodes metal and breeds mildew on the cover itself. Wash and dry the trailer before covering.

**Skipping the under-straps.** A cover that is not strapped under the trailer becomes a sail. Use all anchor points, front to rear, and re-tighten after windy weather.

**Forgetting the spare tire and jacks.** Spare-tire carriers and tongue jacks punch holes in covers within days. Choose a cover with clearance or add padding over these points.

**Leaving the cover on through deep snow.** Heavy snow collapses the fabric onto the trailer and strains the straps. Brush snow off promptly or switch to a framed shelter for winter.

## The Golden Rule

**Cover the tallest point, and strap every anchor.** A cover that flogs in the wind abrades the paint it is supposed to protect.
