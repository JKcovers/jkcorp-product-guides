# Windshield Frost Protection

Frost on the windscreen is not a snowfall problem. It forms on clear, calm nights when the glass radiates heat away and drops below the dew point of the air around it — which is why a car can be frosted on a morning when nothing else is. The glass is the coldest exposed surface on the vehicle, so it frosts first and hardest — and that is why the method matters more than the product.

## Why the windscreen frosts first

Metal and glass lose heat to the sky at different rates, and the windscreen faces upward with the least insulation behind it. On a clear night the surface can fall below freezing while the air temperature is still above it. Moisture in the air then deposits directly onto the glass as ice.

Two consequences follow:

- **Overcast nights are usually frost-free.** Clouds reflect heat back and slow the cooling.
- **Wind prevents frost.** Moving air keeps the surface closer to air temperature.

So frost is a clear, calm-night event — which makes it predictable, and therefore preventable.

## The four methods, and what each is good for

**A windscreen cover.** A fitted panel over the glass stops the surface from cooling below the dew point. It is the only method that prevents frost rather than removing it, and it is the one that keeps the glass clear of ice, leaves and blown debris at the same time.

**Scraping.** Effective and immediate, but it removes frost that has already formed, and the plastic blade wears against the glass if grit is trapped under it.

**De-icer spray.** Fast for a light frost. It works by lowering the freezing point of the film on the glass, so it needs to be applied before the ice hardens, and it does not help if the wipers are frozen to the screen.

**Warm water.** The most damaging option. It clears the glass quickly and then refreezes in the door seals, the wiper mechanism and the scuttle drains, where it causes the problems that appear weeks later.

A cover and a scraper are complementary: the cover for forecast frost nights, the scraper as the fallback.

## What to look for in a windscreen cover

- **Fitting method.** The cover must stay on overnight in wind. Magnetic edges, door-shut flaps, mirror anchors and wiper tucks are the common systems, and the right one depends on the vehicle.
- **Waterproof outer, soft inner.** The outer surface has to keep precipitation out; the inner surface has to be soft enough not to mark the glass or the paint at the edges.
- **Edge coverage.** Shrinking coverage to the glass alone leaves the wiper blades and the base of the screen exposed, and those are what seize.
- **Size.** Covers sized for a generic car leave gaps at the corners, which is where frost creeps back in.

## A practical frost-night routine

1. Check the forecast for a clear, calm night below the dew point.
2. Fit the cover with the wipers parked, and tuck the edges into the door apertures so the cover cannot lift.
3. In the morning, remove the cover before starting the engine. Do not switch on the wipers first.
4. Shake or rinse the cover away from the car, and let it dry before folding.

## The cost argument

Replacing a windscreen costs far more than a cover, and wear from daily scraping accumulates over seasons. It is one of the few accessory categories where the economics are decided by avoided cost rather than convenience.
