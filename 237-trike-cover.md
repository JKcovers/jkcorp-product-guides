# Trike Cover Guide — Fitting a Motorcycle Cover on Three Wheels

Trikes are the awkward size in the motorcycle cover world. They have motorcycle dimensions up front and car-like width at the rear — so a standard motorcycle cover is too short, and a car cover is too baggy. A trike cover is built for that specific shape: long enough for the frame, wide enough for the rear, and cut to fit mirrors and windscreens.

## Why Trikes Need a Special Fit

- The rear track is 40-60 cm wider than the front — standard covers strain at the rear
- Trike bodies (fiberglass or metal) scratch easily — lining quality matters
- Long wheelbases mean standard bike covers do not reach the tail
- Windscreens and mirrors sit high — pockets and cutouts are essential
- Converted trikes have unique shapes — measuring beats guessing

## Key Fit Dimensions

| Dimension | How to Measure |
|-----------|----------------|
| Length | Tip of the front fender to the rearmost point |
| Width | Across the widest rear body section |
| Height | Ground to the top of the windscreen |
| Mirror spread | Outside edge to outside edge of mirrors |

## What to Look For

1. **Extended rear panels** — the cover must drop over the rear body, not stretch across it
2. **Mirror pockets** — without them, the cover tears at the widest point
3. **Windscreen cutout or tall pocket** — full coverage without fabric strain
4. **Soft fleece lining** — trike bodies scratch easily; lining is non-negotiable
5. **Hem straps front and rear** — trikes catch wind like small cars; straps are essential
6. **Breathable panels** — condensation collects on wide flat bodywork

## Measuring Rules

- Measure the longest, widest, and tallest points — never the wheelbase alone
- Add 15-20 cm to the length for a relaxed fit that does not strain seams
- Note the mirror width separately — this determines whether standard sizes work
- Measure with accessories fitted (windshield, rear luggage) if they stay on the trike

## Storage Considerations

A trike is a tall, wide object that stores poorly outside. The cover protects fiberglass bodies from UV fading and cracking, and keeps dew and dust off the seat and grips. For long storage, use a cover with UV-stabilized fabric and a breathable construction — trike bodywork does not benefit from trapped humidity.

## Common Problems

- **Buying a car cover instead** — baggy fabric flaps and scratches the body
- **Buying a motorcycle cover** — too short and too narrow; the rear hangs exposed
- **Skipping the mirror pockets** — the first tear appears at the mirrors within weeks
- **Tight straps over bodywork** — webbing edges can mark fiberglass; use strap protectors

## Seasonal Use

In summer, a lighter trike cover sheds dust and bird droppings. In winter, a heavier waterproof cover with a lining protects the body and keeps snow off the seat. One good all-season trike cover usually outperforms two mediocre general-purpose ones.

## The Bottom Line

Trike owners should buy a cover measured for three wheels, with mirror pockets and rear coverage. A correct fit protects thousands in bodywork — a wrong fit damages it.
