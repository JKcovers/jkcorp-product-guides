# UV Protection Car Covers — Why 99% UV Rejection Matters More Than You Think

Sunlight is the silent killer of car exteriors. Over time, UV radiation breaks down clear coats, fades paint, cracks dashboards, and deteriorates interior fabrics. A car parked outdoors without protection can lose 10-20% of its resale value within five years due to sun damage alone. A UV-rated car cover is the simplest and most effective defense — but not all UV claims are equal.

## "UV Resistant" vs "UV Rejection" — The Critical Difference

This is the most important distinction when shopping for car covers:

- **UV Resistant**: The fabric has some level of UV-blocking ability, but no specific percentage is guaranteed. This is a marketing term, not a specification.

- **UV Rejection**: The fabric has been tested and certified to block a specific percentage of UV radiation. A 99% UV rejection rating means only 1% of UV rays pass through the cover.

Always choose a cover with a stated UV rejection percentage. "UV resistant" without a number means the manufacturer isn't willing to put a spec behind their claim.

## How UV Protection Works in Car Covers

UV protection in car covers comes from three sources:

1. **Fabric density**: Thicker fabrics (higher denier Oxford, multi-layer construction) naturally block more UV because there's more material between the sun and your paint.

2. **UV inhibitors**: Chemical additives mixed into the fabric during manufacturing that absorb or reflect UV radiation. These are what give a cover its rated UV rejection percentage.

3. **Reflective coatings**: Aluminum or titanium dioxide coatings on the outer layer reflect sunlight away, reducing both UV exposure and heat buildup. Some reflective covers can reduce interior temperatures by 15-20°F compared to no cover.

## How to Verify UV Claims

Ask the manufacturer or supplier these questions:

1. What is the tested UV rejection percentage?
2. What testing standard was used? (ASTM, ISO, etc.)
3. How long does the UV treatment last before reapplication is needed?
4. Does the UV protection come from fabric density, chemical treatment, or reflective coating?

A reputable manufacturer will have answers to all four. If they can't answer, the UV claim is probably marketing.
