# Cover Distributor Business Model: How Distributors Make Money

The cover distributor business model is straightforward on paper (buy bulk, sell to dealers and retailers, earn the spread) but profitable in practice only with disciplined SKU selection, inventory planning, and supplier terms. This guide breaks down how cover distributors actually build margins.

## The Basic Model

- **Buy:** containers of car, motorcycle, and boat covers FOB from factories
- **Sell:** to retailers, e-commerce sellers, dealers, and fleets at wholesale markup
- **Margin:** typically 30-60% gross, with net profit depending on turnover and cost control

## Revenue Streams

| Stream | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Wholesale distribution | Core: sell to retail and dealer networks |
| Private label | Your brand on covers, higher margin |
| Value-added services | Sizing support, install guides, warranties |
| Seasonal programs | Pre-season promotions to smooth cash flow |
| Accessories bundling | Covers with tie-downs, storage bags, care kits |

## Cost Structure to Watch

- **Inventory holding cost:** covers are bulky; warehouse space and storage fees eat margins
- **Return handling:** fit and fabric issues are the top return reasons
- **Freight:** FOB + sea freight + local logistics
- **Working capital:** 60-90 days of cash tied up between order and sell-through

## SKU Strategy That Works

- 60% volume SKUs (standard car sizes, all-weather fabric)
- 25% niche SKUs (trucks, EVs, motorcycles, boats)
- 15% seasonal (snow, hail, heat-specific)
- Never stock a SKU without a container-cost and sell-through model

## Supplier Terms to Negotiate

- MOQ flexibility: 100-300 pcs per SKU to test demand
- Mixed-container loading: car + motorcycle + boat covers in one shipment
- Sample support: 2-3 rounds of fit samples before bulk
- QC terms: mid-production and pre-shipment inspection included
- Lead time: 45-60 days with seasonal buffer

## Seasonal Rhythm

| Period | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Q1 | Order planning, sample approvals |
| Q2 | Bulk orders for winter market |
| Q3 | Shipment arrival, pre-season sell-in |
| Q4 | Peak sell-through, reorders |

## First-Order Checklist

- Model container economics before committing to SKUs
- Start with 3-5 high-velocity sizes, not a full catalog
- Negotiate mixed-container loading to test niche SKUs cheaply
- Agree on inspection milestones in the contract, not after production
- Price a return cushion of 2-3% into your first-season margin
- Set reorder triggers by sell-through rate, not calendar date, to avoid stockouts
- Track margin per SKU monthly and drop the bottom 10% without sentiment
- Build one flagship private label line to test brand margin before scaling
- Agree return terms with suppliers before the first season, not after
