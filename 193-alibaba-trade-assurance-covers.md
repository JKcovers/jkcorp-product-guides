# Alibaba Trade Assurance for Cover Buyers — How It Protects You

Buying protective covers from an overseas factory can feel risky — until you know how Alibaba Trade Assurance works. Trade Assurance is the platform's buyer-protection program for orders placed on Alibaba.com. Here is what it does, what it does not do, and how to use it when sourcing covers.

## What Trade Assurance Covers

When a supplier accepts an order with Trade Assurance, they commit to:

- **Product quality** — goods match the agreed specifications
- **Shipping on time** — the cargo departs by the confirmed date
- **Conformity to the contract** — quantity, packaging, and labeling as agreed

If the supplier breaches these terms, the buyer can file a claim and Alibaba steps in to mediate and, where valid, refund.

## How It Works Step by Step

1. Negotiate the order details — price, MOQ, size chart, fabric, logo, packaging
2. Confirm the order with Trade Assurance enabled
3. Pay via the platform (credit card, T/T, or other supported methods)
4. Supplier produces; you track milestones in the order dashboard
5. Goods ship; you confirm receipt and release the payment

## What Trade Assurance Does NOT Cover

Trade Assurance protects the order terms — it is not a product warranty. It does not cover:

- Normal wear after delivery
- Damage caused by improper storage or installation
- Freight damage (that belongs to the shipping insurance claim)
- Disputes about things never agreed in writing

## How to Make It Work for Covers

Covers have specifications that matter: fabric denier, waterproof coating, stitching, zipper quality, and logo accuracy. Write every spec into the order or contract before confirming:

- **Fabric weight and composition** (e.g., 420D Oxford, PU-coated)
- **Size chart** with tolerances
- **Print/logo placement and colors** (approve a digital proof first)
- **Packaging requirements** (polybag, hang tag, carton specs)
- **Inspection terms** (pre-shipment inspection by photo, video, or third party)

## Practical Tips for Importers

- **Check the supplier's Trade Assurance limit** — a low limit signals low transaction history
- **Use it for first orders** — once trust is built, many buyers still keep Trade Assurance on
- **Keep evidence** — chat records, approved proofs, and inspection reports strengthen any claim
- **Understand claim timelines** — act within the claim window, not months later
