# Boat Cover Access Panels and Zippers

An access panel is the feature that decides whether a cover stays on the boat. Owners need to get into the boat without removing the whole cover — to check a bilge, reach a battery switch, retrieve gear or board through a gate. A cover with no access is a cover that gets taken off and left off.

For a buyer, the question is where the panels go and how they are built, because a badly placed or badly sealed panel is a permanent leak.

## What access is actually needed

Before placing a panel, list the reasons the owner will open the cover:

- **Boarding** — a door or gate in the side, at the point where people actually step in.
- **Helm access** — to reach controls, electronics or a battery switch.
- **Bilge and engine access** — for checks and for pumping.
- **Ventilation** — a panel that can be opened on a dry day to air the boat.

A boarding panel is used daily; a bilge-check panel rarely. They need different construction.

## Placement principles

- **Put the panel where the action is**, not where the pattern is convenient. A door on the centreline of the cover is useless if everyone boards from the side.
- **Avoid the highest point.** Water runs down the cover and will find any opening at the top.
- **Avoid low points where water pools**, for the same reason in reverse.
- **Keep the panel clear of structure underneath**, so it can open without fouling a rail, a seat or a console.
- **Consider the open cover's behaviour in wind** — a large panel left open in a breeze becomes a sail.

## Zipper specification

The zipper is the weakest component on any cover, and the part most likely to fail first.

**Coil versus moulded tooth.** Coil zippers are lighter and more flexible, and they tolerate fabric movement. Moulded-tooth zippers are stronger but stiffer and less forgiving on a curved panel.

**Marine-grade sliders and pulls.** Standard garment zippers corrode quickly in salt. Specify sliders and pulls intended for outdoor marine use, and prefer metal or coated metal over plain steel.

**Two-way sliders** allow the panel to open from either end, which matters on a long opening.

**A storm flap over the zipper.** This is the difference between a zipper that leaks and one that does not. The flap should overlap the closed zipper and shed water away from it.

## Installation detail

- **Reinforce both ends of the zipper.** The ends take the highest load when the panel is pulled open.
- **Allow a gutter.** Position the zipper so water on the cover runs beside it rather than into it.
- **Seal the stitching** at the zipper tape if the panel sits on a waterproof section.
- **Provide a means to secure the panel open**, so it does not flap against the boat.

## What to avoid

**Zippers as an afterthought.** A zipper added to a finished pattern usually sits on a curve it cannot follow, and it puckers.

**Long single zippers on a curved panel.** The longer the zipper, the more it fights the curve. Two shorter zippers with a fixed section between them often work better.

**Unflapped zippers on a horizontal panel.** These leak in the first serious rain.

## What to specify

- panel positions marked on the pattern drawing, with the reason for each
- zipper type, slider material and whether it is two-way
- storm flap dimensions and orientation
- reinforcement at both ends
- sealing at the tape
- a note in the care instructions on lubricating and rinsing the zipper
