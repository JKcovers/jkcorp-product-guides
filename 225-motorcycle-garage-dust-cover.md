# Motorcycle Garage Dust Cover — The Indoor Protection Guide

Most motorcycle covers are designed for the outdoors — waterproof, wind-resistant, and heavy. But if the bike lives indoors, the requirements are completely different. A motorcycle garage dust cover protects against the enemy that actually damages a stored bike: dust, dirt, and accidental bumps. Choosing the right indoor cover means understanding what dust does to a motorcycle.

## Why Dust Is a Problem

- Dust settles into tank paint and scratches it during wiping
- Dust and moisture hold against chrome, dulling it over time
- Brake dust and road grime on the chain attract moisture and cause rust
- Dust in the switchgear and levers accelerates wear
- A dusty bike looks neglected even when it runs perfectly

## Indoor vs. Outdoor Cover

| Feature | Indoor (Dust) Cover | Outdoor Cover |
|---------|--------------------|--------------|
| Waterproofing | Not needed | Essential |
| Weight | Light (1-2 kg) | Heavy (2-4 kg) |
| Breathability | Critical — prevents condensation | Important |
| Inner lining | Soft, to protect paint | Soft, to protect paint |
| Wind straps | Optional | Essential |
| Price | Lower | Higher |

## Key Features of a Good Dust Cover

1. **Soft fleece or cotton lining** — protects tank and fairings from scratches
2. **Breathable fabric** — lets humidity escape so chrome and steel do not corrode
3. **Stretch hem** — keeps the cover fitted so dust does not slip underneath
4. **Full-length coverage** — protects wheels, forks, and the rear swingarm
5. **Ventilation grommets** — allow airflow without letting dust settle in

## Fit and Sizing

Indoor covers should fit snugly — a loose cover collects dust between the fabric and the paint, which is worse than no cover at all. Measure the bike's length, height to the top of the screen, and width at the mirrors. Choose a size that follows the bike's outline without baggy sections.

## Common Mistakes

- **Using a waterproof cover indoors** — it traps condensation and corrodes the bike
- **Covering a wet bike** — moisture gets trapped under the fabric; always dry the bike first
- **Skipping the lining check** — cheap covers have abrasive inner surfaces
- **Covering on a dirty floor** — the bottom edge picks up grit and transfers it to the wheels

## Storage Routine

Wipe the bike down, dry the exhaust and chain, then fit the cover. Every few weeks, remove the cover and air the bike — this prevents moisture pockets from forming. If the garage is damp, add a small dehumidifier or silica packs near the bike.

## When an Indoor Cover Is Not Enough

If the garage is unheated and humid, pair the dust cover with a battery tender and a light coat of protective wax on exposed metal. The cover protects from dust; climate control protects from corrosion.
