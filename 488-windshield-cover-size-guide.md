# Windshield Cover: Universal vs Custom Size

The first specification decision on any windscreen cover is whether it will be universal or vehicle-specific. The choice drives inventory cost, fit quality, return rate and the price the product can command. It is a commercial decision as much as a technical one.

## Universal fit

A universal cover is a generously sized panel that will overlap the windscreen aperture of a broad range of vehicles. It is held by straps, magnetic edges, or flaps closed into the doors.

**Advantages**
- One SKU covers hundreds of vehicle models
- Minimal inventory risk and simple purchasing
- Cheapest to produce and to stock
- Easy to range in a retail or online channel where SKU count is limited

**Limitations**
- Overlap at the edges, which can look untidy
- Gaps at the corners of steeply raked or heavily curved screens
- Can obscure wiper park position or mirror mountings
- Coverage around camera and sensor housings is approximate

## Vehicle-specific fit

A specific cover is patterned to one windscreen, with cut-outs that follow the screen outline, wiper position, mirror mount and any sensor housing.

**Advantages**
- Clean appearance and full coverage
- Correct clearance for wipers and cameras
- Supports a premium price point
- Better retention, because the panel is not fighting excess material

**Limitations**
- One SKU per model or model family
- Inventory commitment and slower-moving lines
- Requires accurate patterns, which means measuring vehicles or obtaining reliable data

## How to decide

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| General retail, wide vehicle mix | Universal, in two or three sizes |
| Premium brand, single model focus | Vehicle-specific |
| Fleet or dealer programme, known vehicle list | Vehicle-specific for the dominant model |
| Online-only, long-tail vehicle coverage | Universal with a clear size table |
| Very large or unusually shaped screens | Vehicle-specific, or an oversize universal panel |

A practical middle path is a small number of universal sizes — for example a compact car size, a mid-size size, and a large truck and SUV size. This captures most of the benefit of specific fit at a fraction of the inventory cost, and it is the structure most successful ranges use.

## Sizing by measurement

If a range is to be sold across many vehicle types, specify by the windscreen's own dimensions rather than by vehicle name. The dimensions that matter are the width across the base of the screen, the width across the top, and the vertical height measured at the centre. Vehicles with strongly curved screens also need the corner radius considered, since this is where universal panels leave gaps.

Publishing a table that maps common vehicle models to the correct size is the single most effective way to reduce returns on this product.

## Clearance checks

Whatever the fit, confirm that the cover does not obstruct the wiper arms in their park position, does not cover a rain or light sensor, and does not interfere with the mirror mounting. These are the three points where a cover that otherwise fits well becomes an annoyance.
