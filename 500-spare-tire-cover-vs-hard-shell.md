# Spare Tire Cover vs Hard Shell Cover

Rear-mounted spares are protected by one of two product types: a soft fabric cover or a rigid hard shell. They are often sold against each other, but they solve different problems for different customers. Understanding the split matters both for buyers building a range and for anyone advising an end customer.

## Soft fabric covers

A fabric cover is a fitted sleeve, retained by an elastic hem, a drawstring or a buckle. It wraps the tire's face and tread.

**Advantages**
- Low unit cost and low freight cost
- Folds flat for storage — practical for seasonal removal
- Wide size coverage from overlapping size bands
- Excellent surface for printing and custom graphics
- Fits irregular carriers and unusual tire widths

**Limitations**
- Moves slightly at speed on large tires
- Fabric and coating age with UV exposure
- Less physical protection against impact or sharp contact

## Hard shell covers

A rigid shell is usually moulded plastic or fibreglass, frequently with a metal locking mechanism.

**Advantages**
- Rigid, so it holds a finished shape
- Better resistance to impact and abrasion
- Lockable variants deter casual theft of the spare
- Often used as a styling element on SUVs and off-road builds
- Some designs accept paint-to-match finishing

**Limitations**
- Higher unit cost and much higher freight cost — bulky and not foldable
- Size-specific, so a broader inventory is needed to cover a range of tires
- Mould tooling makes customisation expensive and only viable at volume
- Can rattle or loosen on rough surfaces if mounting is poor

## Choosing between them

| Consideration | Soft cover | Hard shell |
|---|---|---|
| Unit and freight cost | Low | High |
| Custom graphics | Excellent | Limited without tooling |
| Impact protection | Basic | Strong |
| Lockable security | No | Available |
| Size coverage per SKU | Wide range | Narrow |
| Seasonal storage | Folds flat | Bulk storage needed |
| Typical buyer | Dealers, accessory brands, general retail | Off-road enthusiasts, premium SUV owners |

## Where they compete and where they do not

They overlap at the mid-market, but the buying logic differs. A customer concerned with appearance and brand projection can satisfy either. A customer who wants low-cost protection during a road trip wants the fabric cover. A customer building an off-road vehicle with a lockable, painted spare housing wants the shell.

For a distributor, stocking both is sensible because the fabric cover carries the volume and the shell carries the margin. The important operational difference is inventory complexity: a handful of fabric SKUs can cover most tires, while shells require a separate SKU per size.

## The practical recommendation

Sell the fabric cover as the default and the hard shell as the upgrade. Make the size-selection process identical for both, so a customer who returns for the other format already knows which size to order.
