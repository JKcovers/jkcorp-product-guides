# Fireplace and Outdoor Fire Pit Covers — Protection for Heat and Elements

Outdoor fire pits and fireplaces are expensive, heavy, and exposed to brutal weather year-round. A quality cover protects the investment — but fire equipment covers have special requirements standard furniture covers don't.

## Why Fire Equipment Needs Specific Covers

Fire pits face: rain that rusts the steel bowl, ash residue that traps moisture, UV that fades powder coating, and soot that stains fabric. They also stay hot for hours after use — a cover that touches a warm surface can melt.

## Cover Features for Fire Equipment

- **Heat tolerance:** Look for covers rated to at least 150°F (some reach 200°F+) — hot metal contact resistance
- **Waterproof with sealed seams:** Rain is the #1 rust cause
- **UV-resistant outer:** Protects powder-coated finishes
- **Ventilation:** Trapped moisture from a slightly damp pit promotes rust
- **Drawstring or elastic hem:** Wind security
- **Stain resistance:** Ash and soot don't wash out of cheap fabric

## Sizing

Measure the pit's outer diameter (or width/depth for square pits) and height. Add 2-4 inches for fit clearance. Custom sizes available for unusual shapes.

## The Heat Safety Rule

Always let the fire pit cool completely (2-4 hours minimum) before covering. Covering a warm pit:
- Traps heat and accelerates cover degradation
- Risks melting the fabric
- Traps moisture inside (condensation on a cooling pit)

## Cleaning

- Shake out ash and debris weekly
- Wipe with a damp cloth
- Rinse after rain (acidic rain residue degrades fabric)
- Air dry before storing
