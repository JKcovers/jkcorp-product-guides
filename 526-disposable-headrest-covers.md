# Disposable Headrest Covers

A disposable headrest cover solves a hygiene problem that a washable cover cannot: it is used once and thrown away, so there is no laundering cycle, no storage of damp covers and no risk of transferring contamination between users.

That makes it a different product from a reusable cover, with a different cost model and a different set of buyers.

## Where they are used

- **Service loan cars and courtesy vehicles**, where the next driver expects a clean interior.
- **Test drives and demonstrator vehicles**, particularly in high-turnover dealerships.
- **Auction and remarketing sites**, where vehicles are inspected and moved by many people.
- **Ride-hail and taxi**, as an alternative to laundering.
- **Medical and patient transport**, where hygiene requirements are stricter.
- **Valeting and detailing**, to keep a freshly cleaned interior clean until handover.

In most of these, the requirement is not comfort. It is a visible, single-use barrier that can be removed in seconds.

## Material

**Non-woven polypropylene.** The standard material: cheap, light, tear-resistant enough for a single use, and available in white or printed.

**Paper or tissue blends.** Cheaper and softer, but weak when handled and prone to tearing at the elastic.

**PE film.** Waterproof and cheap, but non-breathable and unpleasant against the head in a warm car.

**Laminated non-woven with a film backing.** Used where a liquid barrier is required, at higher cost.

The choice is driven by whether the cover must resist liquid or only contact.

## Design and fit

- **Elasticated hem** is the usual closure: fast to fit, no hardware, and it accommodates a range of headrest sizes.
- **A rectangular or slightly tapered shape** covers the rear face, which is what the next user sees.
- **Perforated rolls or dispenser packs** reduce handling and keep unused covers clean.
- **Printed branding** is common: a dealer logo on a disposable cover is seen by every subsequent user.

Fit should be loose enough to slip over a headrest in one motion, and tight enough not to fall off in use.

## The cost model

Disposable covers are bought on cost per use, not per unit, and the comparison should include:

- **Unit cost**, which is low but scales with volume.
- **Laundering cost** avoided, including collection, washing, drying and loss.
- **Storage**, which is bulkier than a stack of reusable covers but does not need drying space.
- **Waste disposal**, which is the main objection and often a procurement criterion.

A reusable cover wins on cost at high volume with reliable laundry; a disposable wins where turnover is unpredictable or hygiene requirements are strict.

## Environmental considerations

Single-use plastics attract scrutiny, and buyers increasingly ask about it.

Practical responses:

- specify a recyclable or compostable non-woven where the barrier requirement allows
- reduce the material weight without compromising tear strength
- offer a reusable option alongside, for high-volume routes
- state the material clearly so disposal is correct

## What to ask a supplier

- material composition and weight
- whether it resists liquid or only contact
- elastic type and expected fit range
- packing format: loose, roll or dispenser
- print capability and minimum quantity
- disposal guidance, and whether a recyclable grade is available
