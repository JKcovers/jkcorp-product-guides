# Large Dog Seat Covers — Handling 100+ Pound Passengers

A Great Dane, Mastiff, or Bernese Mountain Dog is a whole different challenge for a pet seat cover. Weight, claws, and momentum test the cover's limits every ride.

## The Big Dog Stress Test

Large dogs exert 3-5x the force of small dogs on a cover: repeated shifting, standing, and sudden turns create stress at seams, anchors, and the cover's edges. A cheap cover fails within months.

## What Large Dog Owners Need

**Heavy-duty fabric (600D+):** Claws dig into the surface constantly. 600D Oxford or quilted heavy polyester resists punctures that destroy 210D covers.

**Reinforced seams and anchors:** The seams take the dog's weight. Double-stitched seams and heavy-duty metal or reinforced plastic clips are essential.

**Full waterproof backing:** Large dogs drool more, and a 100-lb dog can't be lifted out — the cover must handle accidents without leaking to the seat.

**Door flaps:** A 100-lb dog exiting the vehicle claws the door panel. Flaps protect that area.

## The Anchor Rule

Large dogs need the cover anchored at BOTH headrests and the seatback. Loose covers slide under a dog's weight and become a trip hazard.

## Sizing

Measure the rear seat width and the seatback height. SUVs and trucks usually need XL covers. A cover sized for a compact car will rip at the seams with a large dog.

## Safety Note

Never let a large dog ride in the front passenger seat — the airbag is a serious danger. Always rear seat, always anchored cover or crash-tested crate.
