# Autumn Car Protection Checklist — Prepare Your Vehicle Before Winter

Autumn is the easiest season to skip: the sun is softer, the storms are rare, and your car looks fine after a weekend drive. But the months between September and November decide how your paint, rubber, and interior survive winter. An autumn car cover strategy is cheap insurance that pays off in spring. Here is the full checklist.

## Why Autumn Matters

- Falling leaves trap moisture against paint and etch clear coat within weeks
- Sap, bird droppings, and pollen are at their annual peak
- Cooler nights create condensation cycles that feed mildew
- UV exposure continues — up to 30% of summer intensity even on cloudy days

A car parked outdoors in autumn is exposed to more chemical damage than mechanical damage. Leaf acids, tree sap, and dew are the top three culprits.

## The Autumn Checklist

### 1. Deep Wash and Seal

Start with a full wash, including wheel wells and under the wipers. Clay bar the horizontal surfaces where leaves and sap collect. Apply a sealant or wax as the base layer — the cover protects, but the sealant protects the car from the cover itself on windy days.

### 2. Inspect Trim and Rubber

Check door seals, sunroof drains, and wiper blades. Autumn debris clogs drains faster than any other season. A blocked sunroof drain means water inside the cabin by November.

### 3. Choose the Right Cover

For autumn, the key requirement is breathable waterproof fabric. A cover that is waterproof but not breathable traps condensation under the fabric — the exact problem you are trying to avoid. Look for:

- 300D+ Oxford polyester or a multi-layer all-weather shell
- Water-repellent finish with a hydrostatic head rating of 3000mm or higher
- Ventilation panels or breathable inner lining
- Soft fleece lining to protect the fresh wax job

### 4. Weekly Check Routine

- Clear leaves off the cover every 3-4 days — a heavy pile of wet leaves adds weight and blocks breathability
- Check the straps and buckles after windy nights
- Re-tension the cover once a week as it settles

### 5. Battery and Tires

If the car will sit for more than two weeks, connect a trickle charger. Check tire pressure monthly — a cover doesn't stop slow leaks, and cold weather drops pressure 1-2 psi per 10°F.

## Autumn vs Winter Covers

| Feature | Autumn Cover | Winter Cover |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| Core job | Debris + dew + UV | Snow load + ice + cold |
| Weight | 300-420D | 420-600D+ |
| Priority | Breathability | Waterproofing |
| Fit | Close, custom | Slightly looser for layering |

A quality all-weather cover bridges both seasons. That is why most OEM buyers order one spec that works from September through March.

## When to Upgrade to Winter Setup

Move to the winter spec when night temperatures stay below 7°C (45°F) consistently. That is when condensation behavior changes and snow load becomes a real risk. If you store the car completely, switch to a long-term storage cover with higher water resistance.

## Final Walk-Through

Before the cover goes on for the last time this season:

1. Wash and dry the car completely
2. Seal the paint
3. Lubricate locks and hinges
4. Set tire pressure 3-5 psi above normal for storage
5. Fit the cover and secure all straps
