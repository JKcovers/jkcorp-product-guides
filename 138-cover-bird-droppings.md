# Bird Droppings and Tree Sap — Why They Destroy Paint and How Covers Stop Them

Bird droppings and tree sap are the two most underrated paint killers. Both are acidic, both bond to clear coat, and both cause permanent damage within days. A car cover is the only passive defense.

## The Damage Chemistry

**Bird droppings:** pH 3-4 (acidic). In direct sun, the acid heats and etches clear coat within 24-48 hours. The damage is a permanent dull spot that requires paint correction to remove.

**Tree sap:** Bonds chemically to clear coat. Removal requires solvent (rubbing alcohol or dedicated sap remover), which can strip wax and stress the clear coat. If sap sits for weeks in sun, it can etch permanently.

## The Damage Timeline

| Time | Bird Dropping | Tree Sap |
|------|---------------|----------|
| 1 day | Surface residue | Sticky spot |
| 2-5 days | Clear coat etching begins | Bonds to clear coat |
| 1-2 weeks | Permanent etch spot | Deep bond, possible etch |
| 1+ months | Requires professional correction | May require repaint |

## How Covers Help

A cover creates a physical barrier: droppings and sap hit the cover, not the paint. When the cover gets dirty, you wash the cover — not the car. The paint underneath stays untouched.

## The "Quick Rinse" Trap

Some owners rinse droppings off daily instead of using a cover. This works but requires constant vigilance — one missed day in summer sun is enough to etch. A cover is passive protection that never forgets.

## Cover Selection for This Threat

- **Full coverage:** The cover must reach the roofline and hood completely
- **Easy to wash:** A cover that's a hassle to clean gets abandoned
- **Durable outer:** Droppings and sap degrade fabric too; choose UV-treated Oxford

## The Bottom Line

If your car parks under trees or near birds (which is most outdoor parking), a cover is the cheapest paint insurance available. A $60 cover prevents $300-2,000 in paint correction.
