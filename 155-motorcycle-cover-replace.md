# Motorcycle Cover Replacement Signs — When It's Time to Let Go

A worn-out motorcycle cover isn't just cosmetic — a degraded cover can actually damage your bike. Here's how to know when to replace yours.

## The Danger of an Old Cover

A cover past its prime stops protecting and starts harming:
- **Cracked waterproofing lets rain through** — then the wet cover holds moisture against the bike
- **Frayed seams catch wind and flap** — abrading your paint
- **UV-weakened fabric tears** — exposing the bike mid-storm
- **Mildew spores** — transfer to the bike's seat and plastics

## The 6 Replacement Signs

**1. Water stops beading.** Pour water on the cover. If it soaks in instead of beading up, the waterproof coating is gone.

**2. Fabric feels brittle or stiff.** Pinch the fabric. If it cracks or feels papery, UV has broken down the fibers.

**3. Visible fraying or tears.** Any tear larger than a coin will grow — wind finds every weak point.

**4. Elastic hem has lost stretch.** A loose hem means wind gets under the cover.

**5. Persistent musty smell.** Mildew has colonized the fabric — cleaning won't fully remove it.

**6. Color has faded noticeably.** Fading means the UV inhibitors are exhausted — the cover is no longer protecting your bike's paint.

| Cover Type | Typical Lifespan |

## Extending Life

- Wash every 2-3 months (outdoor covers)
- Store dry in the included bag
- Repair small tears immediately with patch tape
- Never machine-dry (kills waterproofing)
