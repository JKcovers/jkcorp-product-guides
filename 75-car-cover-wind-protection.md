# Car Cover Wind Protection — Why Straps and Elastic Hems Matter

A car cover that flaps in the wind is worse than no cover at all. The constant fabric movement abrades paint, wears out the cover, and lets water in at stress points. Here's how to ensure your cover survives windy conditions.

## What Wind Does to a Poorly Secured Cover

At 20 mph, wind lifts the fabric and slaps it against the car body thousands of times per hour. Each slap is a micro-scratch on the clear coat. At 40+ mph, the cover can balloon and rip at seams or tear free entirely.

## The Four Wind-Protection Systems

**Under-body straps (essential):** 2-4 straps that pass under the car and cinch the cover to the body. This is the primary defense — the cover becomes a fitted second skin rather than a loose sheet.

**Elastic hem (essential):** A full elastic band around the bottom edge keeps the cover hugging the bumper lines. Combined with straps, it prevents wind from getting under the cover.

**Mirror pockets (important):** Pre-shaped pockets for side mirrors eliminate the loose fabric flaps at the car's widest point — the area that catches wind first.

**Weighted edges (nice-to-have):** Some premium covers add weighted hems for extra hold-down force in high-wind regions.

## Installation Matters

- Always use ALL the straps, not just the front ones
- Position straps per the manufacturer's diagram (usually under the front, middle, and rear)
- After installation, run a hand around the hem to check it's fully seated under the bumper
- Re-tighten after a windy day — straps stretch slightly on first use
