# Cover Logo Design Tips — 10 Rules for a Brand That Sells

Your logo on a car cover is walking advertising — every parked car with your brand is a billboard. But cover logos have unique constraints: fabric surfaces, stitching, print limits, and exposure to sun and weather. These cover logo design tips help you create a logo that looks sharp, survives outdoors, and sells.

## Tip 1: Design for the Fabric, Not the Screen

What looks crisp on a screen can blur on fabric. Logos for covers need bold shapes, strong contrast, and clean lines.

**Rule:** test your logo at small size on fabric before committing to production.

## Tip 2: Keep It Simple

Detailed logos with fine lines and gradients do not survive embroidery, heat transfer, or screen printing. Simple logos scale and reproduce better.

**Rule:** if the logo is unclear at 3 cm, simplify it.

## Tip 3: Choose High-Contrast Colors

On a dark cover, a dark logo disappears. On a light cover, a light logo vanishes. Contrast is everything.

**Rule:** pick logo colors that contrast with the cover fabric you will use.

## Tip 4: Account for UV Fading

Outdoor covers sit in brutal sun. Some ink colors fade faster than others — reds and oranges fade first.

**Rule:** specify UV-resistant inks or embroidery threads for outdoor covers.

## Tip 5: Size the Logo for the Surface

A logo sized for a business card is lost on a car cover. Covers offer large print areas — use them.

**Rule:** scale the logo to the cover surface; a 20-40 cm logo is typical for car covers.

## Tip 6: Respect the Print Method

Each method has limits: embroidery needs simplified shapes, screen printing has color limits, heat transfer allows photo detail but can peel.

**Rule:** choose the method first, then design within its limits.

## Tip 7: Include the Website or Handle

The point of the logo is leads. Add a URL or social handle in the design where space allows.

**Rule:** one clear contact element — website or handle — on the cover.

## Tip 8: Consider Placement

The logo placement affects both looks and print quality — hood, trunk, or side panels are the standard spots.

**Rule:** place the logo where the fabric is flat — avoid seams and stress areas.

## Tip 9: Get a Production Proof

A digital mockup is not a production proof. Ask for a physical or high-res proof on the actual fabric.

**Rule:** approve only after seeing the logo on the real cover fabric.

## Tip 10: Protect Your Design

Before sending logo files to suppliers, know your rights and the supplier's usage terms.

**Rule:** keep vector files (AI/EPS) ready, and clarify logo ownership in the order terms.

## The Golden Rule

**Simple, bold, high-contrast, and tested on real fabric.** A cover logo that follows these rules becomes a brand asset, not just decoration.
