# Boat Covers and T-Tops — Compatibility Guide

T-top boats (center consoles with a hardtop) present a cover-fitting challenge. Standard covers don't accommodate the T-top structure. Here's what works.

## The T-Top Problem

A T-top adds height (4-5 feet above the gunwale), a hard roof surface, and often electronics (radar arch, antennas). Three options exist for covering a T-top boat:

## Option 1: T-Top-Specific Cover

A custom cover with a built-in T-top enclosure — a canopy section that fits over the hardtop and connects to the hull cover with zippers.

**Pros:** Complete protection, integrated look, covers electronics.
**Cons:** Most expensive option, needs professional fitting.

## Option 2: Cockpit-Only Cover

A cover that encloses the open cockpit area only, attaching to the T-top's underside and the gunwales — leaving the hardtop exposed.

**Pros:** Much cheaper, still protects the cockpit (seats, console, electronics below).
**Cons:** The hardtop itself stays exposed to weather.

## Option 3: Full Cover With Cutouts

A custom cover with shaped cutouts that fit around the T-top supports.

**Pros:** One-piece protection.
**Cons:** Custom fabrication required; the hardtop roof itself is still exposed to UV.

## Decision Guide

| Situation | Recommendation |
|-----------|---------------|
| T-top with electronics, year-round storage | Option 1 (full enclosure) |
| T-top, cockpit protection priority | Option 2 (cockpit cover) |
| Budget constraint | Option 2 |
| Aluminum T-top (less UV concern) | Option 2 |

## Measuring for a T-Top Cover

Measure: gunwale-to-gunwale beam, centerline length, T-top height above gunwale, and the footprint of the hardtop. Custom manufacturers need all four.
