# Furniture Cover Waterproof Ratings — What 2000mm Actually Means

Furniture cover listings throw around numbers like "3000mm waterproof" — but most buyers don't know what the number means. Here's how to read waterproof ratings and choose the right protection for your patio.

## What the Rating Means

The number is the hydrostatic head — the height of a water column (in millimeters) the fabric can withstand before water penetrates. It's a lab measurement, not a marketing claim.

| Rating | Performance | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| 1000mm | Light drizzle, morning dew | Covered patios |
| 2000mm | Moderate rain | Most outdoor furniture |
| 3000mm | Heavy sustained rain | Exposed patios, all-season |
| 5000mm | Extreme downpours, pooling water | Fully exposed, no cover above |

## Why Higher Isn't Always Better

Beyond 3000mm, the extra waterproofing usually comes from heavier coatings that reduce breathability. On furniture covers, breathability matters: without airflow, condensation forms under the cover, and mildew grows on cushions. The sweet spot for most patio furniture is 2000-3000mm with good breathability.

## Other Specs That Matter More

- **UV resistance:** The #1 killer of furniture covers. Look for UV-treated fabric, not just waterproofing.
- **Seams:** Taped or sealed seams prevent leaks at the stitch lines — the most common failure point.
- **Ventilation:** Flaps or vents reduce condensation and wind lift.
