# Magnetic vs Hook Attachment for Fender Covers

A fender cover that will not stay in place fails at its only job. Retention is therefore the specification that decides whether the product works, and two systems dominate: magnets sewn into the cover's edges, and hooks or straps that fix to a point on the vehicle.

## Magnetic attachment

Magnets are sewn along the edges and corners of the cover, holding it against the vehicle's steel wing.

**Strengths**
- Fast to fit, reposition and remove
- No straps to route, nothing to clip
- Sits flat across the whole panel, giving even coverage
- No anchor points needed on the vehicle

**Limits**
- Requires a ferrous body panel. Aluminium, composite and plastic panels give nothing to grip
- Magnet quality varies widely. Thin, low-grade magnets hold poorly and weaken over time
- Exposed magnet edges can scratch paint if not encapsulated
- Very close to the panel edge, so positioning matters on a curved wing

The single most important specification is **full encapsulation** — the magnet sealed inside the fabric with no metal exposed. This protects the paint and stops the magnet corroding.

## Hook and strap attachment

Hooks or straps fix to a point on the vehicle, typically the wheel arch lip, a bolt hole, or under the bonnet edge.

**Strengths**
- Works regardless of body panel material
- Very secure once tensioned, because the load is carried by a fixed anchor
- Cheap to produce and easy to repair
- Unaffected by panel curvature

**Limits**
- Slower to fit; requires finding an anchor point each time
- Anchor points are not always available in a convenient position
- Straps can be routed into hot or moving areas if used carelessly
- Tension concentrated at the anchor can distort the mat

## Which to choose

| Condition | Recommended |
|---|---|
| Steel-bodied vehicles, frequent use | Magnetic |
| Aluminium or composite body panels | Hook and strap |
| Mixed fleet of unknown vehicles | Combined magnet and hook |
| Exposure to wind (open bay, outdoor work) | Combined, or hook with secure anchor |
| Fastest possible fitting | Magnetic |
| Lowest unit cost | Hook and strap |

## Combined systems

The most reliable covers use both methods: encapsulated magnets along the leading edges to hold the panel flat, plus hooks or straps at the corners to carry the primary load. This costs more and complicates production, but it addresses the most common failure — a corner lifting or the mat sliding under a leaning technician.

For professional use on a mixed vehicle fleet, the combined system is usually the correct specification. It removes the need to keep two different covers in the workshop and eliminates the failure mode that produces the most customer complaints.

## What to verify before ordering

Test retention on a real vehicle with someone leaning on the cover, not simply laid flat on a bench. Check that magnets are fully sealed and consistently strong between batches. Confirm the stitching at every attachment point is reinforced, and that no part of the retention system contacts the paint or intrudes into the engine bay.
