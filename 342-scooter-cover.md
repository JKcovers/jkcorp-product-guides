# Scooter Cover Buying Guide: Small Bike, Smart Protection

Scooters are the most exposed vehicles on the road: parked on streets, outside shops, and on apartment balconies for hours at a time. A scooter cover is a cheap insurance policy for a machine that costs thousands. Here is what to look for in a scooter cover.

## Why Scooters Need Covers

- **Street parking:** scooters sit in the weather far more than cars
- **UV damage:** dashboards and plastics fade quickly in sun
- **Rain and dew:** moisture attacks electrical connectors and metal parts
- **Seat damage:** rain pools on seats and seeps into the storage compartment
- **Theft visibility:** a covered scooter is less attractive to casual thieves

## Sizing a Scooter Cover

Scooters range from 50cc commuters to 300cc maxi-scooters. Measure length (bumper to bumper, typically 65-85 inches) and height (seat and mirrors). Size classes:

| Class | Length | Examples |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Small | Under 70 inches | 50cc commuters |
| Medium | 70-78 inches | 125-150cc scooters |
| Large | 78-90 inches | Maxi-scooters with tall screens |

## Material and Features

- **210D-300D weatherproof polyester:** the mainstream choice
- **Heat-shield lining:** protects the seat and bodywork from sun
- **Elastic hem:** keeps the cover on in wind
- **Lock loop:** pairs with a cable or disc lock
- **Reflective strips:** visibility for street parking at night
- **Storage bag:** compact for carrying in the underseat compartment

## Installation and Care

- Install from the front, pulling the cover over mirrors and screen
- Cinch the elastic hem under the floorboard
- Wipe the cover dry before folding to prevent mildew
- Wash monthly in mild detergent and re-treat waterproofing yearly

## Quick Takeaways

- Measure length and seat height; 50cc commuters and maxi-scooters need different classes
- Heat-shield lining is the key upgrade for sun-soaked seats and dashboards
- Use the lock loop with a cable or disc lock in high-theft areas
- Carry the storage bag in the underseat compartment so the cover is always on hand
- Wipe the cover dry before folding to keep the underseat storage mildew-free
- Add a light strap or bungee over the hem in windy street-parking spots

## Common Mistakes

- Buying a motorcycle cover that is too large (flaps and scratches)
- Skipping the lock loop in high-theft areas
- Ignoring heat shielding for dark-colored seats
- Storing a wet cover in the underseat compartment
