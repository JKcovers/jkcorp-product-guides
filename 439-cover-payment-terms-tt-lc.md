# Payment Terms for Cover Orders: T/T vs L/C

Payment terms decide who carries the risk at each stage of an order. For a first order with a new cover factory, that clause matters more than price and more than lead time.

T/T and L/C are the two structures most cover orders use. Neither is inherently safer; they shift risk rather than remove it.

## T/T in practice

A telegraphic transfer is a bank wire, and the common structure for covers is a deposit followed by a balance:

- **30/70** — 30% on order confirmation, 70% before shipment. The default for most first orders.
- **50/50** — more common on smaller or custom orders where the factory carries more risk.
- **Net terms** — 30 to 60 days after shipment. Rare for a new supplier, achievable after a track record.

The risk sits with the buyer on the deposit and with the seller on the balance. A factory that ships before full payment is extending credit; a buyer who pays in full before shipment is extending it in the other direction.

## L/C in practice

A letter of credit moves the guarantee to the banks. The buyer's bank commits to pay against compliant documents, so the seller is protected by a bank rather than by the buyer's word.

That protection has a cost and a discipline:

- **Cost.** Opening, advising and confirming fees, plus a margin or cash collateral. On a small cover order these can be a meaningful share of the value.
- **Discipline.** Payment depends on documents matching the credit exactly. A typo in a description, a late shipment date, or a missing certificate becomes a discrepancy, and discrepancies are where most L/C disputes begin.

An L/C is worth considering on a large first order with an unfamiliar supplier, or where your bank requires it. It is overkill on a 500-piece trial.

## The middle options

**Trade Assurance and platform escrow** sit between the two. Payment is held by the platform and released on agreed milestones. It costs little, requires both parties to use the platform, and covers the common failure modes rather than every one.

**Documentary collections** are cheaper than an L/C but offer weaker protection, because the bank does not guarantee payment — it only handles documents.

## What to agree alongside the terms

The payment clause is only as good as what it is paying for:

- a written specification, signed off before the deposit
- an inspection right before the balance is released
- a clear statement of what counts as delivery
- a defect remedy that survives the final payment

Without those, the safest payment structure still leaves you arguing about a product you have already paid for.

## A practical default

For a first cover order: 30/70 by T/T, with a pre-shipment inspection and the balance released against the inspection report. For a large order with a new supplier: consider an L/C, or split the order into two shipments so the second payment is informed by the first delivery.
