# Spare Tire Cover Installation Guide

Installation looks trivial, and for most spare tires it is. The problems appear with the wrong size, with an aggressive off-road tire, or with a cover that relies on the wrong retention method. This guide sets out a repeatable process and the checks that prevent a cover coming loose at speed.

## Before you start

Confirm the cover's stated size band against the tire, not the rim. The correct reference is the overall diameter of the mounted, inflated tire, together with its section width where depth matters. If the cover is elasticated, it should be a firm fit before installation — a cover that goes on easily is likely to move in service.

A clean tire matters more than it seems. Grit trapped between the cover and the sidewall will abrade both. Wipe the tire down before fitting.

## Fitting a rear-carrier spare

1. **Clean the tire** and let it dry. Avoid silicone dressings, which leave a slippery film.
2. **Locate the valve stem.** Most covers are fitted so the stem opening or a marked panel aligns with the valve.
3. **Fit the top first.** Pull the cover over the upper edge and work downward around the circumference, distributing the elastic evenly.
4. **Work the hem around the lower edge** by rotating the cover slightly rather than stretching one section hard.
5. **Check the fit** — the hem should sit behind the widest point of the tire, not on the tread shoulder.
6. **Confirm clearance** to the rear door, camera, sensors and handle.
7. **Test at low speed** before highway use, then re-check the hem position.

## Drawstring and buckle variants

A drawstring cover is tightened after fitting and then tied off, with the excess tucked away so it cannot flap. A buckle or strap design is tightened in sequence around the circumference, adjusting incrementally rather than fully tensioning one point at a time. On any design where tension is adjustable, re-check after the first drive.

## Under-vehicle winch mounts

A winch-mounted spare is raised into position from beneath. Fully elasticated covers are the practical choice here, because there is no access to a drawstring once the tire is stowed. Check clearance to exhaust components and to the chassis as the tire is raised; a cover that fits when lowered can foul when stowed.

## Motorhome and van side mounts

Side-mounted spares sit proud of the body, so the cover needs enough depth to wrap the full section width. Where the vehicle is stored outdoors for long periods, prioritise UV stability and fit security over graphics.

## Maintenance after installation

Remove and clean the cover periodically rather than washing it in place. Dirt held against the fabric shortens its life, and the removal also lets you check the tire. Follow the manufacturer's care instructions: a water-resistant coating is the component most easily destroyed by hot washing or strong detergent.

## When a cover will not fit properly

If the hem cannot sit behind the tire's widest point, the cover is too small. If the fabric balloons or the hem lifts at the top, the cover is too large or the retention method is unsuitable for the tire's weight. Neither is fixed by installing it more tightly — specify a different size band.
