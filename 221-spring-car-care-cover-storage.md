# Spring Car Care After Cover Storage — The Reawakening Checklist

After months under a cover, your car deserves a proper spring awakening. A cover protects through winter, but storage itself creates conditions — condensation, dust settling, battery drain — that need attention before the first drive. This spring car care after cover storage checklist gets you back on the road safely.

## The First Uncovering

1. **Choose a dry, low-humidity day** — uncovering on a damp day undoes months of protection
2. **Remove the cover carefully** — fold it as you go, don't drag it across the paint
3. **Inspect the paint immediately** — look for moisture, mildew, or stains in raking light
4. **Air out the car** — open all doors and the trunk for a few hours

## The 10-Point Spring Checklist

### 1. Inspect the Cover

Before folding it away for the season:

- Check for mildew on the inner lining (treat or clean immediately)
- Look for wear at contact points
- Wash, dry, and store it loose in a breathable bag
- A cover stored damp will be ruined by fall

### 2. Paint and Body

- Wash the car thoroughly to remove dust and any residue that settled on the paint
- Check for new scratches or dings from storage (straps, debris, animals)
- Re-wax or inspect the sealant — winter storage wears the protective layer

### 3. Battery

If the battery stayed in the car, check the charge with a multimeter. Expect it low after months of standby drain. Charge it fully before starting; jump-starting a deeply discharged battery stresses the alternator.

### 4. Fluids

| Fluid | Check |
|-------|-------|
| Oil | Level and condition |
| Coolant | Level and freeze protection still valid |
| Brake fluid | Level and color (dark = contaminated) |
| Washer fluid | Top up with fresh mix |

### 5. Tires

- Check pressure — cold months lose 2-5 psi per 10°F
- Inspect for flat spots from long-term parking
- Look for cracking on sidewalls, especially if the car sat in UV

### 6. Brakes

Surface rust on rotors is normal after storage — it clears after the first few stops. If the car sat for months, test brakes gently in an empty lot before normal driving.

### 7. Rubber Seals

Inspect door and trunk seals for hardening or cracking. Lubricate with silicone dressing. A seal that failed during winter storage will leak in spring rains.

### 8. Interior

- Vacuum and clean — dust settled for months
- Check for mildew smells (a moisture absorber that failed)
- Inspect upholstery for mold or moisture damage

### 9. Undercarriage

After the first wash, inspect the undercarriage for rust, corrosion, or signs of rodents (nesting material, chewed wires). Rodents love stored cars.

### 10. First Drive

Take a 20-30 minute drive to warm everything up, circulate fluids, and let the alternator recharge the battery. Listen for new noises — belts and hoses sometimes harden over storage.

## If Something's Wrong

- **Mildew on paint** — wash with mildew cleaner, then clay and polish the affected area
- **Flat-spotted tires** — drive 50-100 km on highways to round them out; severe cases need replacement
- **Battery won't hold charge** — replace it; a sulfated battery never recovers fully
