# Motorcycle Cover and Lock — The Right Way to Combine Security

A cover hides your bike. A lock secures it. Together they create a deterrent that makes your bike dramatically less attractive to thieves. Here's how to combine them properly.

## The Layered Security Model

| Layer | Purpose | Time to Defeat |
|-------|---------|:--:|
| Visible cover | Hides value, signals owner cares | 30-60 sec to remove |
| Disc lock | Immobilizes wheel | 2-5 min with angle grinder |
| Chain + ground anchor | Anchors bike to fixed object | 5-10 min with tools |

Thieves target bikes they can steal in under 3 minutes. Two layers push most bikes past that threshold.

## The Cover + Lock Connection

**Lock holes:** Quality covers include reinforced openings (grommeted or webbing) that let a chain pass through the cover AND around the wheel or frame. This means:
- The thief can't remove the cover without cutting the chain
- The bike is anchored while still fully hidden
- You get two security layers with one lock

## How to Set Up

1. Park and let the bike cool (for heat-panel safety)
2. Route a chain through the cover's lock hole and around a fixed anchor (post, ground anchor, or through the rear wheel + frame)
3. Close the chain lock
4. Add a disc lock on the front wheel as a third layer
5. Position the cover so the lock holes align with your anchor point

## Common Mistakes

- **Locking through the cover only:** If the lock doesn't secure the bike to anything, it's pointless
- **Front wheel only:** Bikes are lifted and carried; always anchor the frame if possible
- **No cover at all:** A bike visible on the street attracts opportunistic thieves
- **Locking a light bike to itself:** A thief can load a 400-lb bike into a van; anchor to something fixed

## The Bottom Line

Cover alone = deterrent. Cover + anchored lock = defense. Cover + anchored lock + disc lock + alarm = as close to theft-proof as street parking gets.
