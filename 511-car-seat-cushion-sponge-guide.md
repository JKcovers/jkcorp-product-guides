# Car Seat Cushion Sponge Guide

In cushion manufacturing, "sponge" is a trade word for foam, and the choice of sponge is the single biggest influence on how a car seat cushion feels, how long it lasts and what it costs. Two cushions can look identical from the outside and behave completely differently, because the difference is inside.

## The sponge families used in seat cushions

**Moulded polyurethane foam** is the material most vehicle seats are built from. It is cast in a mould to a contoured shape, which lets the cushion support the body where load actually falls instead of presenting a flat slab. Moulded foam can be produced at different densities and firmness levels from the same chemistry.

**Memory foam** is a dense, temperature-sensitive polyurethane that slowly recovers its shape. It spreads pressure well over a long sitting period, but it is heavier, more expensive and warmer to sit on than standard foam.

**Closed-cell foam** — polyethylene or EVA — does not absorb water and holds its shape under repeated loading. It is firmer per unit of thickness, which makes it useful for thin cushions and for any product that may get damp.

**Gel and gel-infused layers** are usually added over a foam base rather than used alone, because gel redistributes pressure but does not provide structure.

## Density, firmness and support are three different things

Buyers often treat these as one number. They are not:

- **Density** is how much material is in a given volume. It relates to durability and how the foam ages, not to how soft it feels.
- **Firmness** is how much the foam deflects under load. Two foams of the same density can be made at different firmness.
- **Support** is how the finished cushion distributes load, which also depends on shape and thickness.

A low-density foam can be made firm, and a high-density foam can be made soft. Judging durability from how a sample feels in the hand is unreliable; the useful question is how the foam is specified and how it has been load-tested.

## Thickness and shape

Adding thickness is not the same as adding comfort. A thick soft cushion lets the sitter sink until the body meets whatever is underneath, which on a car seat means the seat frame. A thinner cushion with the right firmness and a contoured shape usually performs better.

Shape does more work than most buyers expect. A raised front edge reduces the tendency to slide forward; a relief cut under the thighs removes pressure on the backs of the legs; a lumbar support at the base changes posture rather than just cushioning.

## Breathability and heat

Closed cushions trap heat, and a car interior in summer is already the hardest environment a cushion will see. Perforated covers, breathable mesh fabric or ventilation channels through the foam all help, and the choice should match the climate the product is sold into.

## What to specify on a purchase order

- foam type and density, stated numerically
- firmness or load-deflection target
- overall thickness and contoured profile
- cover fabric and whether it is breathable
- rated load and how it was verified
