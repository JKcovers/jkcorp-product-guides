# Custom Logo Fender Cover for Brands

A branded fender cover occupies an unusual position among promotional products. It is used by professionals, it is visible in a workshop every working day, and it stays in service for years. For tool brands, parts distributors, dealerships and workshops, that combination delivers more sustained exposure than most giveaway items.

## Why the format suits branding

The cover is a large, mostly flat panel that is laid out in the open while work is carried out. In a workshop it is seen by technicians, by customers collecting vehicles, and by anyone walking through the bay. Unlike a sticker or a sign, it is handled rather than merely looked at.

The audience match is also unusually precise. Anyone using a fender cover is already working on a vehicle, which is exactly the audience for tool brands, lubricants, parts suppliers and service businesses.

## Where branding tends to sit

The most legible position is the centre of the panel, oriented so it reads correctly when the cover is in place on the vehicle rather than when it is folded on a shelf. This distinction is frequently missed in artwork approval, and it is the most common reason a branded cover looks wrong in use.

Three practical placement rules:

- Keep artwork clear of the outer edges, which curve and are stretched when the mat is positioned.
- Avoid the areas where magnets are sewn; stitching and hardware interfere with print.
- Use high-contrast marks. The panel is viewed at a distance and at an angle, so fine detail and small text are lost.

## Decoration methods

Printing directly onto the face material gives the widest design freedom and works on both vinyl-faced and coated textile covers. Heat transfer offers similar freedom with a different hand feel. Sewn-on patches and embroidered badges are suited to smaller marks and give a premium appearance, but they add a step and cost, and they can lift at the edges over time.

For workshop use, print durability matters more than on a retail item: the cover will be wiped down, folded and handled daily. Confirm the print resists the fluids used in the environment it is intended for.

## What to settle before ordering

| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Panel dimensions | Determines printable area after edge margin |
| Face material | Determines which decoration methods are viable |
| Artwork orientation | Must read correctly when fitted, not folded |
| Fluid exposure | Determines print and face durability requirements |
| Colour references | Brand colours must be specified rather than described |
| Order volume | Drives method choice and setup cost |

## Sampling

Approve a physical sample, positioned on the cover in the orientation it will be used. A flat digital proof will not show how the design reads across a curved panel, nor how the print sits relative to the magnet seams.

## The commercial case

A branded fender cover sits in the same bracket as branded workshop consumables: modest unit cost, long service life, and daily visibility to a narrowly defined audience. For a tool brand or parts distributor selling into the trade, it is one of the more efficient branding surfaces available.
