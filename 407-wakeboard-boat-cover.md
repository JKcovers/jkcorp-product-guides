# Wakeboard Boat Cover Guide

A wakeboard boat is defined by its tower. Everything else about the cover — the pattern, the support, the fit, the way it trailers — follows from the fact that a tall, rigid, load-bearing structure stands in the middle of the boat and cannot be covered by a normal cover shape.

Getting this wrong produces the most common failure in the segment: a cover stretched over a tower, abrading at the contact points, and pooling water in the gap between the tower legs.

## Tower up or tower down

The first decision is whether the cover is designed for the tower folded or erected.

**Tower up.** The cover is cut with openings or sleeves for the tower legs and a raised section over the top. This allows the boat to be covered with the tower ready to use, and it is the usual choice for a boat kept on a lift or mooring.

The difficulty is sealing around the tower legs. Each opening is a path for water, and each leg is an abrasion point. Reinforced collars and a raised boot around each leg solve both, and they are the parts that separate a good wakeboard cover from a generic one.

**Tower down.** The cover is a conventional shape and the tower folds flat. Simpler and cheaper, but only practical if the tower folds easily and the boat is not used frequently.

Most buyers want tower-up, and most complaints come from covers that attempt it without proper leg reinforcement.

## Support and water shedding

A wakeboard boat has no hardtop, so the cover needs support:

- **The tower itself** can carry part of the cover if the pattern is built for it, but it must not be the only support.
- **A pole over the cockpit** lifts the cover so water sheds aft.
- **The windshield** provides a hard point forward, but should not carry tension.

The open bow on many wake boats is deep and wide; as with any bowrider shape, it needs its own support or it becomes a basin.

## Equipment that changes the pattern

- **Tower speakers and lights** sit outside the tower legs and must be cleared, not pressed.
- **Board racks** project sideways and will tear a cover that is cut close.
- **Bimini tops** may stay up under the cover, which adds height at the rear.
- **Bow filler cushions** raise the bow floor.

A supplier building a wakeboard pattern needs the boat's actual tower configuration, not just its length. This is the dimension that most often causes a return.

## Trailering

Wake boats are frequently trailered, and the tower makes them tall. Two consequences:

- **The cover must tolerate highway airflow**, which means a closing hem and no loose spans. A cover bridging from the tower to the bow at speed will balloon and fail.
- **Tower-mounted accessories** should be removed or the cover specified to clear them, because a cover flapping against a speaker pod will damage both.

## What to look for

- reinforced collars or boots at every tower leg
- clearance for speakers, lights and board racks
- bow support, independent of the windshield
- a closing hem, capable of trailering
- vents, since a wake boat has a large enclosed cockpit volume
- sealed seams at the tower openings, which are the primary leak path

## Buying notes

Ask for the tower configuration on the pattern drawing, and for a fitment check on the actual boat with the tower up. A wakeboard cover that has not been fitted to a tower is a general boat cover with a hole in it.
