# Stadium Cushion MOQ and OEM Guide

Stadium cushions are one of the easier accessories to source on an OEM basis. There is no electronics, no tooling for most constructions and no safety certification in most markets. The difficulty is not manufacturing capability — it is writing a brief specific enough that two suppliers quote the same product.

## What a supplier needs to quote accurately

A quotation built on "stadium cushion, good quality" cannot be compared with any other. Five inputs make it comparable:

1. **Dimensions** — length, width, and thickness, as ranges if a size run is intended.
2. **Foam specification** — type and density, and the firmness target.
3. **Construction** — cover fabric, base fabric, whether the cover is removable, and how seams are finished.
4. **Attachment and features** — sleeve, loops, straps, handle, pockets.
5. **Branding** — plain, printed, embroidered, or label only, with artwork and placement.

Everything downstream depends on these being written down, and the foam specification is the one most often left vague.

## What drives the minimum order quantity

MOQ in this category is not set by tooling, because soft cushions generally need none. It is driven by:

- **Material runs** — foam and coated fabric are bought in batches, so a small order can be limited by the mill or foam supplier minimum rather than by the cushion maker.
- **Print setup** — screen printing carries a setup cost per colour; sublimation and transfer printing carry a per-design setup. Printed cushions therefore carry a higher minimum than plain ones.
- **Cover construction** — a removable cover with a zip adds sewing operations and raises the practical minimum.

Ask for the minimum per design and per colour separately. A headline MOQ that hides a per-colour minimum is the most common source of a quotation that cannot be met.

## Sampling

Sampling is cheap in this category and should not be skipped. Order a pre-production sample against the approved specification and check:

- finished dimensions against the tolerances quoted
- foam firmness under a seated load, not by hand
- attachment method on the intended seat profile
- print position relative to seams and fold lines
- stitching at the handle and strap anchors

A sample that passes on a table can still fail on the real bench, and attachment problems are much cheaper to fix at sample stage than in a production run.

## Quality control points

- dimensional consistency against the approved sample across the run
- foam recovery after repeated loading
- foam does not absorb water where closed-cell was specified
- bond between fabric and any coated or laminated backing
- print adhesion and colour consistency between lots
- stitch strength at handles, straps and pocket anchors

A pre-shipment inspection covering these points is proportionate for this product class.

## Landed cost, not unit price

Soft cushions pack at high density, so freight is usually a small share of cost — but not a negligible one on a bulky cushion ordered at the cheapest unit price. Compare landed cost including freight and duty rather than FOB figures alone, and check whether the carton size was optimised, because that is where a supplier can quietly add cost.
