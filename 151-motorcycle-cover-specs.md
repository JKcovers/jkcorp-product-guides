# Motorcycle Cover Weight and Wind Rating — Specs That Actually Matter

Motorcycle cover specs can be confusing: fabric weight, wind ratings, and waterproof ratings all get thrown around. Here's what each number actually means and how it translates to real-world protection.

## Fabric Weight: GSM vs Denier

**Denier (D)** measures the thickness of individual fibers — 210D, 420D, 600D. Higher = thicker yarn.

**GSM (grams per square meter)** measures the total fabric weight including coatings. A 420D Oxford can range from 160 to 250 GSM depending on the coating.

**The practical rule:** For outdoor motorcycle covers, look for 420D+ fabric and 180 GSM+. Anything lighter is indoor-grade. A 600D/250 GSM cover is the heavy-duty tier that survives years of outdoor abuse.

## Wind Rating: What It Means

Wind ratings describe how much wind the cover can resist when properly secured. Typical ratings:

| Rating | Real Condition | Cover Type |
|--------|---------------|------------|
| 20 mph | Light breeze | Budget covers |
| 40 mph | Strong gusts | Mid-range with straps |
| 60 mph | Storm-force | Heavy-duty multi-layer |

**Important:** Wind ratings assume the belly strap and elastic hem are properly installed. A 60 mph-rated cover without its straps fails at 20 mph.

## Waterproof Rating: Hydrostatic Head

Measured in millimeters (mm) of water column the fabric can withstand. For motorcycle covers:

- **1000mm** — light rain, marginal
- **2000mm** — moderate rain, minimum for outdoor
- **3000mm+** — heavy sustained rain, recommended

## The Seam Factor

Ratings mean nothing if the seams leak. Check for:
- Taped or sealed seams on premium covers
- Double-stitched stress points
- Heat-welded construction on waterproof models

## How to Read a Spec Sheet

When comparing covers, build a quick checklist:
- [ ] Fabric: 420D+ / 180 GSM+
- [ ] Wind rating: 40 mph+ with straps
- [ ] Waterproof: 3000mm+
- [ ] Seams: sealed/taped
- [ ] Heat panels: 300°F rated
