# CE and UKCA Marking for Car Covers: What Actually Applies

Buyers routinely ask for a "CE certificate" for car covers. In most cases that request cannot be met, because a textile car cover is not a product that CE marking applies to. Understanding why saves months of chasing a document that does not exist for this category.

This is not legal advice, and requirements differ by product construction and market. It is a map of what to ask for instead.

## What CE marking actually is

CE marking is a declaration that a product meets the requirements of specific European directives or regulations that apply to it — machinery, electrical equipment, toys, personal protective equipment, construction products and similar categories.

It is not a general quality mark, and it is not available for every product. A product is CE marked because a directive covering it requires it, not because a buyer would like it.

UKCA works the same way for the Great Britain market: it applies to products within scope of UK regulations, not to everything sold.

## Why a car cover usually falls outside

A standard textile car cover — cut, sewn, coated, with no electrical parts — is not within the scope of the directives that require CE marking. Asking a factory for a CE certificate for it produces one of three outcomes: a refusal, a certificate for a different product, or a document that is not what it appears to be.

The third outcome is the risk. Certificates of conformity for unrelated products circulate in this trade, and a buyer who files one without reading the scope has a compliance problem, not a solution.

## What does apply in the EU

**Chemical restrictions.** REACH restricts certain substances in textiles, and the candidate list of Substances of Very High Concern creates communication obligations above a threshold. Test reports — SVHC screening, azo dyes, formaldehyde — are the evidence buyers normally ask for.

**General product safety.** The EU's General Product Safety Regulation requires consumer products to be safe and to carry traceability information. A declaration and a technical file are the practical outputs.

**Textile labelling.** Fibre composition must be stated for textile products sold to consumers, with defined fibre names.

**Flammability**, for products where it applies, is a separate question and depends on the end use.

## When CE might genuinely apply

If the cover incorporates an electrical element — a heated cover, an integrated fan, a powered device — then electrical safety and electromagnetic compatibility directives come into scope, and CE marking becomes relevant for that assembly.

That is a different product with a different compliance path, and it should be quoted and certified as such.

## What to ask a supplier instead

- Which test reports exist for the fabric, and to which standard
- Whether the report covers the colour you are ordering
- A declaration of conformity to the applicable product safety rules
- Fibre composition statement for labelling
- The name and address of the responsible economic operator in the EU

That last item is now a practical requirement for placing goods on the EU market, and it is the question most buyers forget to ask.
