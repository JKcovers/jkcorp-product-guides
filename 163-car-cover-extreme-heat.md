# Car Cover for Extreme Heat Deserts — Temperature Management That Works

Desert climates push car covers to their limits — 120°F+ ambient heat, brutal UV, and sand that never stops blowing. A standard cover fails in months. Here's what desert drivers need.

## The Desert Challenge

Desert parking means:
- Surface temperatures over 180°F on dark paint
- UV intensity 2-3x higher than temperate regions
- Wind-blown sand that abrades everything
- Extreme temperature swings (100°F+ day, 50°F night)

## The Desert Cover Specs

**Light color — non-negotiable:** Silver or white reflects 70-80% of solar radiation. Dark covers absorb heat and turn your car into a solar oven. This is the single most important desert feature.

**Reflective outer layer:** Aluminum or titanium-dioxide coated fabric reflects sunlight before it reaches the car. Tested interior temperatures drop 15-25°F versus no cover.

**99% UV rejection:** Desert UV destroys paint, dashboards, and tires faster than anywhere. "UV resistant" marketing isn't enough — demand the percentage.

**Sand-resistant weave:** High-density 600D Oxford blocks wind-blown sand better than loose-weave fabrics.

## Desert Usage Tips

1. Let the car cool 20 minutes before covering — covering a hot engine traps heat
2. Use a windshield sunshade under the cover for maximum effect
3. Rinse sand off the cover before removing it — sand scratches paint
4. Check straps monthly — heat and wind stress them faster

## The Bottom Line

Desert car owners should never buy a dark, non-reflective cover. Light reflective covers with 99% UV rejection are the only sensible choice for year-round desert parking.
