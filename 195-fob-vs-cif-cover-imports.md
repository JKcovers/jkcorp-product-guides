# Understanding FOB vs CIF for Cover Imports

FOB and CIF are the two most common incoterms in cover importing — and the most misunderstood. The difference is not just a few letters: it decides who pays for shipping, who bears the risk, and how you budget the landed cost. Here is a plain-English guide.

## FOB — Free On Board

With FOB, the seller's responsibility ends when the goods are loaded onto the vessel at the port of shipment. The buyer arranges and pays for:

- Ocean freight
- Insurance
- Customs clearance at destination
- Inland transport after arrival

**Risk transfers to the buyer** once the cargo is on board.

## CIF — Cost, Insurance and Freight

With CIF, the seller pays for and arranges:

- Freight to the destination port
- Marine insurance (minimum cover)
- Export clearance

**Risk still transfers at the port of shipment** — CIF changes who pays for freight and insurance, not who carries the risk of loss in transit.

## The Key Difference in One Table

| Aspect | FOB | CIF |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Seller arranges export clearance | Yes | Yes |
| Seller pays ocean freight | No | Yes |
| Seller pays marine insurance | No | Yes (minimum cover) |
| Risk transfers | At shipment port | At shipment port |
| Buyer controls shipping | Yes | Limited |
| Price comparison | Lower quote | Higher quote (freight included) |

## Which One for Cover Imports?

**Choose FOB if you:**
- Buy in volume and negotiate your own freight rates
- Want control over the shipping line and schedule
- Work with a freight forwarder you trust

**Choose CIF if you:**
- Import smaller volumes where buying freight is inefficient
- Prefer a single price from the supplier
- Are new to importing and want fewer moving parts

## The Hidden Costs Both Miss

Whatever the incoterm, the landed cost still includes destination port fees, customs duty, VAT/tariff, and trucking from the port to your warehouse. Always ask the supplier for the carton dimensions and weight so your forwarder can quote the final leg accurately.

## Common Mistakes

- Thinking CIF means the seller owns the risk in transit — it does not
- Comparing FOB quotes with CIF quotes directly — add freight to FOB first
- Ignoring insurance: minimum CIF cover is not full replacement value
