# Spare Tire Cover Buying Guide: Sizing, Materials and Mounting Options

A spare tire is the one component on a vehicle that sits outdoors permanently and gets almost no attention. That combination — constant UV exposure, zero maintenance, and a rubber compound that degrades when unprotected — makes a spare tire cover one of the most logical accessories in the category. For buyers, it also happens to be a straightforward product to range: low unit cost, simple construction, and a strong visual surface for customisation.

This guide covers the specification decisions that matter before a first order.

## What the product actually protects

A spare tire exposed on a rear carrier or under a vehicle degrades in three ways: UV breaks down the rubber compound, ozone and temperature cycling crack the sidewall, and dirt embedded in the tread holds moisture against the casing. A cover addresses all three by blocking light and keeping the surface clean. The buyer benefit is easy to explain, which is why the product sells without much education — a useful property for an accessory line.

## Sizing: measure the tire, not the rim

This is where most sourcing errors happen. The rim diameter printed on a tire sidewall describes the wheel, not the mounted tire. The cover has to fit the **overall diameter** of the inflated tire.

A rear carrier also needs a depth allowance, because the cover wraps the tread as well as the sidewall. The practical approach is to specify:
- overall tire diameter
- tire section width
- a tolerance range rather than a single dimension

Selling covers in overlapping size bands, rather than one size per rim diameter, reduces returns significantly. The full calculation for working out overall diameter from a sidewall code is covered in the linked size chart guide.

## Materials

Elasticated covers in a moderate denier polyester, typically with a water-resistant backing, are the mainstream construction. Heavier woven fabrics with a soft inner layer suit premium and off-road positioning. Leather-look vinyl remains popular in some markets for aesthetic reasons, but it is less forgiving in extreme cold and needs a different care message.

Key specification points:
- **Fabric weight** — heavier fabric holds shape on larger tires and resists flapping at speed.
- **Backing** — a soft or fleece lining protects polished wheels and reduces abrasion.
- **Elastic hem** — the retention method. A full elastic hem fits a range of tires; a drawstring or buckle gives a tighter, more secure hold on large off-road tires.
- **Stitching** — reinforced at the hem, where tension is highest.

## Mounting methods

Rear-mounted carriers are the most common. Others include under-vehicle winch mounts, side mounts on vans and motorhomes, and hard-shell carriers. Each changes the ideal fit: a winch-mounted cover benefits from a fully elasticated design, while a side mount on a motorhome often needs a larger panel because the tire sits proud of the body.

## Customisation

The cover is a flat, high-visibility panel, which is why printed and logoed versions outsell plain ones in many markets. Manufacturers use printed covers as dealer and brand promotion, and off-road buyers use them for personal graphics. Confirm the print method, the colour-fastness expectation, and how the artwork is positioned relative to the hem before approving a sample.

## Quality checks before ordering

Verify sizing against a real mounted tire, not a specification sheet. Check the elastic recovery after repeated stretching, the bond between fabric and backing, and whether the print survives a wash cycle. Ask for the care instruction wording — a cover that is marketed as machine washable but cannot survive one generates returns.
