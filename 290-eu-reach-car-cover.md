# EU REACH for Car Covers: Compliance Without the Headache

EU REACH for car covers is a recurring question for importers because REACH is the strictest chemical regulation in the world's second-largest cover market. The good news: covers are a low-chemical-risk textile product, and compliance is manageable with the right documentation from your factory.

## What REACH Requires

REACH (Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals) restricts substances of very high concern (SVHC) in articles sold in the EU. For a car cover, the practical obligations are:

- **SVHC declaration:** confirm the cover does not contain restricted SVHCs above the 0.1% threshold per article.
- **Restriction compliance:** specific entries (e.g., lead, phthalates, certain flame retardants) must not be exceeded.
- **Downstream communication:** importers must be able to provide safety information to customers on request.

## Where Car Covers Typically Face Issues

- **Dyes and pigments:** non-compliant azo dyes are a classic finding in cheap textiles.
- **Coatings:** PVC or PU coatings may contain restricted plasticizers or lead stabilizers.
- **Hardware:** grommets and buckles can carry nickel or lead, which have their own restrictions.
- **Waterproofing treatments:** some water-repellent chemistries are restricted or being phased out.

## Documents to Request from the Factory

1. REACH declaration (signed, per product line)
2. SVHC test report (current 239-substance list at time of test)
3. Specific restriction tests for lead, phthalates, nickel where hardware is used
4. Coating material safety data sheets

## Practical Compliance Steps

- Request REACH documents at sample stage and lock them into the contract
- Re-test annually or when the factory changes dye or coating suppliers
- Keep declarations per SKU, not per brand
- Choose suppliers who already export to the EU, not first-time exporters

## The Cost of Non-Compliance

Customs holds, batch recalls, and marketplace delisting are the realistic risks. A single non-compliant batch can cost more than a full year of compliance testing, so documentation is cheap insurance.

## Quick Compliance Checklist

- Signed REACH declaration per product line in hand before ordering
- SVHC test report dated within the last 12 months
- Hardware tested for nickel and lead release where grommets are used
- Declaration refresh whenever the factory changes dye or coating suppliers
- Keep all documents on file for importer audits and retailer requests
- Build compliance into the contract so documents arrive with each shipment
