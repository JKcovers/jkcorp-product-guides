# OEM Cover Quality Certificates — ISO, REACH, and What Actually Matters

Certificates are the trust currency of B2B trade — but not all certificates matter equally for covers. Buyers who know the difference between a marketing badge and a verifiable document negotiate from strength. Here is the guide to cover quality certificates.

## The Certificates That Matter for Covers

| Certificate | What It Proves | Relevance |
|-------------|----------------|-----------|
| ISO 9001 | Quality management system in place | High — process confidence |
| ISO 14001 | Environmental management | Medium — sustainability buyers |
| REACH | EU chemical compliance (textiles) | High for EU markets |
| CE | EU conformity (where applicable) | Product-specific — check |
| RoHS | Restricted hazardous substances | Low for covers, high for e-covers |
| SGS/BV inspection reports | Third-party QC on your shipment | High — order-specific |

## ISO 9001 — Read It Correctly

ISO 9001 certifies that a factory has a working quality management system — documented processes, trained staff, corrective actions. It does NOT certify product quality. A certified factory is still capable of mistakes; it is just more likely to catch them.

## REACH — The EU Textile Requirement

REACH restricts harmful chemicals in products sold in the EU. Covers sold in Europe should be tested for restricted substances — especially since fabric dyes and waterproof coatings can contain phthalates or heavy metals. Ask for the REACH test report, not just a claim.

## Material and Test Reports

Useful order-specific documents:

- **Fabric weight report** (GSM/denier verification)
- **Waterproof test report** (hydrostatic head, e.g., 2000mm+)
- **UV resistance report**
- **Color fastness report** — critical for printed covers
- **Flammability test** — required for some markets and applications

## How to Verify a Certificate

A scanned certificate proves nothing by itself. Verify:

1. Certification body — is it an accredited registrar (e.g., SGS, TUV, BSI)?
2. Certificate number — searchable on the registrar's public database?
3. Scope — does it cover the product type and factory address?
4. Validity dates — certificates expire; check the audit cycle
5. Factory name — matches the company you are dealing with

## What to Ask the Factory

- "Please send your ISO 9001 certificate and registrar details"
- "Do you have REACH test reports for this fabric type?"
- "Can you provide a test report with our order batch number?"
- "Which third-party labs have you worked with?"

## Red Flags

- Certificate image with cut-off edges or edited dates
- "We have ISO" but no registrar name
- Test reports without batch numbers or lab letterhead
- Reluctance to share documents before payment
