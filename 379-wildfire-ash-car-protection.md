# Wildfire Ash Protection for Cars

Wildfire ash is not the light grey dust it resembles. It is a mixture of burnt organic material, unburnt carbon and mineral compounds, and it stays chemically active after it lands. That is what makes the handling instructions different from ordinary dust removal — and what makes a cover worth more than it looks during fire season.

## Why ash damages paint

Ash deposits cause problems in three distinct ways:

- **Abrasiveness.** Ash contains hard mineral particles. Wiping or brushing a layer of ash drags those particles across the clearcoat and leaves fine scratches across the whole panel.
- **Chemical activity.** Ash is alkaline and reacts with water. After rain or morning dew, the wet layer becomes a mildly caustic film sitting directly on paint, and it stays there as long as it is not rinsed off.
- **Persistence.** Ash does not wash away completely on its own. A thin film tends to remain in panel gaps, window channels and trim recesses.

The first rule follows: never wipe ash dry. The second: do not let it sit wet.

## Where a cover helps

During a fire event, ash falls continuously for hours or days. Re-rinsing a car repeatedly is not practical, and in some conditions outside water use is restricted or simply a bad idea.

A cover changes the problem. Instead of ash landing on paint, it lands on a removable panel. At the end of the event the cover is taken off, rinsed away from the vehicle and cleaned — and the paint underneath was never exposed to the deposit.

Cover requirements in this situation are specific:

- **Fine-particle blocking.** The relevant property is the smallest particle the fabric stops, not the fabric weight. A tight weave matters more than a heavy one.
- **Full coverage.** Ash settles everywhere, including the roof, the window channels and the base of the windscreen. A partial cover leaves the highest-deposit areas exposed.
- **Secure fit.** A cover that flaps pumps ash-laden air underneath, which defeats the purpose. During fire season the fit is the specification that earns its cost.
- **No soft nap on the outside.** Fleece and textured outer surfaces trap ash and then hold it against the panel when the cover is refitted.

## Removal without doing damage

1. **Do not shake the cover over the car.** Take it off carefully and move it away before disturbing the ash.
2. **Rinse, never brush.** Both the cover and the car should be rinsed with water. Brushing is what creates the scratches.
3. **Rinse the car after removing the cover.** A cover does not seal perfectly, and a light deposit will have passed underneath.
4. **Clean the cover fully.** Ash left in the fabric is a load of abrasive waiting for the next fitting.
5. **Check drains and channels.** Ash consolidates in the windscreen scuttle, door drains and sunroof channels, which is where water damage starts.

## What a cover cannot do

A car cover protects paint and glass. It does not filter cabin air: smoke and fine particulates enter through the ventilation system and the door seals regardless. For cabin protection, the answer is the vehicle's recirculation setting and its cabin filter, not the cover.

A cover also does nothing for ash that has already bonded to paint. Once the surface has been marked, the remedy is a wash followed by machine polishing.
