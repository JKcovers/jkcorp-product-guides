# Trunk Mat Sizing and Fit

Most trunk mat returns are not quality complaints. They are fit complaints, and they usually come from two causes: a universal mat sold as if it were vehicle-specific, or a measurement taken from the wrong place.

Getting the dimensions right is a short exercise, and it removes most of the risk.

## Measure the floor, not the trunk

The trunk's external dimensions are irrelevant. What matters is the floor area the mat will sit on, measured inside the trim.

Take five measurements:

1. **Length**, from the seat-back line to the tailgate sill.
2. **Width at the widest point**, usually just inside the tailgate.
3. **Width at the narrowest point**, usually at the wheel arches.
4. **Depth of any well or recess**, where the floor is not flat.
5. **Seat-back angle**, for liners that extend up the seat backs.

A mat cut to the widest width will buckle at the wheel arches. A mat cut to the narrowest will leave gaps at the tailgate.

## The shaping problem

A trunk floor is rarely a rectangle. Typical obstructions:

- **Wheel arch intrusions**, curving inward on both sides.
- **Seat-back hinges and brackets**, projecting into the floor area.
- **Tie-down loops and rails**, recessed or raised.
- **A load sill step** at the tailgate.
- **A floor panel hinge**, where the under-floor storage opens.

A moulded mat is shaped around all of these. A flat mat has to be trimmed, and the trimmed edge is where it curls and frays.

## Trim-to-fit versus moulded

**Trim-to-fit.** The mat arrives oversized with cut lines. Cheap, universal, and dependent on the owner cutting accurately. The exposed cut edge is not sealed, and the fit is approximate at curves.

**Moulded.** Shaped to one vehicle's floor. Correct fit, formed lip, and no cutting. Model-specific, so the coverage list matters.

**Universal flat.** Neither shaped nor generous enough to trim usefully. The weakest option, and the one most likely to slide.

For a product sold online, the coverage list is the specification. A buyer who cannot find their vehicle will not order.

## Fit points that cause complaints

- **Blocked under-floor access.** The mat must lift with, or be cut around, the floor panel.
- **Covered tie-downs.** Owners use them, and a mat that hides them gets modified.
- **Interference with the load sill.** A mat that rides up the sill prevents the tailgate closing cleanly.
- **Movement.** A mat with no retention slides forward under braking and bunches against the seat backs.
- **Odour in heat.** Not a fit issue, but it is reported as one.

## Retention

A trunk mat should not rely on friction alone.

- **Moulded mats** locate on the floor's own contours.
- **Flat mats** need tabs, hook-and-loop patches or press studs.
- **Liners** attach at the seat-back headrests or with straps.

## Reducing returns

- publish the vehicle coverage list, with model years
- state whether the mat is moulded, trim-to-fit or universal
- show the mat installed in the actual vehicle
- state the lip height
- state whether under-floor storage remains accessible
- give the packed dimensions and weight

## What to ask a supplier

- the vehicle list and the model years covered
- whether the pattern is digitised from the actual floor
- lip height and whether it is formed or folded
- retention method
- whether the under-floor hatch is cleared
- tolerance on the cut dimensions
