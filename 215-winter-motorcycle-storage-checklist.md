# Winter Motorcycle Storage Checklist

Storing a motorcycle for winter is not the same as parking it in the garage. Without proper preparation, a bike that ran perfectly in October emerges in April with a dead battery, rusted tank, cracked tires, and seized cables. This winter motorcycle storage checklist covers every system, so spring starts with a ride instead of a repair.

## The 10-Step Winter Storage Checklist

### 1. Clean Thoroughly

Wash the bike completely — road salt and grime are corrosive and attract moisture. Dry every surface, including under the seat and behind the fairings. A clean bike stored under a cover stays clean; a dirty bike rusts under the dirt.

### 2. Fuel Treatment

Fill the tank to the top to prevent condensation, and add a fuel stabilizer. Run the engine for 5-10 minutes to circulate treated fuel through the carburetors or injectors. An empty tank rusts from the inside over winter.

### 3. Oil Change

Change the oil before storage. Old oil contains combustion acids that attack engine internals while the bike sits. Fresh oil protects the engine through months of inactivity.

### 4. Battery Care

- Remove the battery and store it indoors on a smart maintainer
- If it stays in the bike, disconnect the negative terminal
- Never leave a battery on the bike without a maintainer — it will discharge and sulfate

### 5. Tire Pressure and Position

- Inflate tires to the manufacturer's recommended pressure (or a few psi higher)
- Park on a center stand if available — this removes weight from the tires
- If a stand isn't available, rotate the wheels monthly to prevent flat spots
- Check tire pressure monthly; cold air loses pressure

### 6. Rust Protection

- Apply a light coat of rust inhibitor to the chain, fork tubes, and exposed metal
- Wipe chrome with a protectant
- Spray fogging oil into the cylinders through the spark plug holes (or run the engine with the fuel off to starve the cylinders)

### 7. Cables and Controls

Lubricate throttle, clutch, and brake cables. A cable that's dry in October will be seized by March. Check for fraying while you're there.

### 8. Coolant and Fluids

Verify coolant is at the correct level and concentration for freezing temperatures. Check brake fluid level. In very cold regions, verify the coolant mix protects to at least -30°C.

### 9. Choose the Right Cover

| Cover Type | Suitability |
|------------|-------------|
| Breathable motorcycle cover | Best — allows moisture out |
| Waterproof sealed cover | Risk — traps condensation |
| Indoor dust cover | Garage only |

A breathable motorcycle cover is critical. Even indoors, temperature swings create condensation. A non-breathable cover traps it against the paint and chrome.

### 10. Location and Final Check

- Store in a dry, ventilated space if possible
- Avoid direct contact with concrete floors (moisture rises) — use a mat or boards
- Make sure the cover is secured but not so tight that it presses on the windscreen

## Monthly Winter Visit

Once a month, uncover the bike in a dry garage, check for rust or moisture, and re-cover it. This 10-minute habit catches problems early and keeps the cover from holding condensation against the paint.
