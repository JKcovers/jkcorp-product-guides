# Hail Season Preparation for Vehicles — Complete Guide

Hail season costs vehicle owners billions in dent repair every year. A single storm can total a parked car's resale value in minutes. While no fabric cover stops a baseball-sized hailstone, proper hail season preparation dramatically reduces damage from the small-to-medium hail that accounts for most claims. Here is what actually works.

## Hail Size vs. Damage

| Hail Diameter | Typical Damage | Cover Help |
|---------------|----------------|------------|
| < 1 cm (pea) | Light dings on horizontal panels | High — padding absorbs impact |
| 1-2 cm (marble) | Visible dents, windshield risk | Moderate — reduces severity |
| 2-5 cm (golf ball+) | Panel damage, cracked glass | Low — seek shelter instead |
| 5 cm+ (baseball) | Severe structural damage | None — must be indoors |

The practical takeaway: covers and padding genuinely protect against the most common hail events, but shelter wins against everything.

## The Hail Protection Stack

### 1. Shelter First

- Covered parking is the only 100% protection
- Parking under a solid carport with a roof is second best
- Never park under trees — hail breaks branches, and falling limbs do more damage than hail

### 2. Padded Hail Covers

A dedicated hail car cover has a thick inner padding layer (often 10-25mm of cotton or foam batting) between the outer shell and a soft lining. The padding:

- Absorbs the kinetic energy of small-to-medium hail
- Spreads impact over a wider area, reducing dent depth
- Cushions the car when the cover itself is hit

### 3. Improvised Padding

If a dedicated cover isn't available:

- Pool noodles under a normal cover add surprising protection
- Moving blankets under a tarp work for one-off storms
- Cardboard over the roof and hood helps but flattens quickly

### 4. Layer the Coverage

A two-layer approach — soft blanket against the paint, waterproof cover on top — outperforms either layer alone. The blanket cushions; the cover stays waterproof.

## Hail Forecast Protocol

When hail is forecast:

1. Move the car to shelter if possible — this is the only reliable answer
2. If not, deploy the padded cover and secure all straps
3. Park away from trees, power lines, and poles
4. Fold side mirrors in; remove the antenna if possible
5. After the storm, inspect the roof, hood, trunk, and mirrors for dings before removing the cover

## After-Storm Inspection

- Check the paint in raking light — dents show better from an angle
- Document damage with photos before moving the car (insurance)
- Check the cover for punctures; a hail-punctured cover may need seam repair
- If the cover held up but shows impact marks, inspect the lining for tears that could scratch paint later

## Buying a Hail Cover

Key specs for a real hail cover: thick batting padding (not just fleece lining), waterproof outer shell, reinforced elastic hem, and a storage bag. Beware of covers marketed as "hail resistant" with only 2-3mm of padding — they protect against dust, not hail.
