# Boat Cover vs Mooring Cover — What's the Difference?

Boat owners often use "boat cover" and "mooring cover" interchangeably — but they're different products built for different jobs. Using the wrong one causes premature failure.

## Boat Cover (Trailering/Storage)

The general term for a cover used during transport or storage out of water.

- **Trailering covers:** Reinforced for highway speeds, extra tie-downs, snug fit
- **Storage covers:** Full protection for long-term dry storage, usually breathable

## Mooring Cover

A cover specifically designed to stay on the boat WHILE MOORED in water.

**Key differences:**

| Feature | Mooring Cover | Trailering Cover |
|---------|:--:|:--:|
| Seam sealing | ✅ Fully sealed | ⚠️ Partial |
| Fit | Loose enough to shed water | Snug for wind |
| Ventilation | Critical (moisture at mooring) | Moderate |
| UV resistance | ✅ Maximum | Standard |
| Reinforcement | Standard | ✅ Highway-strength |

## Why the Difference Matters

A mooring cover left on during a trailer trip flaps apart at 60 mph. A trailering cover left on at a wet mooring lets water through unsealed seams and rots the deck underneath.

## The Right Purchase

- Boat stays on a trailer: buy a trailering/storage cover
- Boat lives at a slip/mooring: buy a mooring cover
- Both: buy both, or a premium cover rated for dual use (rare)
