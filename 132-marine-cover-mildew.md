# Marine Cover Mildew — Prevention and Treatment Guide

Mildew is the most common reason boat covers get replaced early. It's not just ugly — mildew degrades fabric, smells terrible, and can damage the boat underneath. Here's how to prevent and treat it.

## Why Boat Covers Mildew

Mildew grows where three things coexist: moisture, warmth, and organic matter (dust, pollen, salt residue). Boat covers face all three constantly:
- Condensation from temperature swings
- Trapped moisture from a wet boat
- Dust and organic debris on the fabric

## Prevention (The Real Solution)

**1. Breathability is non-negotiable:** A breathable cover (solution-dyed polyester, breathable acrylic) lets vapor escape. Non-breathable covers are mildew factories.

**2. Never cover a wet boat:** Let the boat dry before covering. Wet hulls create the perfect mildew environment.

**3. Clean the boat before covering:** Organic residue feeds mildew.

**4. Ensure ventilation:** Use support poles so the cover doesn't sit flat on damp surfaces.

**5. Allow periodic airing:** On dry days, remove the cover and let everything breathe for an hour.

## Treatment (If It Happens)

**Mild mildew (surface spots):**
1. Rinse with fresh water
2. Scrub with a mildew cleaner (or 1:4 white vinegar:water)
3. Rinse thoroughly
4. Air dry in full sun (UV kills mildew)

**Heavy mildew:**
1. Use a commercial marine mildew cleaner
2. Follow the label — these are strong chemicals
3. Rinse completely
4. Re-waterproof after cleaning (cleaners strip coatings)

## The Mildew Warning

Never use bleach on marine fabric — it degrades the fibers and waterproofing. Never machine-dry a mildewed cover (heat sets the stain and smell).

## When to Replace

If mildew has penetrated the fabric's core (the smell persists after cleaning, the fabric shows dark staining throughout), replace the cover. Mildewed fabric is weakened and will fail early.
