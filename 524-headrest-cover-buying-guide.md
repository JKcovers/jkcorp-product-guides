# Headrest Cover Buying Guide

A headrest cover is a small product with four distinct markets: protecting the original upholstery, refreshing a worn interior, hygiene in shared vehicles, and branding. Each one implies a different specification, and a product built for one rarely serves the others well.

## The four uses

**Protection.** Keeps hair products, sweat and sun off the headrest, which is one of the first parts of an interior to show wear.

**Refresh.** Covers worn or stained headrests without reupholstering.

**Hygiene.** In taxis, ride-hail cars, loan cars and medical transport, a cover that can be changed between users is a practical measure.

**Branding.** A printed cover puts a logo at eye level behind every passenger.

## Fit: the hard part

Headrests are not standard. The main variables:

- **Shape.** Sleeve-type (a separate padded block on two posts), integrated (part of the seat back), and adjustable-with-wings.
- **Post spacing.** The distance between the two metal posts varies by manufacturer.
- **Post diameter.**
- **Width and height** of the pad.
- **Angle**, since some headrests tilt forward.

A cover sized for a sleeve-type headrest will not fit an integrated one, and a cover with fixed post holes will not fit a different post spacing.

The practical answer for a universal product is a stretch fabric with no post holes, which wraps the whole headrest. For a vehicle-specific product, the post spacing must be measured.

## Attachment

- **Elastic hem.** Simple, cheap, and adequate for a stretch cover.
- **Hook-and-loop flap.** Adjustable and fast to fit, but the hook side can catch hair and fabric.
- **Zip.** Secure and neat, but slower and a corrosion point in a damp interior.
- **Drawstring.** Adjustable, but can loosen with movement.

For high-turnover use such as ride-hail, speed of change matters more than neatness, which favours an elastic or hook-and-loop design.

## Material

- **Stretch polyester or spandex blend.** Fits a range of shapes, prints well, and is the standard for branding.
- **PU leather.** Wipes clean, looks original, and suits protection and refresh.
- **Fabric with foam backing.** Comfortable but absorbs, and is poor for hygiene use.
- **Non-woven.** Cheap, disposable, and used for single-use hygiene covers.

## Safety note

Headrest covers are generally outside the restraint system, but two points deserve care:

- **Do not obstruct the headrest's adjustment mechanism.** A cover that jams the posts prevents correct positioning, which matters for whiplash protection.
- **Keep the cover thin at the front face.** A thick pad changes the head-to-rest distance and reduces the protection the headrest was designed to give.

A cover should be a skin, not an added cushion, unless it is a purpose-made comfort product with that trade-off understood.

## Branding considerations

- **Print area.** The rear face is what passengers see; the front is rarely visible.
- **Colour fastness.** Headrests are in direct sun, and cheap dyes fade quickly.
- **Repeat orders.** A fleet will reorder; the print reference must be reproducible.
- **Washability.** Branded covers in shared vehicles need frequent washing without the print cracking.

## What to ask a supplier

- which headrest types the pattern covers
- post spacing and diameter, if the design uses post holes
- fabric composition and stretch range
- print method and colour fastness
- wash instructions and shrinkage
- packed quantity per set
