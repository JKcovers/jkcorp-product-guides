# Boat Cover Fabric Repair — Patch or Replace?

Boat cover damage is inevitable — UV, wind, and years of service take their toll. The question is always: patch it or replace it? Here's the decision framework.

## When to Patch

Small damage in a fabric that's otherwise healthy:

| Damage | Patch? |
|--------|:--:|
| Tear under 2 inches | ✅ |
| Small burn hole (cigarette, exhaust) | ✅ |
| Seam separation under 4 inches | ✅ |
| Grommet pull-out | ✅ |
| Torn tie-down loop | ✅ |

## When to Replace

| Damage | Replace? |
|--------|:--:|
| Multiple tears across the fabric | ✅ |
| Fabric brittle or sun-rotted | ✅ |
| Waterproofing gone (water soaks through) | ✅ |
| Mildew embedded in the weave | ✅ |
| Seams failing in multiple places | ✅ |

**The key test:** Is the fabric itself still strong, or is it just the tear that's damaged? If you can easily tear it further by hand, the fabric is done — patches won't hold.

## How to Patch

**For fabric tears:**
1. Clean and dry the area
2. Use marine-grade patch tape or fabric adhesive
3. Apply patch to BOTH sides for strength
4. Seal edges with seam sealer
5. For seams: hand-stitch with marine thread, then seal

**For grommet pull-out:**
- Use a reinforcement plate kit — patching fabric alone won't hold a grommet

## Patch Materials

- **Self-adhesive marine tape:** Quick, temporary
- **Fabric + adhesive:** Stronger, semi-permanent
- **Stitch + seal:** Permanent repair for seams

## The Cost Logic

A patch kit costs $15-30. A new quality boat cover costs $300-800. If the fabric is sound, patching is obviously worth it. If the fabric is degraded, patching is throwing money away.
