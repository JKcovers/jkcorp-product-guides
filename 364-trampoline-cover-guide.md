# Trampoline Cover Guide — Protect Your Trampoline from Weather and Debris

Trampolines sit in the yard year-round, exposed to rain, UV and falling branches. Without a cover, the jumping mat degrades, the frame rusts and the springs corrode — and a worn mat is a safety hazard. A trampoline cover extends the life of every component.

## Why Your Trampoline Needs a Cover

- **Mat protection** — UV light is the #1 cause of mat weakening and tearing
- **Frame and spring protection** — rain and humidity cause rust on metal parts
- **Debris defense** — leaves, acorns and bird droppings stay off the jumping surface
- **Safety** — a well-maintained mat and springs are critical for safe bouncing
- **Longer lifespan** — a covered trampoline lasts years longer

## Choosing the Right Size

Trampoline covers are sized by **frame diameter** — common sizes are 8 ft, 10 ft, 12 ft, 14 ft and 15 ft. Measure the outer edge of the metal frame from one side to the other. Match your measurement to the nearest size; some covers stretch slightly to fit. Also note whether your trampoline has an enclosure net — covers are designed for use with the net in place or removed.

## Features to Look For

- **Elastic hem with straps** — wraps under the frame to hold the cover secure
- **UV-stabilized fabric** — resists fading and embrittlement
- **Water drainage** — a slight dome or taut fit stops water pooling
- **Reinforced edges** — withstands repeated stretching during installation

## Care and Maintenance

- Remove leaves and debris before covering
- Ensure the cover is taut — sagging fabric collects water and snow
- Check straps and elastic hems each season; replace when stretched out
- Clean with mild soap and water once a year

## Key Takeaways

- Measure the frame diameter and match the nearest cover size
- 600D Oxford with under-frame straps handles all weather best
- Keep the cover taut so water drains instead of pooling
- A covered trampoline is safer and lasts several years longer
