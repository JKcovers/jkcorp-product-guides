# Car Cover Snow Load Rating — Don't Let Winter Collapse Your Cover

Heavy snow is a cover killer. A cover rated for light dusting can collapse under 30 cm of wet snow, trapping moisture against the paint and tearing at the grommets. Understanding snow load ratings — and the physics behind them — keeps your car protected all winter.

## The Snow Load Problem

Wet snow weighs roughly 100-200 kg per cubic meter. A sedan's roof area of about 4 square meters can carry 400-800 kg of packed snow. That load:

- **Collapses flat covers** onto the paint, where trapped moisture freezes
- **Tears grommets** as the fabric strains against its anchors
- **Stretches seams** permanently, ruining the fit
- **Creates ice sheets** that damage the paint when the snow slides off

## What Makes a Cover Snow-Resistant

| Feature | Role in Snow Resistance |
|---------|------------------------|
| Dome/tent shape | Sheds snow instead of pooling it |
| Center support poles | Hold fabric up under heavy loads |
| High-denier fabric (600D+) | Resists tearing under weight |
| Reinforced grommets | Survive strap tension as snow accumulates |
| Sloped panels | Guide snow off the sides |

## The Pole Question

For regions with regular heavy snow, poles make the difference. A pole-supported cover forms a peak that sheds snow continuously. Pole-less covers need manual snow clearing after every storm.

## Snow Maintenance Routine

1. After each storm, push snow off with a soft broom — never a shovel
2. Never let snow sit for days; freeze-thaw turns it into ice armor
3. Check straps after clearing — snow load stretches them
4. If the cover is frozen to the car, wait for thaw before removal

## Reading the Rating

There is no universal snow load standard for car covers. Use these proxies:

- **Fabric denier** — 600D and above for snow regions
- **Pole support** — required for areas with 30 cm+ single storms
- **Waterproof coating** — wet snow must not soak through
- **Brand claims** — treat "winter rated" as marketing until you see fabric specs

## When to Skip the Cover

In extreme storms, the best move is sometimes no cover. If a blizzard dumps more snow than you can clear for days, a cover can trap ice against the paint and tear under weight. When in doubt, clear snow off the cover after every storm or remove the cover entirely and park under any available shelter. A clean, dry, covered car beats a snow-buried covered car.

## Key Takeaways

- Snow load is a function of fabric, shape, and support — not just thickness
- Pole-supported covers shed snow automatically; flat covers require manual clearing
- In heavy snow regions, buy fabric 600D+, poles, and reinforced grommets
