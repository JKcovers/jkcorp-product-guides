# Fender Cover Maintenance and Storage

A fender cover is bought to protect paint, which means the cover itself must never become the thing that damages it. That is almost entirely a matter of maintenance and storage, and it is the part of ownership that customers receive the least guidance on.

## Why maintenance matters more than it appears

Grit trapped in a cover is abrasive. A mat that has picked up swarf, sand or dried coolant and is then laid on the next vehicle transfers that material directly onto the panel, with the weight of a leaning technician pressing it in. The cover designed to prevent scratches becomes the cause of them.

Two habits prevent this: wiping down between vehicles, and never storing a contaminated cover against a clean one.

## Cleaning by material

| Face material | Routine | Periodic |
|---|---|---|
| Vinyl or PVC-faced | Wipe with a damp cloth after use | Wash with mild detergent, rinse, hang dry |
| Coated textile | Wipe down; brush off dry contamination first | Hand wash; avoid high temperature |
| Heavy untreated textile | Brush off dry debris | Machine wash per supplier instructions; dry fully |

The recurring mistake is washing a water-resistant or coated cover at high temperature. That is what destroys the coating, and once the coating is gone, fluid resistance goes with it. The care instruction on the label should be the same as the one used in production testing; if the two disagree, the product will generate returns.

## What not to do

- Do not machine wash a vinyl-faced cover unless the supplier explicitly supports it.
- Do not dry any cover on a radiator or in direct sun for extended periods, which ages the face material.
- Do not store a damp cover folded. Trapped moisture causes mildew and degrades the coating.
- Do not use solvent-based cleaners on printed panels, which lift the print.
- Do not store a cover with the magnets bearing on the fabric's face, where they can abrade it.

## Storage

The cover should be stored flat or rolled rather than creased along the same fold every time, since repeated creasing along one line is how coatings crack. A storage sleeve or hanging point in the workshop keeps the cover off the floor, where it picks up the grit that causes the problem in the first place.

For workshops, a simple rule works well: one cover per bay, hung when not in use, wiped before it is put away. That is enough to keep contamination under control without adding a process.

## When to replace

A cover reaches the end of its useful life when the backing has hardened or worn through, when the coating no longer resists fluid, when the edge binding has frayed to the point that magnets are exposed, or when stitching at an attachment point has failed.

Those are the four failure points, and each of them is a paint risk rather than a cosmetic one. A cover with an exposed magnet should be taken out of service immediately.

## What to tell customers

Publish the care instructions clearly on the product and on the listing. The most common cause of a poor review on this product class is not a manufacturing fault but a customer who washed it wrongly, and clear guidance prevents both the damage and the complaint.
