# Car Cover Waterproofing Re-Treatment — Restore the Protection

Your car cover's waterproofing doesn't last forever. After 12-18 months of sun and rain, water stops beading and starts soaking through. The fix is re-treatment — a simple, inexpensive way to extend cover life.

## Signs the Waterproofing Is Gone

1. Water soaks into the fabric instead of beading on the surface
2. The cover feels heavier when wet (absorbing water)
3. Rain passes through to the car after a storm
4. Fabric stiffens as the coating wears

## How Waterproofing Works

Car cover waterproofing comes from two sources:
- **Factory-applied coatings** (PU, PVC) — durable but eventually degrade
- **DWR treatments** (durable water repellent) — a chemical layer on the fabric surface that makes water bead

Re-treatment replaces the DWR layer; it can't restore a worn PU coating, but it can dramatically improve water beading.

## The Re-Treatment Process

**Step 1 — Clean the cover:** Wash thoroughly (gentle cycle, cold water, mild detergent) and air dry completely. Treatment won't bond to a dirty fabric.

**Step 2 — Choose the product:** Use a fabric waterproofing spray or wash-in treatment designed for outdoor fabrics (Nikwax, Grangers, or similar). Silicone-based sprays work well on most cover fabrics.

**Step 3 — Apply:**
- Spray method: Apply evenly while damp, focusing on seams and high-wear areas
- Wash-in method: Add to the washer, run, then air dry

**Step 4 — Cure:** Let the treatment dry/cure per product instructions (usually 24-48 hours) before use.

## Frequency

Re-treat every 12-18 months for outdoor covers, or whenever water stops beading. Covers in harsh UV or coastal climates need treatment more often.

## What Re-Treatment Won't Fix

- Fabric tears or holes — repair first
- Deteriorated PU coatings — replacement territory
- Mildew — clean before treating
- Sun-rotted fabric — the material itself is gone
