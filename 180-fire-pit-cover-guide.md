# Fire Pit Cover Buying Guide — Size, Material and Care

Fire pits sit outside all year, yet they are among the least protected outdoor items. Rain fills the bowl, ash becomes mud, and steel rusts. A properly fitted fire pit cover solves all of this — here is what to look for.

## Why Your Fire Pit Needs a Cover

- **Rust prevention** — standing water is the #1 killer of steel fire pits
- **Ash management** — wind scatters wet ash across the patio
- **Debris protection** — leaves and insects accumulate in the bowl
- **Extending lifespan** — a covered pit lasts 2-3x longer

## Choosing the Right Size

Measure the outer diameter of the bowl at its widest point, including any rim or lip. Add 5-10 cm for a comfortable fit. For square or rectangular pits, measure both length and width. A cover that is too tight will tear at the seam; one that is too loose will hold water on top.

## Key Features to Check

- **Elastic hem or drawstring** — keeps the cover on during storms
- **Breathable lining** — prevents condensation rust from the inside
- **Heat resistance** — the cover must handle the pit being slightly warm; always let the pit cool fully before covering
- **UV treatment** — prevents fabric degradation in summer sun

## Important Safety Rules

1. Never cover a fire pit while the fire is still burning or the bowl is hot
2. Wait until ash is completely cold and dry
3. Keep the cover away from open flames — it is protection from weather, not fire

## Care and Maintenance

- Brush off ash and debris before covering
- Wipe the bowl dry when possible
- Clean the cover with mild soap and water each spring
- Store the cover flat in winter if the pit is moved indoors

## Key Takeaways

- Measure the outer bowl diameter and add 5-10 cm for a safe, snug fit
- 600D Oxford with an elastic hem handles all-season weather best
- Never cover a hot pit — wait until ash is cold and dry
- A covered fire pit lasts two to three times longer than an uncovered one
