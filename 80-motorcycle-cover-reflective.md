# Motorcycle Cover Reflective Strips — Small Feature, Big Safety

Reflective strips on a motorcycle cover aren't a fashion detail — they're a safety feature that can save your bike from being hit by a car. Here's why they matter and what to look for.

## The Nighttime Visibility Problem

A motorcycle parked on a street at night is nearly invisible. It sits low, has no lights (when parked), and blends into the road. Other vehicles approaching at night — especially in poor weather — may not see it until it's too late.

## How Reflective Strips Help

Reflective tape works by bouncing headlight beams back toward the source. When a car's headlights hit the reflective strips on your cover, the driver sees a bright outline of the bike from 200+ feet away — enough reaction time to avoid contact.

## Where the Strips Should Be

- **Front and rear:** The most likely collision directions
- **Sides:** For bikes parked at angles or in narrow streets
- **High position:** Strips at the top of the cover are visible over parked cars

## Quality Matters

- Look for certified reflective material (retroreflective, not just glossy)
- Strips should be sewn on, not glued — glued strips peel in weather
- Quality strips stay reflective for the cover's lifetime; cheap ones dull in 6 months

## The Numbers

A parked bike with a reflective cover is visible at 200-300 feet. Without it, a dark bike on a dark street is often visible at under 50 feet — too late for a driver at 30 mph (stopping distance ~60 feet).
