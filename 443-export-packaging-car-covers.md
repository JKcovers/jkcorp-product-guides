# Export Packaging for Car Covers: Carton Engineering

Consumer packaging and export packaging are two different problems. The retail box is designed to be seen on a shelf. The export carton is designed to survive a container, a crane, a humid hold and a forklift — and it is where most avoidable damage to a cover shipment happens.

This guide covers the carton, not the polybag inside it.

## Why covers are awkward to pack

A cover is bulky, compressible and light. That combination creates specific risks:

- **Compression.** Cartons stacked six high in a container crush the bottom layer, and a crushed carton creases the fabric inside.
- **Humidity.** A container crossing a climate zone sweats. Sealed cartons trap that moisture against coated fabric.
- **Handling.** A large, light carton is easy to drop and easy to puncture at the corners.
- **Weight distribution.** Covers are low-density, so cartons are large relative to their mass and easy to throw.

## Getting the carton specification right

Four numbers decide whether the carton performs:

**Board grade.** Specified as a burst or edge-crush value, not as "strong". Ask for the flute type and the board combination, and match it to the stacking height you actually plan.

**Internal dimensions.** Determined by the folded cover plus the polybag, with clearance for the fold to settle. Too tight and the carton bulges; too loose and the cover shifts and abrades.

**Stacking strength.** Calculated from the board and the load, not assumed. If the container plan stacks six high, the bottom carton carries five cartons — specify for that.

**Closure.** Tape alone is normal; strapping is common on larger cartons. Whichever is used, the carton should not rely on the tape for structural strength.

## The humidity question

Coated covers are the worst case: the coating resists moisture movement, so condensation that forms inside a sealed polybag stays against the fabric for the whole voyage.

Three practical measures:

- a desiccant sachet sized to the internal volume, not a token one
- a small vent hole in the polybag, or a perforated bag, so vapour can migrate
- cartons that are dry before packing — a carton assembled in humid air carries that moisture

## Marking and identification

Shipping marks should let a warehouse identify the carton without opening it: SKU, size, colour, quantity, carton number, and the order reference. Side marks matter more than top marks, because cartons are usually read from the side of a stack.

Add handling symbols that match reality. A fragile symbol on a carton that will be top-loaded is decoration.

## What to check before the container closes

- a drop test on a loaded carton, at least from handling height
- a compression check at the planned stack height
- carton weight against manual handling limits
- a container loading plan with the cartons actually measured

This is cheap to do once and expensive to skip. Damage found at destination is rarely recoverable, and the argument about who caused it usually costs more than the goods.
