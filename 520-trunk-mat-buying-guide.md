# Trunk Mat Buying Guide

A trunk mat takes the spills, grit, wet dog and leaking shopping bag that would otherwise reach the carpet, and it can be hosed down in a way carpet cannot.

Specifying one properly comes down to five decisions: material, lip height, surface grip, fit, and how it is cleaned.

## What a trunk mat actually does

- **Contains liquid.** A raised lip holds a spill until it can be dealt with, instead of letting it soak into the carpet and underlay.
- **Protects against abrasion.** Cargo slides, and sharp edges dig in.
- **Provides grip**, so items do not slide into the tailgate on every corner.
- **Covers worn carpet**, often the real reason a used car buyer fits one.
- **Deadens sound slightly**, on a moulded mat with a dense backing.

## Material

**Rubber.** Heavy, durable, waterproof, and the best choice for wet cargo. Odour can be an issue in cheap compounds, particularly in heat.

**TPE.** A thermoplastic elastomer: lighter than rubber, flexible, low odour, and easy to mould into a vehicle-specific shape. The dominant material for OEM-style mats.

**Carpet.** Matches the interior and looks original, but absorbs liquid and is difficult to clean thoroughly.

**Quilted leatherette or padded fabric.** Soft, good looking, and used where cargo needs cushioning. Not waterproof at the seams.

**Foam or EVA.** Light and cheap, but compresses and tears under load.

The practical split is rubber or TPE for utility, carpet for appearance, quilted for a premium look with light use.

## Lip height and containment

The lip is the raised edge, and it is the feature that decides whether the mat contains anything.

- **A shallow lip** looks neat and is easy to load over, but a spill will run off.
- **A deep lip** contains liquid and debris, but makes sliding heavy items in harder.
- **A formed lip that follows the trunk's shape** is better than a flat border, because it uses the trunk's own walls.

For a trunk used for wet gear, garden waste or pets, a deeper lip is worth the loading inconvenience.

## Surface grip

A smooth mat lets cargo slide. Options:

- **Ribbed or channel patterns**, which also channel liquid away from the cargo.
- **Textured rubber**, which grips but traps dirt in the texture.
- **Raised nodules or a diamond pattern**, effective but harder to clean.
- **A removable anti-slip insert**, which can be replaced when worn.

## Fit

**Vehicle-specific moulded mats** fit the floor, the wheel arches and the seat-back line. They cost more and only fit one model.

**Trim-to-fit mats** are cut with a template or scissors. Cheap and universal, but the cut edge is exposed and the fit is approximate.

**Universal flat mats** are the weakest option: they slide, curl at the edges and rarely match the trunk floor.

Where the trunk has under-floor storage, a spare wheel hatch or tie-down points, the mat must be specified around them rather than over them.

## Cleaning and care

- **Rubber and TPE**: remove, hose, dry fully before refitting.
- **Carpet**: vacuum, then spot clean; avoid soaking the backing.
- **Quilted**: wipe, and check the seams, which are the first part to leak.

A mat left damp against the trunk floor is worse than no mat, because it holds moisture against the paint.

## What to ask a supplier

- material and its odour behaviour in heat
- lip height and whether it is formed to the vehicle
- surface pattern and how it is cleaned
- whether the mat clears under-floor storage and tie-downs
- weight and packing dimensions for the retail channel
- vehicle coverage list, since a moulded mat is model-specific
