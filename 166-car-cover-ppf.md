# Car Cover and Paint Protection Film (PPF) — Better Together

Paint Protection Film (PPF) and car covers are often seen as alternatives. They're actually complementary layers — and using both gives you protection that neither provides alone.

## What PPF Does

PPF is a transparent polyurethane film applied to the paint that:
- Absorbs rock chips and scratches
- Self-heals minor swirl marks with heat
- Blocks chemical etching (bird droppings, sap)
- Lasts 5-10 years

**What PPF does NOT do:** It doesn't stop UV from degrading the film itself, doesn't keep dust off, and doesn't prevent the film from developing its own issues over time.

## What a Cover Adds on Top of PPF

| Threat | PPF Alone | PPF + Cover |
|--------|:--:|:--:|
| Rock chips (driving) | ✅ | ✅ |
| UV degradation of PPF | ❌ | ✅ (cover blocks UV) |
| Dust accumulation | ❌ | ✅ |
| Bird droppings | ✅ (etches film instead) | ✅ (never touches) |
| Sun-induced PPF yellowing | ❌ | ✅ |
| Water spotting | ❌ | ✅ |

## The Key Insight

PPF is self-sacrificing — it absorbs damage so the paint doesn't. But the film itself degrades from UV. A cover extends PPF's life by shielding it from the sun when parked. This is the strongest argument for using both.

## The Combination Strategy

1. Apply PPF to high-impact areas (front bumper, hood, mirrors)
2. Use a cover whenever the car is parked for more than a few hours
3. The cover protects the film; the film protects the paint on the road
