# Distributor Agreements for Cover Brands

A distributor agreement is where a supply relationship becomes a business partnership, and the clauses that matter are rarely the ones either side negotiates first. Price gets the attention; territory, targets and termination decide whether the arrangement survives year two.

These are the terms worth getting right before the first container.

## Territory and exclusivity

Exclusivity is the most requested term and the most dangerous to grant casually.

Define the territory precisely — countries, not "Europe"; channels, not "retail". A distributor with exclusive rights to a region and no performance obligation has an option, not a commitment.

If exclusivity is granted, tie it to a condition: a minimum purchase volume per year, reviewed annually, with exclusivity lapsing if the target is missed. That structure protects both sides and is far easier to agree than a pure grant.

## Volume commitments

A target should be measurable, dated and tied to consequences. Three elements:

- an annual value or unit target
- a quarterly or half-yearly checkpoint
- what happens on a miss — loss of exclusivity, renegotiation, or termination

Targets set without a consequence are forecasts. Forecasts are useful for planning but they do not protect the brand.

## Pricing and price protection

State the price basis: ex-works, FOB or delivered, and the currency. Then state what happens when costs move — raw material, freight, exchange rate.

Two workable models: a fixed price for a defined period with review at the boundary, or a formula tied to an indexed input. What does not work is silence, which produces either a mid-season increase the distributor cannot absorb or a supplier quietly reducing specification.

## Brand and intellectual property

The distributor needs the right to use the brand and product images. The agreement should say:

- which marks may be used, and on what materials
- whether the distributor may register the brand in its territory — normally not
- who owns customer data and listings created by the distributor
- what happens to the brand assets on termination

That last clause prevents the common ending where a former distributor keeps using the brand imagery on a marketplace listing.

## Term, renewal and termination

A first term of one year with renewal by performance is standard and fair. Include:

- notice periods for non-renewal
- immediate termination grounds — insolvency, brand misuse, breach of compliance obligations
- an orderly wind-down: how long the distributor may sell remaining stock

The wind-down clause matters more than the termination clause. Most disputes at the end of a relationship are about stock that can no longer be sold.

## What to leave out

Avoid open-ended support obligations with no cap — marketing funds, free samples, unlimited training. If support is part of the offer, put a value on it, because an uncapped obligation is a cost that grows with the distributor's requests rather than with sales.
