# Export Regulations for Textile Covers: What Shippers Must Know

Export regulations for textile covers affect every shipment, from labeling and flammability to chemical compliance and tariffs. Covers are classified as textile articles in most customs regimes, which triggers specific documentation and product rules. Importers who plan for them avoid customs holds, chargebacks, and fines.

## HS Classification Basics

Most covers fall under HS Chapter 63 (made-up textile articles), typically 6307.90 for car covers or 6306.12/6306.19 for tarpaulin-type products. Classification drives duty rates and documentation, so confirm the correct code with your supplier and customs broker before shipping.

## Labeling and Origin Rules

- **Country of origin labeling:** most destination markets require the cover to state its country of manufacture on the product or packaging.
- **Fiber content labels:** US and EU rules require fiber composition disclosure for textile products.
- **Care instructions:** optional in many markets but expected by retailers.

## Flammability Compliance

- **US (CPSC):** textile covers used in vehicles can fall under flammability rules; request a flammability test report from the factory.
- **EU (EN 71 / general product safety):** covers for children's products or furniture may need additional safety documentation.
- Always ask for the test report, not a verbal claim.

## Chemical Compliance

- **EU REACH:** restricts SVHC substances; suppliers should provide a REACH declaration or test report.
- **Proposition 65 (California):** relevant for covers sold into California retail; lead and phthalate limits apply.
- **PFAS restrictions:** several US states and the EU restrict PFAS treatments used in waterproofing; confirm your coating is compliant.

## Documentation Checklist per Shipment

| Document | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Commercial invoice | Value, HS code, origin |
| Packing list | Carton counts, weights, dimensions |
| Bill of lading | Transport and delivery terms |
| Certificates of origin | Duty preference claims |
| Test reports | Flammability, REACH, UV as required |

## Practical Advice for Buyers

- Get compliance documents at sample stage, not after the container ships
- Confirm the factory's coating and dye suppliers meet destination rules
- Use a licensed customs broker at the destination
- Track regulation changes; chemical rules update faster than most industries

## Common Compliance Mistakes

- Assuming "certified" means compliant for every destination market
- Skipping flammability testing until a retailer demands the report
- Treating REACH and Proposition 65 as optional for small shipments
- Relying on verbal assurances instead of signed declarations
