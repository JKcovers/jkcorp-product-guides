# Aluminum Boat Cover — Protecting Your Jon Boat and Fishing Rig

Aluminum boats are lightweight, affordable, and everywhere — from jon boats to deep-V fishing rigs. They also have specific covering needs: the riveted hull, thin gauge metal, and simple open layouts make them easy to damage with a poorly chosen cover. Here is the guide to aluminum boat covers.

## Aluminum Boat Vulnerabilities

- **Riveted seams** — water and debris work into rivets, causing corrosion over time
- **Thin hull metal** — a sagging cover with pooled water can dent the gunwales
- **Bare aluminum oxidation** — UV and salt accelerate surface oxidation (white powder)
- **Open layout** — no cabin means every item aboard is exposed
- **Trailer exposure** — the trailer and outboard are as exposed as the hull

## Why Aluminum Boats Need Covers

- Prevents oxidation and corrosion of bare aluminum
- Keeps leaves, bird droppings, and debris out of the open hull
- Protects seats, casting decks, and electronics
- Extends trailer and outboard life with full coverage
- Reduces cleaning time — a covered boat is ready to fish

## Choosing the Right Cover

### Material

| Fabric | Use |
|--------|-----|
| 300D Oxford | Light indoor storage |
| 420D Oxford | Standard outdoor seasonal use |
| 600D Oxford | Full-time outdoor storage |
| PVC-backed canvas | Maximum waterproofing (watch breathability) |

### Fit Options

- **Universal covers** — workable for simple jon boats with flat profiles
- **Semi-custom** — adjustable shock-cord hems fit multiple models
- **Custom fit** — best for boats with raised casting decks, trolling motors, or unique layouts

### Must-Have Features

- **Center support pole** — prevents water pooling on open decks
- **Trolling motor pocket or gap** — protects the bow-mounted motor
- **Ventilation** — open aluminum boats still trap condensation
- **Reinforced elastic hem** — keeps the cover secure in wind
- **Tie-down straps** — for towing security

## Fitting Tips for Aluminum Boats

1. Install the support pole at the widest flat area
2. Fit the bow first, then the stern — aluminum boats are stiff and unforgiving of force
3. Avoid pulling the cover over sharp rivets and fittings; use padding at contact points
4. Secure straps under the trailer, not just the hull
5. Check the gunwale area for chafe — thin aluminum edges cut fabric

## Trailering with the Cover

Aluminum boats are often trailered uncovered for simplicity, but covered trailering protects both boat and outboard:

- Use a trailering-rated cover with reinforced straps
- Check the cover after the first 10 miles
- Remove the cover for long highway trips unless it's specifically rated

## Maintenance

- Wash with mild soap — avoid harsh cleaners that strip coatings
- Re-apply DWR treatment seasonally
- Inspect rivet contact areas for fabric wear
- Store dry to prevent mildew
