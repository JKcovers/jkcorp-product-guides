# How to Read a 600D PVC SVHC Test Report

Most suppliers send a PDF with the word PASS on it. Very few buyers can tell what was tested, against which threshold, or what the pass actually covers. That gap is where compliance problems start.

This is a worked example using a real report for the PVC-coated fabric used in pet seat covers — including the two substances that were detected, and why the report still passed.

## What the report tests

The report (Waltek Testing Group HCT, Shenzhen; report WTH25H07191029C, issued 24 July 2025) covers four screenings on a 603# fabric sample in medium grey:

- 250 substances on the ECHA candidate list of Substances of Very High Concern, under EU Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 (REACH)
- 1 proposed SVHC
- 4 potential intentional SVHC
- Resorcinol, against G/TBT/N/EU/803

All four returned PASS.

## Why 0.1% is the number that matters

Under REACH, the working threshold for an SVHC in an article is 0.1% by weight. Above it, communication and notification obligations apply. Below it, the article is treated as not containing the substance at a level that triggers them.

A pass is therefore not a statement about purity. It is a statement about a threshold.

## Reading the result line by line

| Substance | CAS | Reporting limit | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| DEHP | 117-81-7 | 0.0100% | 0.0114% |
| Melamine | 108-78-1 | 0.0100% | 0.0741% |
| Other 248 SVHC | — | 0.0100% | Not detected |
| 1 proposed SVHC | — | 0.0100% | Not detected |
| 4 potential intentional SVHC | — | 0.0100% | Not detected |
| Resorcinol | 108-46-3 | 0.0100% | Not detected |

Two substances were detected. DEHP at 0.0114% is roughly 11% of the threshold; melamine at 0.0741% is roughly 74% of it. Both sit below 0.1%, which is why the report passes — but neither is zero.

That is the most useful thing to understand about this class of report. Screening works to a reporting limit, and a result above that limit but below the threshold is still a pass.

## The report says so itself

The remarks note that identified results are based on a worst-case scenario, and that further investigation is required to confirm whether the substance is present. The screening is deliberately conservative: it reports what might be there rather than certifying what is absent.

## What a pass does not mean

This report does not establish that the fabric is:

- free of phthalates — DEHP was detected
- compliant with REACH Annex XVII restrictions, which are separate from the candidate list
- compliant with PAH, azo dye, formaldehyde or extractable heavy metal limits
- suitable for food contact
- compliant with OEKO-TEX, CPSIA or California Proposition 65

Each of those needs a different test. A supplier who presents one SVHC report as covering them is overstating it.

## One sample, one colour

The submitted sample was 603# in medium grey. Pigments and dyes differ between colourways, so evidence for grey is evidence for grey. If you order charcoal, navy or a print, ask whether the report covers that colour.

## What to ask any supplier

- the full report, not the summary page
- the laboratory and report number, so it can be verified
- the reporting limit, and whether any result falls between that limit and 0.1%
- the sample identity and the colour tested
- the issue date, since the candidate list is updated periodically
- which additional tests apply in your market

Telling a buyer that two substances were detected and remained below threshold is more useful than a document with no numbers on it. It is also the only version that survives the buyer checking.
