# Winter Cover Mistakes — Top 10 That Cost You Paint

Winter is the harshest season for both cars and covers — snow load, ice, road salt, and freezing temperatures expose every weakness. These 10 winter cover mistakes are the most common, and each can cost you paint, fabric, or both.

## Mistake 1: Using a Summer Cover in Winter

A lightweight dust cover has no water resistance, no insulation, and no wind rating. In snow and ice, it soaks through and freezes onto the car.

**Fix:** switch to a winter-rated cover — waterproof outer, padded or insulated construction, wind anchoring.

## Mistake 2: Covering a Damp Car

Winter air is humid; a damp car under a cover freezes into a moisture trap that feeds rust and mildew all season.

**Fix:** dry the car thoroughly before covering — especially wheel wells and door seams.

## Mistake 3: Leaving Ice Buildup Untouched

Ice accumulating on the cover adds weight, stresses straps, and can freeze the cover to the paint.

**Fix:** clear heavy snow and ice buildup periodically, and free any frozen edges before removing.

## Mistake 4: Skipping Wind Anchoring

Winter storms are windy. An unanchored cover flaps, rubs paint, and can blow off entirely, leaving the car exposed.

**Fix:** use all straps, and consider extra bungees or a lockable cable in storm-prone areas.

## Mistake 5: Allowing Snow Pools

Flat covers collect snow that melts and refreezes, stretching fabric and leaking onto the car.

**Fix:** use support poles or choose a cover with a built-in peak so snow slides off.

## Mistake 6: Covering With a Frozen Cover

A frozen stiff cover is brittle. Forcing it over the car cracks the coating and tears seams.

**Fix:** warm the cover briefly indoors, or gently flex it before installation.

## Mistake 7: Ignoring Condensation Checks

Winter covers trap condensation even when they breathe. Left unchecked, moisture builds up under the cover.

**Fix:** check under the cover every few weeks, and air it out on dry days.

## Mistake 8: Using Road Salt as an Excuse to Skip

Some owners skip covers in winter because "salt gets everywhere." Actually, a proper cover keeps salt spray off the paint.

**Fix:** cover as usual, and wash the car (or at least the underbody) when salt accumulates.

## Mistake 9: Storing the Cover Wet in Spring

When winter ends, the wet, salty cover goes straight into the bag — destroying it for next season.

**Fix:** wash, dry, and properly store the cover in spring.

## Mistake 10: Forgetting Snow Load on the Vehicle Itself

Even with a cover, heavy snow on flat roofs stresses the vehicle. The cover should shed snow, not collect it.

**Fix:** use a peaked or supported cover, and clear heavy accumulations promptly.

## The Golden Rule

**Winter-rated cover, dry car, anchored against wind, and checked regularly.** The right winter setup protects the car through the worst months.
