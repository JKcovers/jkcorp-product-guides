# Outdoor Kitchen Cover Guide — Protect Grills, Counters and Appliances

An outdoor kitchen is a major investment in stainless steel, stone and electronics — all exposed to rain, UV and grease. A single cover protects the whole station: grills, side burners, refrigerators and countertops.

## Why Your Outdoor Kitchen Needs a Cover

- **Stainless steel protection** — rain and humidity cause pitting and rust spots
- **Electronic protection** — keeps control panels, igniters and outlets dry
- **UV defense** — sun fades finishes and degrades seals and hoses
- **Grease and debris control** — covers stop dust and leaves from baking onto surfaces
- **Longer appliance life** — refrigerators and grills last years longer under cover

## Sizing an Outdoor Kitchen Cover

Measure the **full length of the kitchen island**, the **depth from front to back**, and the **height at the tallest point** (usually the grill hood or refrigerator). Covers are sold in lengths from 6 ft up to 16 ft+. Add a few inches to each dimension so the cover drapes without stretching over sharp edges.

## Features to Look For

- **Drawstring or elastic hem** — keeps the cover anchored in wind
- **Reinforced seams** — heavy covers take strain from repeated fitting
- **Ventilation** — reduces condensation that attacks stainless steel
- **Handle cut-outs or hooks** — let you fit the cover around knobs and faucets
- **UV-stabilized fabric** — resists fading over long summers

## Care and Usage Tips

1. Let grills cool completely before covering
2. Wipe down stainless steel surfaces before covering to prevent staining
3. Secure the cover at both ends — wind flapping wears seams fastest
4. Clean the cover twice a year with mild soap and water

## Key Takeaways

- Measure length, depth and height, then size up slightly
- 600D Oxford with drawstring hem is the all-season standard
- Always cover a cool, wiped-down kitchen
- A covered outdoor kitchen keeps its looks and function for years
