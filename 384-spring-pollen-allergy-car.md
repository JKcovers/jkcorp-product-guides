# Spring Pollen and Allergy Season Cars

Pollen season is an exterior problem for paint and an interior problem for the people in the car. The two are caused by the same airborne load, but they are solved in completely different ways — and a cover helps with only one of them.

Understanding which problem is being addressed is what stops a buyer from expecting a cover to do something it cannot.

## The exterior problem

On paint, pollen behaves like a mildly acidic, sticky particulate. It bonds to the surface, resists being blown off, and turns into a paste once it is damp with dew. Left in place through repeated damp cycles, it marks the clearcoat.

The correct response is rinsing, not wiping. Wiping grinds the grains into the paint.

## The interior problem

Inside the car, pollen enters through the ventilation system, the door and window seals, and every time a door is opened. Once inside it settles into the carpet, the seat fabric and the ventilation ducts, and it is re-suspended every time the cabin air moves.

This matters to two groups of buyers, and they want different things:

- **Allergy sufferers** want the pollen load in the cabin minimised while the car is parked and while it is being driven.
- **Fleet and rental operators** want the interior to stay presentable through a season of short trips and frequent door openings.

Neither is solved by an exterior cover.

## What actually reduces cabin pollen

**Recirculation when parked.** Switching the ventilation to recirculation before shutting the car down closes the fresh-air path. It is a single action with a measurable effect on how much pollen enters while parked.

**Cabin filter condition.** The cabin filter is the barrier that matters, and its condition is usually unknown to the owner. Replacing it at the start of the season is cheap and directly relevant — a loaded filter passes pollen and restricts airflow at the same time.

**Keeping windows closed while parked.** An obvious point that is routinely ignored on warm spring days.

**Cleaning the interior properly.** Vacuuming the seats and carpet, and wiping the hard surfaces, removes the reservoir that otherwise re-suspends pollen all season. A quick brush raises more pollen than it removes.

**Cleaning the exterior before opening doors.** Pollen sitting on the roof and around the door apertures falls into the cabin as soon as a door moves. Rinsing the car first prevents that transfer.

## Where an exterior cover fits

A cover is the right product for the paint problem, and it contributes indirectly to the cabin problem by keeping the pollen load off the bodywork. It does not filter air, and it should not be sold as an allergy product.

The cover requirements for the pollen season are the tight ones: a fine, smooth weave that sheds pollen rather than holding it, an accurate fit so flapping does not pump pollen-laden air underneath, and a light colour that reduces heat and shows deposits less.

The maintenance half of the equation is what most owners miss. A pollen-loaded cover transfers its load onto the car, and then into the cabin, the next time it is handled. Rinsing the cover weekly through the peak weeks is what keeps it working.

## The season in one line

Keep pollen off the paint with a tight-fitting cover, keep it out of the cabin with the recirculation setting and a fresh cabin filter, and rinse both the car and the cover rather than brushing either.
