# Motorcycle Chain and Cover Maintenance — The Complete Routine

The chain and the cover are the two most neglected parts of a motorcycle storage routine. A rusted chain destroys sprockets, and a dirty cover scratches the bike it is supposed to protect. Motorcycle chain and cover maintenance is simple — a few minutes of care that saves hundreds in parts.

## Why the Chain Needs Attention

- A chain left dirty and wet rusts within days in humid weather
- Rusted links stiffen, wear sprockets, and can snap at speed
- Salt from winter roads is the fastest chain killer
- A slack chain slaps the swingarm and damages the chain guard
- Storage is when chains rust most — the bike sits still while moisture does its work

## The Storage Routine

1. **Clean the chain** — use a dedicated chain cleaner (not degreaser) and a soft brush
2. **Dry it** — wipe off excess cleaner completely
3. **Lube generously** — apply chain lube, let it set, then wipe the outside of the links
4. **Adjust tension** — per the owner's manual before long storage
5. **Fit the cover** — over the whole bike, with the chain now protected

## Chain Guards and Covers

| Component | Job | Maintenance |
|-----------|-----|-------------|
| Chain guard | Stops debris and clothing catching | Check for cracks, replace if loose |
| Chain cover | Shields chain from rain and grit | Clean with the chain, re-check fasteners |
| Swingarm protector | Reduces chain slap wear | Replace when worn thin |
| Master link clip | Keeps the chain closed | Check for damage every wash |

## Cover Maintenance in the Same Session

While the chain is drying, tend the motorcycle cover:

- **Brush off** dirt and dust before it embeds in the fabric
- **Check seams** at the stress points — mirrors, rear, and hem
- **Re-treat waterproofing** if water soaks through
- **Clean the lining** — grit trapped inside scratches the tank
- **Store dry** — never fold a wet cover

## What Kills Chains

- **Under-lubrication** — metal-on-metal wear
- **Over-lubrication** — excess lube flings off and collects grit
- **Pressure washing** — blasts lube out of the links and forces water in
- **Long idle periods** — rust forms where lube has dried out
- **Wrong lube** — grease too thick for O-ring chains blocks the seals

## Seasonal Deep Clean

Twice a year, do a deeper service: remove the chain guard and clean behind it, inspect the sprocket teeth for hooking, check the chain for tight spots, and verify the chain cover seals properly. This is also the time to re-treat the cover's waterproofing and check its straps.

## The Bottom Line

Chain care and cover care belong together — do both at the same time, and neither gets forgotten. A lubed chain and a clean cover mean a bike that is ready to ride after months of storage.
