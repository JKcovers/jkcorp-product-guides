# Motorcycle Cover Waterproofing — Re-Treatment Guide

A motorcycle cover is only as good as its waterproofing. After a season of sun and rain, the water-repellent finish wears off, seams relax, and the fabric starts absorbing water. The result: a heavy, wet cover that traps moisture against the bike. Re-treating a motorcycle cover is a 30-minute job that extends its life by years.

## Signs the Waterproofing Is Gone

- Water soaks into the fabric instead of beading and rolling off
- The cover feels heavy and cold after rain
- Damp patches appear on the inside lining
- Condensation forms under the cover overnight
- The fabric shows fading or stiffening on the top surface

## Why Covers Lose Waterproofing

| Cause | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| UV exposure | Breaks down the DWR coating |
| Washing | Detergent strips the water-repellent finish |
| Abrasion | Rubbing against the bike wears the coating |
| Age | Coatings degrade naturally over 2-3 years |
| Fuel spills | Solvents dissolve the coating |

## The Re-Treatment Process

1. **Clean the cover** — wash with a technical fabric cleaner (never regular detergent with bleach), rinse thoroughly
2. **Dry completely** — the fabric must be fully dry before treatment
3. **Apply DWR spray** — a Nikwax- or Grangers-style water repellent, held 20-30 cm from the surface
4. **Cover all panels** — including seams and the hem; use a seam sealer on taped seams if they leak
5. **Heat-set if required** — some treatments need a gentle tumble or iron on low heat to activate
6. **Test with water** — sprinkle water; it should bead and run off

## Product Types

- **Spray-on DWR** — easiest for large covers, re-applied twice a season
- **Wash-in treatment** — mixed with water in the wash cycle, more even coverage
- **Seam sealers** — a dedicated product for stitching lines and stress points
- **Waterproofing wax** — for cotton-canvas covers, restores both look and performance

## Do's and Don'ts

- **Do** re-treat in dry, warm weather — the spray needs time to cure
- **Do** treat seams twice — they fail first
- **Don't** use fabric softener — it blocks breathability
- **Don't** machine-dry on high heat — it damages the coating and shrinks the fit
- **Don't** re-treat a torn cover — repair the fabric first

## Prevention Between Treatments

Keep the cover clean — dirt holds moisture and accelerates coating loss. Store it dry and folded loosely, never crammed into a bag while wet. Park the bike so the cover sheds water rather than pooling on the seat area.

## When to Replace

If the base fabric is cracked, the seams are fraying, or the lining has degraded, re-treatment will not save it. A cover that absorbs water weighs down on the bike and scratches the paint. Replace it before the next wet season.
