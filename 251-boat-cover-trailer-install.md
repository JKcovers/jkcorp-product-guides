# Boat Cover Install on Trailer — Step-by-Step Guide

Installing a boat cover on a trailered boat is a two-person job done best in a specific order. Done right, the cover fits tight, drains water, and survives highway speeds. Done wrong, it pools water, flaps, and abrades the hull. Here is the step-by-step guide to boat cover install on trailer.

## Preparation Before Installing

1. **Clean the boat** — dirt and debris trapped under a cover scratch the hull
2. **Dry everything** — covering a wet boat traps moisture
3. **Close all hatches and latches** — water must not enter anywhere
4. **Position the boat on the trailer** — centered, with proper tongue weight
5. **Remove antennas or lower them** — protrusions tear covers

## Step 1: Install Support Poles

Support poles prevent water pooling on flat spans:

- Place poles at the widest, flattest areas of the deck
- Adjust pole height so the cover slopes toward the sides
- Add a center pole for wide boats — 8 feet and over
- Never let the cover rest flat over a span wider than 3-4 feet

## Step 2: Lay the Cover Over the Boat

- Unfold the cover and drape it over the boat with the bow end toward the bow
- Work from the bow toward the stern, smoothing as you go
- Pull the cover over the gunwales evenly — don't yank one side
- Align any bow or stern markings with the boat's centerline

## Step 3: Fit the Elastic Hem

- Start at the bow, tucking the elastic hem under the rub rail
- Work around one side, then the other, evenly
- Finish at the stern
- The hem should grip firmly but not stretch the fabric tight

## Step 4: Secure the Straps

- Attach bow strap to the trailer bow stop or winch post
- Run side straps under the trailer frame, crossing to the opposite side
- Attach stern straps to the trailer's stern cross member
- Tighten straps evenly — overtightening wrinkles the fabric

## Step 5: Check Drainage

- Inspect the cover from a distance — any flat area where water could pool needs a pole or strap adjustment
- Water must run off toward the sides and stern
- Tug the fabric at low points — it should be taut

## Step 6: Test at Speed (For Trailering Covers)

- Drive 5-10 miles, then stop and check every strap
- Look for flapping, ballooning, or chafing at the rub rail
- Re-tighten as the cover settles

## Common Install Mistakes

1. Skipping support poles — pooled water collapses the cover
2. Overtightening straps — wrinkles the fabric and stresses seams
3. Installing on a wet boat — trapped moisture damages upholstery
4. Leaving antenna up — tears the cover at highway speed
5. Forgetting the trailer straps — cover lifts off the boat in wind

## Removal

- Uncover on a dry day
- Fold the cover loosely — never store it wet or tight
- Inspect seams and contact points while it's off
- Clean and treat before storage
