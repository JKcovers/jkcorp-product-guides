# QC Inspection for Protective Covers — What to Check Before Shipping

Quality control separates a professional cover factory from a workshop. For buyers importing car covers, boat covers, and seat covers, a proper QC inspection is the difference between a sellable shipment and a container of returns. Here is what to check at each stage.

## The Three Inspection Stages

| Stage | When | Focus |
|-------|------|-------|
| Pre-production | Before fabric cutting | Materials, samples, approvals |
| In-production | Mid-run | Stitching, printing, workmanship |
| Pre-shipment | Before loading | Final random inspection (AQL) |

## Key Checkpoints for Covers

### 1. Fabric

- Weight and denier match the spec (weigh and measure samples)
- Waterproof coating is uniform — no thin spots or cracks
- Color matches the approved sample under natural light
- No pulls, holes, or foreign yarns in the weave

### 2. Construction

- Stitching density: 8-10 stitches per inch is the standard for covers
- Seams are straight, double-stitched at stress points
- Zippers run smoothly and are correctly attached
- Elastic hems have consistent tension and correct circumference

### 3. Printing and Logo

- Print placement matches the approved proof within tolerance
- Colors are accurate — compare against the approved color card
- Print is cured/heat-set so it does not crack or peel

### 4. Sizing

- Measure the finished product against the agreed size chart
- Tolerance is usually ±2-3% of the stated dimensions
- Confirm the size label matches the actual product

### 5. Packaging

- Polybags are sealed and correct
- Hang tags and barcodes are attached and accurate
- Cartons are strong, correctly labeled, and within export size/weight limits

## AQL Sampling Explained

Most professional inspections use AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) random sampling. Common levels for covers: general inspection level II, with acceptable limits of 0 for critical defects, 1.0-1.5 for major defects, and 4.0 for minor defects. The inspector picks a random sample; if defects stay within the limits, the lot passes.

## How to Arrange Inspection

- **Video/photographic inspection:** the factory live-streams or photographs the checkpoints — fastest, low cost
- **Third-party inspection:** SGS, Bureau Veritas, or Intertek inspect on your behalf — most independent
- **Self-visit:** for large orders, visit the factory during production

## Red Flags in a QC Report

- Fabric weight consistently below spec
- Zipper failures in the sample batch
- Printing misaligned on more than a few pieces
- Covers packed wet or without moisture protection
