# Boat Cover Measurement Guide: Measure Once, Fit Forever

A boat cover measurement guide matters more than it seems: boats have hull shapes, windshields, and hardware that make measuring tricky, and a poorly measured cover either flaps loose or strains at the seams. Here is how to measure your boat for a trailerable, mooring, or storage cover.

## What to Measure

| Dimension | How to Measure | Notes |
|-----------|----------------|-------|
| Centerline length | Bow tip to transom, straight line | Exclude swim platforms and engines |
| Beam (width) | Widest point, gunwale to gunwale | At the widest hull section |
| Height | Ground to highest point | Windshield, bimini, antennas |
| Windshield rise | Gunwale to windshield top | Needs clearance in the pattern |

Measure on the trailer with the boat level. If the boat is in water, measure length at the deck line and add 5-10% for hull overhang.

## Trailer Considerations

- Include the trailer tongue length only if the cover must protect the winch post
- Measure with the boat strapped down; a cover must clear the tie-downs
- Note the roller and winch stand position for the front cutout

## Common Measuring Mistakes

- Measuring length over the curve of the hull instead of straight line
- Forgetting the windshield and bimini height
- Including the swim platform or outboard in the length
- Ignoring rails, rod holders, and cleats that need clearance
- Measuring on an uneven surface

## Choosing Cover Style by Measurement

| Style | Best Fit | Key Dimension |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| Trailerable cover | Boat on trailer, highway use | Length and height for straps |
| Mooring cover | Boat at dock | Beam and windshield height |
| Cockpit cover | Protect cockpit only | Cockpit length and width |
| Full winter cover | Long-term storage | Length + support poles for snow |

## Quick Takeaways

- Measure length as a straight line, and never include swim platforms or engines
- Beam and windshield height drive the pattern more than length alone
- Measure on a level trailer with tie-downs in place for an accurate fit
- Tall windshields and hardtops justify a custom pattern over a universal cover
- Send the four key numbers to the factory for pattern recommendations before ordering

## Custom vs Universal

Universal covers fit broad size ranges but sag on odd hull shapes. Custom covers made from your measurements fit tight, shed water, and resist wind. For boats with tall windshields or hardtops, custom is worth the extra cost.
