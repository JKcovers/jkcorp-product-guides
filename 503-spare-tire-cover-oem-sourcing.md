# Spare Tire Cover OEM Sourcing Guide

Sourcing spare tire covers on an OEM basis is more approachable than most automotive accessory categories, because the product has few components and no electronics. The difficulty is not manufacturing complexity — it is specification discipline. Most problems in this category trace back to sizing assumptions and undefined material performance rather than production capability.

## What makes a good OEM brief

A supplier can quote accurately from four inputs:

1. **Size coverage** — the tire diameter and section-width bands the cover must fit, expressed as ranges rather than single values.
2. **Material specification** — fabric type and weight, backing, and any water-resistance requirement.
3. **Retention method** — full elastic hem, drawstring, buckle, or a combination.
4. **Branding** — plain, printed, embroidered, or with a sewn-on label, plus artwork and placement.

A brief that says "spare tire cover, decent quality" will produce a quotation that is impossible to compare against another supplier's. Everything downstream depends on these four points being written down.

## Sizing is the highest-risk item

Covers are specified by the overall diameter of the inflated tire, not the rim diameter. Two 15-inch tires can differ by several inches in overall diameter, and off-road assemblies are wider as well as larger. A supplier should be able to demonstrate the calculation from a sidewall code and show which of their size bands covers which tire codes.

Ask for a size chart in the form a customer can verify: tire codes alongside the matching cover size. This is also the single most useful asset a supplier can put on a product page.

## Material and construction questions worth asking

- What is the fabric weight, and how does it behave on a large tire at highway speed?
- Is there a backing layer, and what does it protect against?
- How is the hem reinforced, and is the stitching specified?
- What is the stated care regime, and is a machine-wash claim actually supported?
- How is print durability verified?

The last two matter more than they appear. A cover marketed as machine washable that fails after one wash generates returns that cost more than the product.

## Tooling, MOQ and sampling

Plain covers require no tooling, so minimum order quantities are driven by material runs and are generally negotiable for a trial order. Printed covers add setup and material waste, which raises the minimum. Hard-shell alternatives require mould tooling and are only economic at volume — a separate decision from soft covers.

Always sample against a real mounted tire. A sample fitted to a bench measurement can pass while a sample fitted to an inflated, curved assembly shows distortion in the artwork or a hem that will not seat.

## Quality control points

- Dimensional consistency against the approved sample across the production run
- Elastic recovery after repeated stretching
- Bond between fabric and backing layer
- Print adhesion, UV resistance and colour consistency between lots
- Hem stitching under load

A pre-shipment inspection covering these points is proportionate for this product class.

## Total cost, not unit price

Freight efficiency favours soft covers heavily: they compress and ship at high density, unlike rigid alternatives. For large programmes the freight difference can exceed the unit price difference between suppliers, so always compare landed cost rather than FOB alone.
