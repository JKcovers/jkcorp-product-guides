# Gas Grill vs Charcoal Grill Cover Needs — What's Different

Gas and charcoal grills need different covers. Most buyers assume one cover fits both, which is why covers tear, rust forms, and grills die early. Here is how gas grill and charcoal grill cover needs actually differ.

## The Core Differences

| Factor | Gas Grill | Charcoal Grill |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Heat retention | High — lid stays hot | Moderate |
| Grease | Heavy grease buildup | Ash and soot |
| Weather exposure | Needs full coverage | Needs full coverage |
| Typical size | Larger footprint | Varies, often narrower |
| Interior risk | Gas lines and valves | Ash moisture |

## Heat and the Cover

Gas grills retain heat in the lid and burners long after cooking. Covering a still-warm gas grill traps heat and can warp the fabric or speed corrosion on the hood. Always let a gas grill cool completely — 30-60 minutes — before covering. Charcoal grills cool faster but hold ash that stays warm for hours.

## Moisture Is the Enemy of Both

- **Gas grills:** moisture attacks burner tubes, valves, and the igniter — the most expensive parts to replace
- **Charcoal grills:** moisture turns ash into corrosive sludge that eats the bowl's paint and coating

Either way, the cover must be waterproof **and** breathable to avoid condensation rust.

## Size Differences

Measure both the grill body and the side shelves or propane tank. Gas grills with side burners need wider covers. Charcoal grills with lower bodies need shorter, deeper covers. A cover sized for a gas grill will be baggy on most charcoal kettles — buy the size that matches your actual model dimensions.

## Material Recommendations by Grill Type

| Grill Type | Recommended Cover |
|------------|-------------------|
| Gas (3-4 burner) | 600D Oxford, padded top, straps |
| Gas (with side burner) | Extra-wide 600D, reinforced seams |
| Charcoal kettle | 420D/600D Oxford with drawstring |
| Charcoal smoker | Tall cover, heat-resistant top |

## Common Mistakes

1. Buying one cover for multiple different grills
2. Covering a hot grill and trapping heat
3. Ignoring straps — wind lifts covers and exposes the grill
4. Leaving ash in the bowl under a damp cover
