# Boat Cover Storage Bag and Care

The bag a boat cover comes in is treated as packaging, and that is a mistake. For most of the year the cover lives in that bag, and what happens there determines whether it is usable next season.

A cover folded wet into a sealed bag will be mouldy, musty and possibly stained by the time it is next opened. A cover stored with grit in its folds will abrade itself in transit.

## What the bag has to do

**Contain the cover.** A large boat cover is bulky, and a bag that is too small forces the owner to compress it, which creases the fabric along the same lines every time.

**Let it breathe.** This is the requirement most bags fail. A sealed bag traps residual moisture.

**Drain.** If the cover goes in damp, water collects at the bottom of the bag. A drain or a mesh panel lets it out.

**Carry.** Webbing handles rather than a single drawstring, because a wet canvas cover is heavy.

**Identify.** A label window or a printed panel so the bag can be found among other gear.

## Material and construction

- **Mesh panels** at the base or sides, for drainage and airflow.
- **A coated or water-resistant base**, so the bag does not wick water from a wet floor.
- **A drawstring or zip closure** that is large enough to pass the cover without forcing.
- **Bar-tacked handle attachments**, since the handles carry the full weight.
- **A size that matches the folded cover**, not the boat's length.

The common error is specifying the bag to the boat's length. A 24-foot cover folds to a bundle whose dimensions have little to do with 24 feet.

## How the cover should be stored

The bag only handles part of the problem. The routine matters as much:

1. **Dry the cover fully before storage.** This is the single most important step, and the one most often skipped.
2. **Fold rather than stuff**, so the same creases are not compressed repeatedly.
3. **Keep it off the floor** if the storage area is damp, by hanging the bag or placing it on a pallet.
4. **Store away from UV** if possible. A cover stored in a sunlit shed degrades even when not in use.
5. **Check it mid-season**, and re-dry if there is any sign of damp.

## Signs the storage routine is failing

- **A musty smell on opening** — mould has started.
- **White or grey bloom on the fabric** — mildew or efflorescence from trapped moisture.
- **Stiff patches** — coating degradation, often accelerated by damp storage.
- **Crease lines that have become cracks** — repeated folding along the same line.
- **Corroded hardware** — moisture held against metal fittings.

Any of these means the cover's life has been shortened by storage rather than by use.

## Care of the bag itself

The bag is exposed to the same conditions and is usually neglected.

- **Empty and dry it** after the cover comes out.
- **Brush out grit**, which otherwise abrades the cover.
- **Check the handles and stitching** each season, because a handle that fails under load drops the cover.
- **Repair small tears** before they become a reason to discard the bag.

## What to specify

- bag dimensions matched to the folded cover
- mesh or drain panel
- water-resistant base
- webbing handles, bar-tacked
- a closure large enough to load the cover easily
- a label area for identification
- care instructions printed on the bag
