# Fender Cover OEM Order Guide

A fender cover is a straightforward textile product with no tooling barrier, which makes it accessible for private label programmes. The risk is not manufacturing difficulty but under-specification: a brief that omits the properties that make the product work produces a quotation nobody can compare and a sample that passes inspection while failing in the workshop.

## The brief a supplier needs

A quotation is only comparable if the brief covers six points:

1. **Size structure** — one universal size, a small range, or vehicle-specific patterns, with dimensions for each.
2. **Retention system** — encapsulated magnets, hooks and straps, or a combined design; plus the reinforcement at each attachment point.
3. **Face material** — vinyl or PVC-faced, or coated textile, with the fluid-resistance requirement stated.
4. **Backing** — the soft, non-abrasive layer that protects paint. This is the product's core safety property and must be specified, not assumed.
5. **Padding and weight** — whether impact absorption is required, and the target fabric weight.
6. **Branding** — plain, printed, or with a sewn badge; artwork, orientation and placement.

A brief missing any of these will produce incomparable quotes. The backing and the retention system are the two most commonly omitted and the two that determine whether the product is fit for professional use.

## Retention: the highest-risk specification

Magnets are the mainstream choice and the main source of quality variation. Three things must be specified:

- **Encapsulation.** The magnet fully sealed inside the fabric, with no metal exposed to contact the paint.
- **Grade and strength.** Thin or low-grade magnets hold weakly and weaken over time. State the requirement and verify it on the sample.
- **Reinforcement.** A doubled fabric layer around each magnet, and stitching that will not fail under a leaning technician.

Where the intended vehicles may have aluminium or composite panels, a combined magnet-and-hook design removes the risk of a product that simply does not work.

## Materials and construction

A wipe-clean, fluid-resistant face suits professional use, where contamination transfer between vehicles is a genuine concern. A coated textile face suits retail and light service, where comfort and appearance weigh more heavily.

The backing determines whether the cover protects paint or marks it. Specify the material and verify that it does not abrade a painted surface. The edge binding and the stitching around each magnet are the construction details that decide service life.

## Sampling and quality control

Sample against a real vehicle wing, with someone leaning on the cover, and check that it stays flat and does not slide. Test the face material against the fluids the intended user actually encounters.

Production checks worth requiring:

- Dimensional consistency against the approved sample
- Magnet strength and encapsulation, verified per batch
- Stitch integrity at every edge and attachment point
- Backing material consistency between lots
- Print adhesion and colour consistency, where applicable

## Minimum order and volume

Plain covers involve no tooling, so minimum order quantities are driven by material runs and are generally negotiable for a trial order. Printed covers add setup and material waste and raise the minimum. Combined magnet-and-hook constructions increase labour content, which shifts the price more than the material cost suggests.

## Landed cost

Covers fold flat and ship densely, which keeps freight efficient. Confirm the folded dimensions and packaging format early, and where the product is sold at retail, specify a storage sleeve — it improves presentation and prevents the permanent creasing that leads to coating failure.
