# Side Window Shade and Full Set Guide

A windscreen shade solves the most visible part of the problem, but it leaves three-quarters of the glass uncovered. Light entering through the side and rear windows heats the cabin and reaches the same interior surfaces the front shade is protecting. A full set addresses the whole vehicle, which is why kits outsell single panels in most branded and premium channels.

## What the set contains

A typical full set covers:

- **Windscreen panel** — the largest element, and the one that carries the primary benefit.
- **Front side windows** — usually two panels, shaped to fit around the mirror mount and the window frame.
- **Rear side windows** — often the same shape as the fronts on symmetric vehicles, but not always.
- **Rear screen** — included in some kits and omitted in others, depending on whether the vehicle has a rear wiper or a steeply raked screen.

Some kits also include a sunroof panel. Whether to include one depends on the vehicle list being targeted.

## Why side panels behave differently

Side windows are smaller but more awkward. They are close to the driver's head, so panel thickness and stiffness matter: a floppy panel that falls inward while driving is a distraction and a safety concern. Attachment is also harder, because there is less frame to work with and the window glass itself moves.

The practical attachment options are suction cups, static-cling film, magnetic strips or a flexible frame that presses against the window aperture. Each has a service-life characteristic: suction cups fail when the glass is hot and dusty, static-cling loses grip over time, and flexible frames depend on the aperture geometry.

For a passenger-carrying product, prioritise whatever fails safe — a panel that drops harmlessly rather than one that swings into the cabin.

## Building a set that sells

| Consideration | Practical approach |
|---|---|
| Coverage | Front screen plus all four side windows is the minimum credible set |
| Consistency | Same fabric, colour and edging across every panel |
| Storage | A single bag or sleeve for the whole set — sets that scatter get poor reviews |
| Weighting | Side panels need more stiffness than the windscreen panel |
| Branding | The windscreen panel carries the print; side panels usually stay plain |

A well-packaged set with a storage sleeve commands a materially higher price than the sum of loose panels, and it reduces the chance of the customer losing half the kit.

## Sizing across a range

Full sets are harder to make universal than a single windscreen panel, because side window shapes vary more between vehicles than windscreens do. In practice, sets are offered in a small number of generic sizes with generous tolerance, or as vehicle-specific kits.

Where a range is offered, publishing a model-to-size table is essential. Side window fit is the most common cause of returns on this product, and it is the hardest thing for a customer to assess before buying.

## Quality checks

Confirm the side panels stay in place with the window partly open, verify that no panel obstructs a mirror or a window switch, and test that the fabric does not mark or scratch glass. Check the storage sleeve holds the full set without the panels creasing permanently along their fold lines.
